# Module 5.2: Classification Methods – Support Vector Machine (SVM)

## 1. Introduction to Support Vector Machines (SVM)
A **Support Vector Machine (SVM)** (introduced by Vladimir Vapnik and Corinna Cortes in 1995) is one of the most powerful and mathematically rigorous **supervised machine learning algorithms** used primarily for **binary classification** (and extended to multi-class classification and regression).

* **Core Problem of Linear Classification:**
  Given a linearly separable binary classification dataset $D = \{(\mathbf{x}_1, y_1), (\mathbf{x}_2, y_2), \dots, (\mathbf{x}_N, y_N)\}$ where $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and class labels are **$y_i \in \{+1, -1\}$**, there are **infinitely many possible straight lines (hyperplanes)** that can separate the two classes with zero training error!
* **How SVM Solves This (The Maximum Margin Principle):**
  While a standard Perceptron picks *any* separating line it stumbles upon first, **SVM finds the unique Optimal Separating Hyperplane that maximizes the geometric margin**—the perpendicular distance ("safety buffer" or "street width") between the decision boundary and the nearest training data points of both classes.

## 2. Fundamental Terminology & Geometry of SVM

```text
               Class +1 (+)          H+ : w^T x + b = +1
                  +    +            /
                +   (+)------------+--- <-- Support Vector on H+
                    /             /
                   /   Margin    /====== H0 : w^T x + b = 0 (Decision Boundary)
                  /  (2 / ||w||)/
                 /             /
                /   (-)-------+-------- <-- Support Vector on H-
               /    -    -
          H- : w^T x + b = -1        Class -1 (-)
```

### 1. Separating Hyperplane (Decision Boundary $H_0$)
In a $d$-dimensional feature space, a **Hyperplane** is a flat $(d-1)$-dimensional subspace that divides the space into two halves:
* In **1D:** A single **point**.
* In **2D:** A **straight line**.
* In **3D:** A **2D flat plane**.
* In **$d$-D:** A **hyperplane** defined by the linear equation:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = 0
$$

  where:
  * **$\mathbf{w} = [w_1, w_2, \dots, w_d]^T$** is the **normal weight vector** perpendicular to the hyperplane (determines the **orientation/slope** of the boundary).
  * **$b \in \mathbb{R}$** is the **bias (intercept)** term (determines the **offset/distance** of the hyperplane from the origin).

---

### 2. Margin Hyperplanes ($H_+$ and $H_-$)
We scale $\mathbf{w}$ and $b$ such that the two parallel planes touching the nearest points of each class are given by:
* **Positive Margin Hyperplane ($H_+$):**

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = +1
$$

  All points of Class $+1$ lie on or above this boundary: $\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b \ge +1$ (for $y_i = +1$).
* **Negative Margin Hyperplane ($H_-$):**

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = -1
$$

  All points of Class $-1$ lie on or below this boundary: $\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b \le -1$ (for $y_i = -1$).

Combining both conditions into a single inequality (since $y_i \in \{+1, -1\}$):

$$
y_i \left(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b\right) \ge 1 \quad \text{for all } i = 1, 2, \dots, N
$$

---

### 3. Support Vectors
**Support Vectors** are the critical subset of training data points $\mathbf{x}_i$ that lie **closest to the decision boundary**—specifically, right on the margin hyperplanes $H_+$ and $H_-$ where the equality holds:

$$
y_i \left(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b\right) = 1
$$

* **Why are they called "Support Vectors"?**
  1. Because these boundary points **alone "support" (define and lock in place) the optimal hyperplane**!
  2. **Sparsity Property:** If you delete or move any other training points that lie far away from the margin ($y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) > 1$), the position of the decision boundary **does not change at all**! This makes SVM extremely memory-efficient at test time.

---

### 4. Margin ($\gamma$ or $M$)
The **Margin** is the perpendicular width of the empty "no-man's-land street" between the positive hyperplane $H_+$ ($\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b = +1$) and the negative hyperplane $H_-$ ($\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b = -1$):

$$
\text{Margin } (M) = \dfrac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{w_1^2 + w_2^2 + \dots + w_d^2}}
$$

* Distance from $H_0$ to $H_+$ is $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$, and distance from $H_0$ to $H_-$ is $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$, giving a total margin width of $\displaystyle \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$.

---

## 3. Working of Support Vector Machine

### Step 1: Training Phase (Constrained Optimization)
To **maximize the margin $\displaystyle \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$**, we must **minimize $\|\mathbf{w}\|$** (or equivalently minimize $\displaystyle \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2$ for mathematical convenience in quadratic programming):

$$
\min_{\mathbf{w}, \, b} \quad \dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2
$$

$$
\text{subject to:} \quad y_i\left(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b\right) \ge 1, \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, N\}
$$

### Step 2: Testing / Classification Phase
Once the optimal parameters $(\mathbf{w}^*, b^*)$ are learned, any new unseen test sample $\mathbf{x}_{\text{test}}$ is classified by evaluating which side of the hyperplane it falls on using the sign of the decision function:

$$
\hat{y} = \text{sign}\!\left({\mathbf{w}^*}^T \mathbf{x}_{\text{test}} + b^*\right) = \begin{cases} +1, & \text{if } {\mathbf{w}^*}^T \mathbf{x}_{\text{test}} + b^* > 0 \\ -1, & \text{if } {\mathbf{w}^*}^T \mathbf{x}_{\text{test}} + b^* < 0 \end{cases}
$$

---

## 4. Hard-Margin SVM vs. Soft-Margin SVM
* **Hard-Margin SVM:** Requires $100\%$ of training points to be strictly outside the margin ($y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1$). Works only if data is **perfectly linearly separable** and is very sensitive to a single noisy outlier.
* **Soft-Margin SVM (For Noisy / Overlapping Real-World Data):** Introduces non-negative **slack variables $\xi_i \ge 0$** that allow a few noisy points to violate the margin, controlled by a **Regularization Parameter $C > 0$**:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{w}, b, \xi} \quad \dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 + C \sum_{i=1}^{N} \xi_i \quad \text{subject to } y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1 - \xi_i
$$

  * **Large $C$:** Narrow margin, strict penalty on misclassifications (risk of overfitting).
  * **Small $C$:** Wider margin, tolerates a few margin violations for better generalization.

## 5.Solved University Exam Question (Complete 8-Mark Answer!)

### Question 1 [End Semester Exam - Nov 2025, Part B, Q19 (8 Marks)]
> **Discuss the working of Support Vector Machine. Why is maximizing the margin important, and how does it affect classification?**

#### **Solution:**

#### **Part 1: Working of Support Vector Machine (SVM) (4 Marks)**

1. **Core Objective:**
   * Support Vector Machine (SVM) is a supervised classification algorithm that separates data points belonging to two classes ($y_i \in \{+1, -1\}$) by constructing an **Optimal Separating Hyperplane** in an $N$-dimensional feature space.
   * Among all possible hyperplanes that can separate the two classes, SVM selects the unique hyperplane that has the **maximum geometric margin** (distance) from the nearest training data points of both classes.

2. **Mathematical Formulation of the Decision Boundary and Margin:**
   * The central **Separating Hyperplane ($H_0$)** is defined by:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = 0
$$

     where $\mathbf{w}$ is the weight vector normal to the hyperplane and $b$ is the bias.
   * Two parallel **Margin Hyperplanes ($H_+$ and $H_-$)** are placed on either side of $H_0$ touching the closest data points of each class:
     * $H_+ : \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = +1$ (for Class $+1$, where $\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b \ge +1$)
     * $H_- : \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = -1$ (for Class $-1$, where $\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b \le -1$)
   * The data points lying directly on $H_+$ and $H_-$ ($y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) = 1$) are called **Support Vectors**.
   * The perpendicular distance between $H_+$ and $H_-$ is the **Margin**:

$$
\text{Margin } (M) = \dfrac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}
$$

3. **Optimization & Classification Rule:**
   * To maximize the margin $\displaystyle \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$, SVM solves the convex quadratic optimization problem:

$$
\min_{\mathbf{w}, b} \dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) \ge 1 \;\; \forall i
$$

   * For a new test sample $\mathbf{x}$, SVM predicts the class using $\hat{y} = \text{sign}(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b)$ (Class $+1$ if $>0$, Class $-1$ if $<0$). If the data is non-linearly separable, SVM uses **Soft-Margin slack variables** or **Kernel Functions** to map data into a higher-dimensional space.

---

#### **Part 2: Why Maximizing the Margin is Important & How It Affects Classification (4 Marks)**

[Click here to see the diagram for Small Margin vs Maximum Margin Classifier Generalization](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&q=Small+Margin+vs+Large+Margin+Hyperplane+SVM+Generalization+diagram)

1. **Superior Generalization to Unseen Test Data:**
   * If a decision boundary is placed very close to the training points (a **small margin**), even a tiny variation in new unseen test data will cross the boundary and cause a **misclassification**.
   * By **maximizing the margin**, SVM places the decision boundary right down the middle of the widest possible "safety corridor" between the two classes, ensuring high classification accuracy on unseen test samples.
2. **High Robustness to Noise and Measurement Perturbations:**
   * Real-world test data always contains sensor noise or slight feature fluctuations ($\mathbf{x} + \Delta\mathbf{x}$). A wide margin acts as an error-tolerance buffer: as long as the noise perturbation $|\Delta\mathbf{x}|$ is smaller than the half-margin $\displaystyle \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$, the data point will **not** cross the hyperplane and classification remains $100\%$ accurate.
3. **Reduction of Overfitting (Structural Risk Minimization):**
   * According to Statistical Learning Theory (Vapnik-Chervonenkis / VC Theory), maximizing the margin ($\text{minimizing } \|\mathbf{w}\|^2$) directly restricts the capacity (VC dimension) of the classifier. This prevents the boundary from overfitting to boundary quirks in the training set.
4. **Unique Global Optimum & Sparsity:**
   * Unlike Neural Networks (which can get stuck in poor local minima with arbitrary boundaries), maximizing the margin yields a **strictly convex quadratic optimization problem** with a **single unique global solution** that depends only on a few **Support Vectors**, making classification fast and immune to distant outliers.
