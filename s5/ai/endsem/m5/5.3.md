# Module 5.3: Optimal Separating Hyperplane (OSH)

## 1. What is an Optimal Separating Hyperplane?
In a binary classification problem with linearly separable training data $D = \{(\mathbf{x}_1, y_1), (\mathbf{x}_2, y_2), \dots, (\mathbf{x}_N, y_N)\}$ where $\mathbf{x}_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and $y_i \in \{+1, -1\}$, any hyperplane $\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b = 0$ that correctly places all $+1$ points on one side and all $-1$ points on the other side is called a **Separating Hyperplane**.

However, while infinitely many separating hyperplanes can exist, **there is only ONE Optimal Separating Hyperplane (OSH)**:
* **Definition:** The **Optimal Separating Hyperplane** (also called the **Maximum-Margin Hyperplane**) is the unique separating hyperplane that **maximizes the geometric margin**—the perpendicular distance from the hyperplane to the closest training data points of either class (**Support Vectors**), while lying exactly midway between the positive and negative margin boundaries.

## 2. Mathematical Derivation of Margin Width $\displaystyle M = \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$ (Exam Must-Know!)

Let the central **Optimal Separating Hyperplane ($H_0$)** be defined by:

$$
H_0 : \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = 0
$$

And let the two parallel **Canonical Margin Hyperplanes ($H_+$ and $H_-$)** passing through the nearest points (**Support Vectors**) of Class $+1$ and Class $-1$ be:

$$
H_+ : \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = +1
$$

$$
H_- : \quad \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b = -1
$$

### Method 1: Vector Projection Derivation (Shortest & Cleanest for Exams!)
1. Let $\mathbf{x}_+$ be any positive Support Vector lying on $H_+$, so:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_+ + b = +1 \implies \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_+ = 1 - b
$$

2. Let $\mathbf{x}_-$ be any negative Support Vector lying on $H_-$, so:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_- + b = -1 \implies \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_- = -1 - b
$$

3. Subtract the two equations:

$$
\mathbf{w}^T (\mathbf{x}_+ - \mathbf{x}_-) = (1 - b) - (-1 - b) = 2
$$

4. The **Margin Width ($M$)** is the perpendicular distance between the two parallel planes $H_+$ and $H_-$, which is the projection of the difference vector $(\mathbf{x}_+ - \mathbf{x}_-)$ onto the unit normal vector $\displaystyle \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \frac{\mathbf{w}}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$:

$$
M = \hat{\mathbf{u}}^T (\mathbf{x}_+ - \mathbf{x}_-) = \dfrac{\mathbf{w}^T (\mathbf{x}_+ - \mathbf{x}_-)}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}
$$

5. Substituting $\mathbf{w}^T (\mathbf{x}_+ - \mathbf{x}_-) = 2$:

$$
\mathbf{\text{Margin Width } M = \dfrac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}}
$$

### Method 2: Point-to-Plane Distance Formula
From coordinate geometry, the perpendicular distance from any point $\mathbf{x}_i$ to the hyperplane $\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b = 0$ is:

$$
d(\mathbf{x}_i, H_0) = \dfrac{|\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b|}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}
$$

* For a positive Support Vector $\mathbf{x}_+ \in H_+$, $\displaystyle |\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_+ + b| = 1 \implies d_+ = \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$.
* For a negative Support Vector $\mathbf{x}_- \in H_-$, $\displaystyle |\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_- + b| = |-1| = 1 \implies d_- = \frac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$.
* **Total Margin:**

$$
M = d_+ + d_- = \dfrac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|} + \dfrac{1}{\|\mathbf{w}\|} = \mathbf{\dfrac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}}
$$

## 3. Finding the Optimal Separating Hyperplane (Primal & Dual Formulation)

### A. Primal Optimization Problem
To maximize the margin $\displaystyle M = \frac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}\|}$, we minimize $\|\mathbf{w}\|$, or equivalently minimize the convex quadratic objective $\displaystyle \frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{w}$:

$$
\text{Minimize:} \quad \Phi(\mathbf{w}) = \dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2
$$

$$
\text{Subject to:} \quad y_i\left(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b\right) \ge 1, \quad \text{for } i = 1, 2, \dots, N
$$

---

### B. Method of Lagrange Multipliers & Dual Problem
To solve this constrained optimization problem, we introduce non-negative **Lagrange Multipliers $\alpha_i \ge 0$** (one for each training point $i = 1, \dots, N$) and form the **Lagrangian Function**:

$$
L_P(\mathbf{w}, b, \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \dfrac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 - \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i \Big[ y_i(\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b) - 1 \Big]
$$

Setting the partial derivatives with respect to $\mathbf{w}$ and $b$ to zero ($\nabla_{\mathbf{w}} L_P = 0$ and $\displaystyle \frac{\partial L_P}{\partial b} = 0$) yields **two fundamental optimality conditions**:

1. **Optimal Weight Vector Formula:**

$$
\dfrac{\partial L_P}{\partial \mathbf{w}} = \mathbf{w} - \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i = 0 \implies \mathbf{\mathbf{w}^* = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i}
$$

2. **Zero-Sum Constraint:**

$$
\dfrac{\partial L_P}{\partial b} = -\sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i y_i = 0 \implies \mathbf{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i y_i = 0}
$$

#### **Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) Complementary Slackness Condition:**
For every training point $i$:

$$
\alpha_i \Big[ y_i({\mathbf{w}^*}^T \mathbf{x}_i + b^*) - 1 \Big] = 0
$$

* **Case 1 (Non-Support Vectors):** If a point lies strictly outside the margin ($y_i({\mathbf{w}^*}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b^*) > 1$), then its Lagrange multiplier MUST be zero (**$\alpha_i = 0$**)! It plays zero role in $\mathbf{w}^*$.
* **Case 2 (Support Vectors):** Only for points lying **directly on the margin boundary** ($y_i({\mathbf{w}^*}^T\mathbf{x}_i + b^*) = 1$) can we have **$\alpha_i > 0$**!
* Once $\mathbf{w}^*$ is found, the optimal bias $b^*$ is computed from any Support Vector $(\mathbf{x}_s, y_s)$ using:

$$
y_s\left({\mathbf{w}^*}^T\mathbf{x}_s + b^*\right) = 1 \implies \mathbf{b^* = y_s - {\mathbf{w}^*}^T\mathbf{x}_s}
$$

#### **The Wolfe Dual Optimization Problem:**
Substituting $\displaystyle \mathbf{w} = \sum \alpha_i y_i \mathbf{x}_i$ back into $L_P$ gives the **Dual Problem**:

$$
\text{Maximize:} \quad L_D(\boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i - \dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N} \alpha_i \alpha_j y_i y_j \left(\mathbf{x}_i^T \mathbf{x}_j\right)
$$

$$
\text{Subject to:} \quad \alpha_i \ge 0 \;\;\forall i, \quad \text{and} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i y_i = 0
$$

*(Key Insight for Module 5.4: Notice that in the Dual Problem and in the decision function $\displaystyle f(\mathbf{x}) = \text{sign}\!\left(\sum_{i \in SV} \alpha_i y_i (\mathbf{x}_i^T \mathbf{x}) + b^*\right)$, the training vectors appear **only inside inner (dot) products $\mathbf{x}_i^T \mathbf{x}_j$**!)*

## 4. Worked Numerical Example: Finding the Optimal Separating Hyperplane

### Problem Statement:
> **Find the Optimal Separating Hyperplane, Support Vectors, and Margin Width for a binary classifier with positive class ($y = +1$) point $\mathbf{x}_1 = (2, 2)$ and negative class ($y = -1$) point $\mathbf{x}_2 = (0, 0)$.**

#### **Solution:**

1. **Identify Support Vectors:**
   Since there is only one point in each class, both $\mathbf{x}_1 = (2, 2)^T$ ($y_1 = +1$) and $\mathbf{x}_2 = (0, 0)^T$ ($y_2 = -1$) are **Support Vectors**.

2. **Find Weight Vector $\mathbf{w}$ and Bias $b$:**
   * The normal vector $\mathbf{w}$ points in the direction of $(\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_2) = (2 - 0, 2 - 0)^T = (2, 2)^T$, so let $\mathbf{w} = (w_1, w_2)^T$ with $w_1 = w_2$.
   * Substitute $\mathbf{x}_2 = (0, 0)^T$ into the negative margin hyperplane $\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_2 + b = -1$:

$$
w_1(0) + w_2(0) + b = -1 \implies \mathbf{b = -1}
$$

   * Substitute $\mathbf{x}_1 = (2, 2)^T$ and $b = -1$ into the positive margin hyperplane $\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x}_1 + b = +1$:

$$
2w_1 + 2w_2 - 1 = 1 \implies 2w_1 + 2w_2 = 2 \implies w_1 + w_2 = 1
$$

     Since $w_1 = w_2$ (for minimum $\|\mathbf{w}\|^2 = w_1^2 + w_2^2$):

$$
\mathbf{w_1 = 0.5, \quad w_2 = 0.5} \implies \mathbf{\mathbf{w}^* = [0.5, \; 0.5]^T}
$$

3. **Write the Optimal Separating Hyperplane Equation ($\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{x} + b = 0$):**

$$
0.5 x_1 + 0.5 x_2 - 1 = 0 \quad (\text{or } x_1 + x_2 - 2 = 0)
$$

4. **Compute the Maximum Margin Width ($M$):**

$$
\|\mathbf{w}^*\| = \sqrt{(0.5)^2 + (0.5)^2} = \sqrt{0.25 + 0.25} = \sqrt{0.5} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
$$

$$
M = \dfrac{2}{\|\mathbf{w}^*\|} = \dfrac{2}{1/\sqrt{2}} = \mathbf{2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.828}
$$
