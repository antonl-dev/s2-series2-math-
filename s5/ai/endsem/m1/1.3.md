# Module 1.3: Probability Density Function (PDF)

## 1. Definition of Probability Density Function (PDF)
For a **continuous random variable** $X$, individual point probabilities $P(X = x)$ are always zero. Instead, we describe the probability distribution of $X$ using a non-negative function $f(x)$ called the **Probability Density Function (PDF)** (or simply **density function**).

The PDF $f(x)$ specifies the **rate of concentration of probability per unit length** around a point $x$, such that the probability of $X$ falling within any interval $[a, b]$ is given by the **area under the curve** $f(x)$ between $x = a$ and $x = b$:

$$
P(a \le X \le b) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx
$$

## 2. Fundamental Properties (Axioms) of a Valid PDF
A function $f(x)$ defined over the real line $(-\infty, \infty)$ is a valid Probability Density Function of a continuous random variable $X$ **if and only if** it satisfies the following conditions:

### Property 1: Non-Negativity Condition

$$
f(x) \ge 0 \quad \text{for all } x \in (-\infty, \infty)
$$

* **Reason:** Since probabilities cannot be negative, the density curve can never dip below the horizontal $x$-axis.

### Property 2: Normalization Condition (Total Area Equals 1)

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1
$$

* **Reason:** The interval $(-\infty, \infty)$ represents the entire sample space $S$ (all possible values that the random variable $X$ can take). Since the probability of a sure event $P(S) = P(-\infty < X < \infty) = 1$, the total area under the PDF curve over its entire domain must equal $1$.
* *Note:* If $f(x)$ is non-zero only on a finite support $[c, d]$ and $0$ otherwise, then:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = \int_{-\infty}^{c} 0\,dx + \int_{c}^{d} f(x)\,dx + \int_{d}^{\infty} 0\,dx = \int_{c}^{d} f(x)\,dx = 1
$$

### Property 3: Interval Probability
For any real numbers $a$ and $b$ with $a \le b$:

$$
P(a \le X \le b) = P(a < X < b) = P(a \le X < b) = P(a < X \le b) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx
$$

---

## 3. Crucial Distinction: Probability Density $f(x)$ vs. Probability $P(X = x)$
A very common exam conceptual question is understanding what $f(x)$ actually represents:
1. **$f(x)$ is NOT a probability:** $f(x)$ is a **probability density** (probability per unit length), analogous to mass density in physics ($\text{mass} = \text{density} \times \text{volume}$).
2. **Infinitesimal Probability Interpretation:**
   For a very small interval $[x, x + dx]$ of width $dx$:

$$
P(x \le X \le x + dx) \approx f(x) \cdot dx
$$

   Thus:

$$
f(x) = \lim_{\Delta x \to 0} \dfrac{P(x \le X \le x + \Delta x)}{\Delta x}
$$

3. **Can $f(x)$ be greater than $1$?**
   * **Yes!** While a probability $P(A)$ can never exceed $1$, the density value $f(x)$ **can be greater than $1$** over a narrow interval, provided the total area $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1$.
   * *Example:* If $f(x) = 2$ for $0 \le x \le 0.5$ and $0$ otherwise, then $f(x) = 2 > 1$, yet $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{0.5} 2\,dx = 2(0.5) = 1$.

## 4. Standard Exam Problem Types on PDF

### Type 1: Finding an Unknown Constant $k$ in $f(x)$
* **Method:** Use the normalization property $\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1$, solve for $k$, and verify that $f(x) \ge 0$ for all $x$.

### Type 2: Finding Probabilities of Events / Intervals
* **Method:** Integrate $f(x)$ over the required interval. If $f(x)$ is a piecewise function, split the integral at the boundary points where the formula of $f(x)$ changes!

### Type 3: Checking Independence of Two Events $P$ and $Q$ Defined on $X$
* **Method:**
  1. Compute $\displaystyle P(P) = \int_{P} f(x)\,dx$.
  2. Compute $\displaystyle P(Q) = \int_{Q} f(x)\,dx$.
  3. Find the intersection interval $P \cap Q$ (the overlapping region of $x$ values) and compute $\displaystyle P(P \cap Q) = \int_{P \cap Q} f(x)\,dx$.
  4. Check whether $P(P \cap Q) = P(P) \cdot P(Q)$. If equal, $P$ and $Q$ are **independent**; otherwise, they are **not independent (dependent)**.

## 5. Solved University & Internal Exam Questions

### Question 1 [Series Test-I, Part A, Q2 (2 Marks)]
> **Why must the total area under a probability density function always equal $1$?**

#### **Solution:**
1. **Axiom of Total Probability:** By the fundamental axioms of probability, the probability of the entire sample space $S$ (the certainty that the random variable $X$ takes *some* real value between $-\infty$ and $+\infty$) is always equal to $1$:

$$
P(S) = P(-\infty < X < \infty) = 1
$$

2. **Geometric Definition of PDF:** For a continuous random variable, the probability that $X$ lies in any interval $[a, b]$ is given by the area under its probability density function $f(x)$ over that interval: $\displaystyle P(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
3. **Conclusion:** Therefore, integrating $f(x)$ over the entire real line $(-\infty, \infty)$ gives the total area under the PDF curve, which must equal the total probability of all possible outcomes, i.e., $1$:

$$
\text{Total Area} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = P(-\infty < X < \infty) = 1
$$

---

### Question 2 [Series Test-I, Part A, Q3 (2 Marks)]
> **A continuous random variable $X$ has probability density function $f(x)$. Explain why $P(X = a) = 0$ for any real number $a$, while $P(a \le X \le b) > 0$ for a finite interval $[a, b]$.**

#### **Solution:**
* **For a single point $X = a$:** Probability is the area under the PDF curve $f(x)$. At an exact number $x = a$, the upper and lower limits of integration are identical, meaning the interval width is zero ($a - a = 0$). A line segment has zero geometric area:

$$
P(X = a) = \int_{a}^{a} f(x)\,dx = 0
$$

* **For a finite interval $[a, b]$ (with $a < b$):** The interval has a positive width $(b - a > 0)$. When $f(x) > 0$ over $[a, b]$, integrating over this non-zero width produces a positive area under the curve:

$$
P(a \le X \le b) = \int_{a}^{b} f(x)\,dx > 0
$$

### Question 3 [End Semester Exam - Nov 2025, Part B, Q11 (8 Marks)]
> **The probability density function (pdf) of a continuous random variable $X$ is given by:**
>
> $$
> f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{x}{25}, & 0 \le x \le 5 \\ \dfrac{10 - x}{25}, & 5 \le x \le 10 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
> $$
>
> **Verify whether $P$ and $Q$ are independent if $(P: 0 \le x \le 5)$ and $(Q: 2.5 \le x \le 7.5)$.**

[Click here to see the diagram for Triangular Probability Density Function](https://www.google.com/search?udm=2&q=Triangular+Probability+Density+Function+0+to+10+peak+at+5)

#### **Solution:**

**Condition for Independence:**
Two events $P$ and $Q$ are statistically independent if and only if:

$$
\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = \text{Prob}(P) \times \text{Prob}(Q)
$$

---

#### **Step 1: Find the Probability of Event $P$ ($0 \le X \le 5$)**
In the interval $0 \le x \le 5$, the PDF is $\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{x}{25}$:

$$
\text{Prob}(P) = P(0 \le X \le 5) = \int_{0}^{5} f(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{5} \dfrac{x}{25}\,dx
$$

$$
\text{Prob}(P) = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{5} = \dfrac{1}{25} \left( \dfrac{5^2 - 0^2}{2} \right) = \dfrac{25}{50} = \dfrac{1}{2} = 0.5
$$

---

#### **Step 2: Find the Probability of Event $Q$ ($2.5 \le X \le 7.5$)**
Since the definition of $f(x)$ changes at $x = 5$, we split the integral at $x = 5$:
* From $2.5$ to $5$, $\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{x}{25}$
* From $5$ to $7.5$, $\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{10 - x}{25}$

$$
\text{Prob}(Q) = P(2.5 \le X \le 7.5) = \int_{2.5}^{5} \dfrac{x}{25}\,dx + \int_{5}^{7.5} \dfrac{10 - x}{25}\,dx
$$

**Evaluate the first integral $I_1$:**

$$
I_1 = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_{2.5}^{5} = \dfrac{1}{50} \left( 5^2 - (2.5)^2 \right) = \dfrac{1}{50} (25 - 6.25) = \dfrac{18.75}{50} = 0.375 \quad \left(\text{or } \dfrac{3}{8}\right)
$$

**Evaluate the second integral $I_2$:**

$$
I_2 = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ 10x - \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_{5}^{7.5} = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ \left(10(7.5) - \dfrac{(7.5)^2}{2}\right) - \left(10(5) - \dfrac{5^2}{2}\right) \right]
$$

$$
I_2 = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ (75 - 28.125) - (50 - 12.5) \right] = \dfrac{1}{25} [46.875 - 37.5] = \dfrac{9.375}{25} = 0.375 \quad \left(\text{or } \dfrac{3}{8}\right)
$$

**Total Probability of $Q$:**

$$
\text{Prob}(Q) = I_1 + I_2 = 0.375 + 0.375 = 0.75 \quad \left(\text{or } \dfrac{3}{4}\right)
$$

---

#### **Step 3: Find the Probability of the Intersection Event $P \cap Q$**
* Event $P$ is the interval $[0, 5]$.
* Event $Q$ is the interval $[2.5, 7.5]$.
* Therefore, the intersection $P \cap Q$ is the overlapping interval:

$$
P \cap Q = [0, 5] \cap [2.5, 7.5] = [2.5, 5]
$$

Now compute $\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = P(2.5 \le X \le 5)$:

$$
\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = \int_{2.5}^{5} \dfrac{x}{25}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{25} \left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_{2.5}^{5} = \dfrac{25 - 6.25}{50} = \dfrac{18.75}{50} = 0.375 \quad \left(\text{or } \dfrac{3}{8}\right)
$$

---

#### **Step 4: Verify the Independence Condition**
Check if $\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = \text{Prob}(P) \times \text{Prob}(Q)$:

$$
\text{Prob}(P) \times \text{Prob}(Q) = 0.5 \times 0.75 = 0.375 \quad \left(\text{or } \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3}{8}\right)
$$

Compare with $\text{Prob}(P \cap Q)$:

$$
\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = 0.375
$$

Since $\text{Prob}(P \cap Q) = \text{Prob}(P) \times \text{Prob}(Q) = 0.375$, **the events $P$ and $Q$ are independent.**

### Question 4 [Series Test-I, Part B, Q7(b) (5 Marks)]
> **The probability density function of a continuous random variable is:**
>
> $$
> f(x) = kx, \quad 0 \le x \le 4
> $$
>
> **Find: i) The value of $k$ $\quad$ ii) Mean $\quad$ iii) Variance**

#### **Solution:**

#### **i) Find the value of $k$:**
For $f(x)$ to be a valid PDF, the total area under the curve must equal $1$:

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\,dx = 1 \implies \int_{0}^{4} kx\,dx = 1
$$

$$
k \left[ \dfrac{x^2}{2} \right]_{0}^{4} = 1 \implies k \left( \dfrac{4^2 - 0^2}{2} \right) = 1
$$

$$
k \left( \dfrac{16}{2} \right) = 1 \implies 8k = 1 \implies k = \dfrac{1}{8} = 0.125
$$

So, the PDF is $\displaystyle f(x) = \frac{x}{8}$ for $0 \le x \le 4$.

---

#### **ii) Find the Mean $\mu = E(X)$:**
For a continuous random variable, the mean is:

$$
\mu = E(X) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{4} x \left(\dfrac{x}{8}\right)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{8} \int_{0}^{4} x^2\,dx
$$

$$
\mu = \dfrac{1}{8} \left[ \dfrac{x^3}{3} \right]_{0}^{4} = \dfrac{1}{8} \left( \dfrac{64 - 0}{3} \right) = \dfrac{64}{24} = \dfrac{8}{3} \approx 2.67
$$

---

#### **iii) Find the Variance $\sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X)$:**
First, calculate $E(X^2)$:

$$
E(X^2) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 f(x)\,dx = \int_{0}^{4} x^2 \left(\dfrac{x}{8}\right)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{8} \int_{0}^{4} x^3\,dx
$$

$$
E(X^2) = \dfrac{1}{8} \left[ \dfrac{x^4}{4} \right]_{0}^{4} = \dfrac{1}{8} \left( \dfrac{256 - 0}{4} \right) = \dfrac{256}{32} = 8
$$

Now compute Variance using $\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$:

$$
\text{Var}(X) = 8 - \left(\dfrac{8}{3}\right)^2 = 8 - \dfrac{64}{9} = \dfrac{72 - 64}{9} = \dfrac{8}{9} \approx 0.889
$$

✅ **Final Answers:**
* **i) $\displaystyle k = \frac{1}{8} = 0.125$**
* **ii) $\displaystyle \text{Mean } (\mu) = \frac{8}{3} \approx 2.67$**
* **iii) $\displaystyle \text{Variance } (\sigma^2) = \frac{8}{9} \approx 0.889$**
