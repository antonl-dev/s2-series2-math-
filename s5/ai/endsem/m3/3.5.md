# Module 3.5: The 8-Puzzle Problem

## 1. Introduction to the 8-Puzzle Problem
The **8-Puzzle** is one of the most famous benchmark problems in Artificial Intelligence for studying **uninformed and informed ($A^*$) search algorithms**.
* It consists of a **$3 \times 3$ square board** containing **$8$ numbered sliding tiles** ($1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8$) and **one blank space (`_`)**.
* Any tile horizontally or vertically adjacent to the blank space can slide into the blank space.
* **Objective:** Reach a specified **Goal Configuration** from a given **Start (Initial) Configuration** using the minimum number of moves.

## 2. Formal Search Problem Formulation of the 8-Puzzle

To solve the 8-Puzzle using a search agent, we formulate it using the **five standard components**:

| Component | Formulation for the 8-Puzzle |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. States** | A state description specifies the location of each of the $8$ tiles and the blank in the $9$ squares of the $3 \times 3$ matrix.<br>• Total permutations $= 9! = 362,880$ states.<br>• Exactly half ($9!/2 = \mathbf{181,440}$ states) are reachable from any given starting state. |
| **2. Initial State** | Any valid starting $3 \times 3$ arrangement of tiles $1\text{–}8$ and the blank `_`. |
| **3. Actions** | Defined in terms of moving the **Blank Space (`_`)**:<br>• **`Left`**, **`Right`**, **`Up`**, **`Down`** (depending on where the blank is located):<br>  - Blank at a **Corner** $\rightarrow 2$ legal actions.<br>  - Blank at an **Edge (Middle of side)** $\rightarrow 3$ legal actions.<br>  - Blank at the **Center** $\rightarrow 4$ legal actions. |
| **4. Transition Model** | Given a state and an action (e.g., `Move Blank Up`), returns the resulting $3 \times 3$ state where the blank and the target adjacent tile have swapped positions. |
| **5. Goal Test & Path Cost** | • **Goal Test:** Checks whether the current board matches the target Goal state.<br>• **Path Cost $g(n)$:** Each single move costs **$1$**, so $g(n)$ equals the **number of moves (depth $d$)** taken from the initial state to reach node $n$. |

---

## 3. Heuristic Functions for the 8-Puzzle ($h_1$ and $h_2$)

When solving the 8-Puzzle using Informed Search (**Best-First Search** or **$A^*$ Search** with $f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$), two standard admissible heuristics are universally used:

### 1. $h_1(n)$ — Number of Misplaced Tiles (Hamming Distance)
* **Definition:** $h_1(n)$ is the total number of numbered tiles ($1$ through $8$) that are **not** in their correct goal positions in state $n$.
* **⚠️ Crucial Exam Rule:** **The blank space (`_`) is NEVER counted** in $h_1(n)$ or $h_2(n)$! (Because moving a tile into the blank changes both the tile's position and the blank's position in a single move; counting the blank would overestimate the number of moves and make the heuristic inadmissible).
* **Why $h_1(n)$ is Admissible ($h_1(n) \le h^*(n)$):** Every tile that is out of place must be moved *at least once* to reach its goal square.

---

### 2. $h_2(n)$ — Sum of Manhattan Distances (City-Block / Taxicab Distance)
* **Definition:** $h_2(n)$ is the sum of the **horizontal and vertical distances** of each of the $8$ numbered tiles ($1$ through $8$) from its current coordinates $(r_i, c_i)$ to its goal coordinates $(r_i^{\text{goal}}, c_i^{\text{goal}})$:
  $$h_2(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{8} d_{\text{Manhattan}}(\text{Tile } i) = \sum_{i=1}^{8} \left( |r_i - r_i^{\text{goal}}| + |c_i - c_i^{\text{goal}}| \right)$$
  *(Diagonal moves are not allowed in the 8-puzzle, so a tile must move strictly along rows and columns).*
* **Why $h_2(n)$ is Admissible ($h_2(n) \le h^*(n)$):** In a single move, only one tile can move by $1$ square (either horizontally or vertically). Even if tiles could pass right through each other without blocking one another, each tile $i$ would still require at least $|r_i - r_i^{\text{goal}}| + |c_i - c_i^{\text{goal}}|$ moves to reach its home square.

---

## 4. Heuristic Dominance: Comparing $h_2$ and $h_1$

### Definition of Dominance
Given two admissible heuristics $h_1$ and $h_2$:
* We say that **$h_2$ dominates $h_1$** if:
  $$h_2(n) \ge h_1(n) \quad \text{for all nodes } n$$
  (and $h_2(n) > h_1(n)$ for at least one node $n$).

### Mathematical Proof that $h_2(n) \ge h_1(n)$ for Every State $n$
For each numbered tile $i \in \{1, 2, \dots, 8\}$, let:
* $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ be its contribution to $h_1(n)$ (where $m_i = 0$ if tile $i$ is in its goal position, and $m_i = 1$ if tile $i$ is misplaced).
* $d_i = |r_i - r_i^{\text{goal}}| + |c_i - c_i^{\text{goal}}|$ be its Manhattan distance contribution to $h_2(n)$.

There are only **two possible cases** for any tile $i$:
1. **Case 1 (Tile $i$ is in its correct goal position):**
   Then $r_i = r_i^{\text{goal}}$ and $c_i = c_i^{\text{goal}}$, so:
   $$m_i = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad d_i = 0 + 0 = 0 \implies d_i = m_i$$
2. **Case 2 (Tile $i$ is misplaced):**
   Then $m_i = 1$. Since the tile is not in its goal square, it must be at least $1$ row or $1$ column away from its goal square (an integer $\ge 1$):
   $$d_i \ge 1 = m_i \implies d_i \ge m_i$$

Summing over all $8$ numbered tiles ($i = 1$ to $8$):
$$\sum_{i=1}^{8} d_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^{8} m_i \implies \mathbf{h_2(n) \ge h_1(n) \quad \text{for every state } n}$$

### Why Dominance Matters in $A^*$ Search
Because both are admissible and $h_1(n) \le h_2(n) \le h^*(n)$, **$h_2$ (Manhattan distance) is always closer to the true remaining cost $h^*(n)$ than $h_1$**. Consequently, **$A^*$ using $h_2$ is guaranteed to expand fewer (or at most an equal number of) nodes than $A^*$ using $h_1$**!

## 5. Solved University & Internal Exam Questions

### Question 1 [Series Test-II, Part A, Q2 (2 Marks)]
> **For the 8-puzzle state and goal shown below (`_` denotes the blank), compute $h_1$ (number of misplaced tiles) and $h_2$ (sum of Manhattan distances). Which heuristic dominates the other for this state?**
> 
> $$\text{Start} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 2 & 4 \\ 5 & \_ & 6 \\ 8 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \text{Goal} = \begin{bmatrix} \_ & 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 & 5 \\ 6 & 7 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$

#### **Solution:**

*(Note: This is the exact classic textbook example from Russell & Norvig, Figure 3.28!)*

#### **1. Compute $h_1$ (Number of Misplaced Tiles, excluding blank `_`):**
Let us check each of the $8$ numbered tiles ($1\text{ to }8$) against the Goal state:
* Tile **1**: At $(3, 3)$ instead of $(1, 2) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **2**: At $(1, 2)$ instead of $(1, 3) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **3**: At $(3, 2)$ instead of $(2, 1) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **4**: At $(1, 3)$ instead of $(2, 2) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **5**: At $(2, 1)$ instead of $(2, 3) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **6**: At $(2, 3)$ instead of $(3, 1) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **7**: At $(1, 1)$ instead of $(3, 2) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**
* Tile **8**: At $(3, 1)$ instead of $(3, 3) \rightarrow$ **Misplaced (1)**

All $8$ numbered tiles are out of place:
$$\mathbf{h_1(\text{Start}) = 8}$$

---

#### **2. Compute $h_2$ (Sum of Manhattan Distances for Tiles $1$ to $8$):**
Using $(r, c)$ coordinates from $(1,1)$ to $(3,3)$:

| Tile | Start Position $(r, c)$ | Goal Position $(r^{\text{goal}}, c^{\text{goal}})$ | Manhattan Distance $\|r - r^{\text{goal}}\| + \|c - c^{\text{goal}}\|$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1** | $(3, 3)$ | $(1, 2)$ | $\|3 - 1\| + \|3 - 2\| = 2 + 1 = \mathbf{3}$ |
| **2** | $(1, 2)$ | $(1, 3)$ | $\|1 - 1\| + \|2 - 3\| = 0 + 1 = \mathbf{1}$ |
| **3** | $(3, 2)$ | $(2, 1)$ | $\|3 - 2\| + \|2 - 1\| = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$ |
| **4** | $(1, 3)$ | $(2, 2)$ | $\|1 - 2\| + \|3 - 2\| = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$ |
| **5** | $(2, 1)$ | $(2, 3)$ | $\|2 - 2\| + \|1 - 3\| = 0 + 2 = \mathbf{2}$ |
| **6** | $(2, 3)$ | $(3, 1)$ | $\|2 - 3\| + \|3 - 1\| = 1 + 2 = \mathbf{3}$ |
| **7** | $(1, 1)$ | $(3, 2)$ | $\|1 - 3\| + \|1 - 2\| = 2 + 1 = \mathbf{3}$ |
| **8** | $(3, 1)$ | $(3, 3)$ | $\|3 - 3\| + \|1 - 3\| = 0 + 2 = \mathbf{2}$ |

Summing the Manhattan distances for tiles $1$ through $8$:
$$\mathbf{h_2(\text{Start})} = 3 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 3 + 3 + 2 = \mathbf{18}$$

---

#### **3. Which Heuristic Dominates the Other?**
* **$h_2$ (Manhattan distance) dominates $h_1$ (Misplaced tiles)** because $h_2(\text{Start}) = 18 > h_1(\text{Start}) = 8$ (and in general $h_2(n) \ge h_1(n)$ for all states $n$ while both remain admissible: $8 \le 18 \le h^*(\text{Start}) = 26$).

### Question 2 [Series Test-II, Part B, Q9(a) (5 Marks)]
> **Solve the 8-puzzle below with $A^*$, $f(n) = g(n) + h(n)$, each move costing $1$ (`_` is the blank).**
> 
> $$\text{Start} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & \_ & 4 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \text{Goal} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & \_ \end{bmatrix}$$
> 
> **(i) Compute $h_1$ (misplaced tiles) and $h_2$ (Manhattan distance) for the initial state.**  
> **(ii) Generate all legal successors (moves of the blank) and tabulate $g, h_1, h_2, f_1 = g + h_1$ and $f_2 = g + h_2$ for each.**  
> **(iii) Prove that $h_2(n) \ge h_1(n)$ for every state $n$.**

#### **Solution:**

---

#### **Part (i): Compute $h_1$ and $h_2$ for the Initial State ($g = 0$)**

Given Initial State $S_0$:
$$\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & \_ & 4 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \quad \text{and Goal} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & \_ \end{bmatrix}$$

| Tile | Initial Pos $(r, c)$ | Goal Pos $(r^{\text{goal}}, c^{\text{goal}})$ | Misplaced? ($m_i$) | Manhattan Distance ($d_i$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1** | $(2, 1)$ | $(1, 1)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|2-1\| + \|1-1\| = \mathbf{1}$ |
| **2** | $(1, 1)$ | $(1, 2)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|1-1\| + \|1-2\| = \mathbf{1}$ |
| **3** | $(1, 2)$ | $(1, 3)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|1-1\| + \|2-3\| = \mathbf{1}$ |
| **4** | $(2, 3)$ | $(2, 1)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|2-2\| + \|3-1\| = \mathbf{2}$ |
| **5** | $(1, 3)$ | $(2, 2)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|1-2\| + \|3-2\| = 1 + 1 = \mathbf{2}$ |
| **6** | $(3, 3)$ | $(2, 3)$ | Yes ($1$) | $\|3-2\| + \|3-3\| = \mathbf{1}$ |
| **7** | $(3, 1)$ | $(3, 1)$ | No ($0$) | $\|3-3\| + \|1-1\| = \mathbf{0}$ |
| **8** | $(3, 2)$ | $(3, 2)$ | No ($0$) | $\|3-3\| + \|2-2\| = \mathbf{0}$ |
| **Total** | — | — | **$h_1(S_0) = 6$** | **$h_2(S_0) = 8$** |

✅ **Answer for (i):**
* **$h_1(\text{Initial State}) = 6$** (Tiles $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ are misplaced; Tiles $7, 8$ are in place).
* **$h_2(\text{Initial State}) = 1 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 0 + 0 = 8$**.

---

#### **Part (ii): Generate All Legal Successors of Initial State and Tabulate $g, h_1, h_2, f_1, f_2$**

Since the blank `_` is at the center $(2, 2)$ of the initial state, there are **$4$ legal moves of the blank** (each at depth $g = 1$):

**1. Move Blank `UP` (Swap `_` with Tile `3`):**
$$S_{\text{Up}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & \_ & 5 \\ 1 & 3 & 4 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
* $g = 1$
* **Misplaced Tiles ($h_1$):** Tiles $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ are misplaced $\implies \mathbf{h_1 = 6} \implies \mathbf{f_1 = 1 + 6 = 7}$.
* **Manhattan Distance ($h_2$):** Tile $3$ moved from $(1,2)$ to $(2,2)$, increasing its distance to $(1,3)$ from $1$ to $2$. Thus $\mathbf{h_2 = 8 + 1 = 9} \implies \mathbf{f_2 = 1 + 9 = 10}$.

**2. Move Blank `DOWN` (Swap `_` with Tile `8`):**
$$S_{\text{Down}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & 8 & 4 \\ 7 & \_ & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
* $g = 1$
* **Misplaced Tiles ($h_1$):** Tile $8$ moved out of its goal square! So tiles $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8$ are misplaced $\implies \mathbf{h_1 = 7} \implies \mathbf{f_1 = 1 + 7 = 8}$.
* **Manhattan Distance ($h_2$):** Tile $8$ moved $1$ step away from its goal $\implies \mathbf{h_2 = 8 + 1 = 9} \implies \mathbf{f_2 = 1 + 9 = 10}$.

**3. Move Blank `LEFT` (Swap `_` with Tile `1`):**
$$S_{\text{Left}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ \_ & 1 & 4 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
* $g = 1$
* **Misplaced Tiles ($h_1$):** Tiles $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ are misplaced $\implies \mathbf{h_1 = 6} \implies \mathbf{f_1 = 1 + 6 = 7}$.
* **Manhattan Distance ($h_2$):** Tile $1$ moved from $(2,1)$ to $(2,2)$, increasing its distance to $(1,1)$ from $1$ to $2$ $\implies \mathbf{h_2 = 8 + 1 = 9} \implies \mathbf{f_2 = 1 + 9 = 10}$.

**4. Move Blank `RIGHT` (Swap `_` with Tile `4`):**
$$S_{\text{Right}} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & 4 & \_ \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$
* $g = 1$
* **Misplaced Tiles ($h_1$):** Tiles $1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ are still misplaced $\implies \mathbf{h_1 = 6} \implies \mathbf{f_1 = 1 + 6 = 7}$.
* **Manhattan Distance ($h_2$):** Tile $4$ moved from $(2,3)$ to $(2,2)$, **decreasing** its distance to its goal $(2,1)$ from $2$ to $1$! Thus $\mathbf{h_2 = 8 - 1 = 7} \implies \mathbf{f_2 = 1 + 7 = 8}$.

#### **Tabulated Comparison of All 4 Legal Successors ($g = 1$):**

| Successor Move (of Blank `_`) | Resulting Board State | $g(n)$ | $h_1(n)$ | $h_2(n)$ | $f_1 = g + h_1$ | $f_2 = g + h_2$ |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1. `UP`** *(swap with 3)* | `[2 _ 5 / 1 3 4 / 7 8 6]` | $1$ | $6$ | $9$ | $7$ | $10$ |
| **2. `DOWN`** *(swap with 8)* | `[2 3 5 / 1 8 4 / 7 _ 6]` | $1$ | $7$ | $9$ | $8$ | $10$ |
| **3. `LEFT`** *(swap with 1)* | `[2 3 5 / _ 1 4 / 7 8 6]` | $1$ | $6$ | $9$ | $7$ | $10$ |
| **4. `RIGHT`** *(swap with 4)* | **`[2 3 5 / 1 4 _ / 7 8 6]`** | **$1$** | **$6$** | **$7$** | **$7$** | **$8$ (Best!)** |

*(Notice how $h_1 = 6$ fails to distinguish between `UP`, `LEFT`, and `RIGHT`—giving $f_1 = 7$ for all three—whereas Manhattan distance $h_2$ immediately identifies **`RIGHT`** ($f_2 = 8$) as the unique best move because it moves Tile $4$ closer to its goal!)*

---

#### **Completing the $A^*$ Solution Path from $S_{\text{Right}}$ to Goal:**
Continuing $A^*$ from the best successor **`RIGHT`** ($g=1, h_2=7, f_2=8$), each step uniquely picks the minimum $f_2$:
* **Step 1 ($g=1$): Blank `RIGHT`** (swap with $4$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & 5 \\ 1 & 4 & \_ \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=7, f_2=8$)
* **Step 2 ($g=2$): Blank `UP`** (swap with $5$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 3 & \_ \\ 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=6, f_2=8$)
* **Step 3 ($g=3$): Blank `LEFT`** (swap with $3$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 2 & \_ & 3 \\ 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=4, f_2=7$)
* **Step 4 ($g=4$): Blank `LEFT`** (swap with $2$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} \_ & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=3, f_2=7$)
* **Step 5 ($g=5$): Blank `DOWN`** (swap with $1$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ \_ & 4 & 5 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=2, f_2=7$)
* **Step 6 ($g=6$): Blank `RIGHT`** (swap with $4$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & \_ & 5 \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=1, f_2=7$)
* **Step 7 ($g=7$): Blank `RIGHT`** (swap with $5$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & \_ \\ 7 & 8 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$ ($h_2=1, f_2=8$)
* **Step 8 ($g=8$): Blank `DOWN`** (swap with $6$) $\to \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & \_ \end{bmatrix}$ (**GOAL! $h_2=0, f_2=8$**)

---

#### **Part (iii): Prove that $h_2(n) \ge h_1(n)$ for Every State $n$**
* Let $m_i \in \{0, 1\}$ denote whether tile $i$ ($i = 1, \dots, 8$) is misplaced in state $n$, so $h_1(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{8} m_i$.
* Let $d_i = |r_i - r_i^{\text{goal}}| + |c_i - c_i^{\text{goal}}|$ denote the Manhattan distance of tile $i$ from its goal position, so $h_2(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{8} d_i$.
* For each tile $i \in \{1, \dots, 8\}$:
  1. If tile $i$ is **in its goal position**, then $m_i = 0$ and $d_i = 0$, so $d_i \ge m_i$.
  2. If tile $i$ is **misplaced**, then $m_i = 1$, and since its current row $r_i$ or column $c_i$ differs from its goal by at least $1$ integer step, $d_i \ge 1 = m_i$.
* Summing over all $8$ tiles:
  $$h_2(n) = \sum_{i=1}^{8} d_i \ge \sum_{i=1}^{8} m_i = h_1(n) \implies \mathbf{h_2(n) \ge h_1(n)} \quad \text{for every state } n. \quad \blacksquare$$
