# Module 3.6: The $N$-Queens Problem

## 1. Introduction to the $N$-Queens Problem
The **$N$-Queens Problem** is a classic combinatorial puzzle and Constraint Satisfaction / Search benchmark in Artificial Intelligence (originally proposed as the *8-Queens Puzzle* by Max Bezzel in 1848).

* **Objective:** Place **$N$ chess queens** on an **$N \times N$ chessboard** such that **no two queens attack (threaten) each other**.
* **Existence of Solutions:** Solutions exist for all natural numbers $N \ge 1$ **except** $N = 2$ and $N = 3$:
  * For **$N = 4$ (4-Queens):** There are **$2$ distinct solutions** (`[2, 4, 1, 3]` and `[3, 1, 4, 2]`).
  * For **$N = 6$ (6-Queens):** There are **$4$ distinct solutions** (including `[2, 4, 6, 1, 3, 5]`).
  * For **$N = 8$ (8-Queens):** There are **$92$ distinct solutions** ($12$ fundamental solutions up to rotation/reflection).

## 2. Constraints in Placing Queens on a Chessboard (Crucial for Exams!)

In chess, a Queen can move and attack any number of squares **horizontally (along its row)**, **vertically (along its column)**, and **diagonally (along both main and anti-diagonals)**.

Therefore, for any two distinct queens $Q_i$ placed at position $(r_i, c_i)$ and $Q_j$ placed at position $(r_j, c_j)$ (where $i \neq j$), the **three non-attacking constraints** are:

### 1. Row Constraint (No Two Queens in the Same Row)
$$r_i \neq r_j$$
* Every row from $1$ to $N$ can contain **at most one** (and therefore **exactly one**) queen.

### 2. Column Constraint (No Two Queens in the Same Column)
$$c_i \neq c_j$$
* Every column from $1$ to $N$ can contain **exactly one** queen.

### 3. Diagonal Constraint (No Two Queens on the Same Diagonal)
$$|r_i - r_j| \neq |c_i - c_j|$$
* The absolute difference between their row indices must **not** equal the absolute difference between their column indices.
* Equivalently, this covers both diagonal directions:
  * **Main Diagonal ($\searrow$ Top-Left to Bottom-Right):** $r_i - c_i \neq r_j - c_j$
  * **Anti-Diagonal ($\swarrow$ Top-Right to Bottom-Left):** $r_i + c_i \neq r_j + c_j$

---

## 3. State-Space Representation: 1D Tuple Encoding `[c1, c2, ..., cN]`
If we naively tried to place $8$ queens on any of the $64$ squares of an $8 \times 8$ board, there would be $\binom{64}{8} \approx 4.4 \times 10^9$ states!

To drastically reduce the state space, we enforce the **Row Constraint automatically** by placing **one queen per row** (Row $1$, Row $2$, $\dots$, Row $N$):
* A state is represented as a **1D list/tuple** of length $k \le N$:
  $$\text{State} = [c_1, c_2, c_3, \dots, c_k]$$
  where the **list index $r$** ($1$-based) is the **Row number**, and the **value $c_r \in \{1, 2, \dots, N\}$** is the **Column number** of the queen placed in Row $r$.
* Now, when trying to place a queen in a new row $r$ at column $c$, we only need to check against each already placed queen $(r_i, c_i)$ for $i = 1, \dots, r-1$:
  1. **Column Test:** Is $c == c_i$? (If yes $\rightarrow$ **Reject: Column conflict** with Row $i$).
  2. **Diagonal Test:** Is $|r - r_i| == |c - c_i|$? (If yes $\rightarrow$ **Reject: Diagonal conflict** with Row $i$).

---

## 4. Two Formulations of the $N$-Queens Problem

1. **Incremental Formulation (Solved by DFS Backtracking):**
   * **Initial State:** Empty chessboard `[]` (no queens on the board).
   * **Action:** Add a queen to the next unassigned row $r$ in the first safe column $c \in \{1, \dots, N\}$ that does not violate the Column or Diagonal constraints with rows $1 \dots r-1$.
   * **Backtracking:** If a row $r$ has **no safe column** available (dead end), **backtrack** to row $r - 1$ and move the queen in row $r - 1$ to its next available safe column.
   * **Goal Test:** All $N$ queens are placed safely on the board (depth $N$).

2. **Complete-State Formulation (Solved by Local Search / Min-Conflicts):**
   * Starts with all $N$ queens already placed on the board (one per column) and iteratively moves a conflicted queen within its column to the square with the minimum number of attacks ($h = \text{number of attacking pairs of queens}$, where $h = 0$ is the Goal).

## 5. Quick Walkthrough: 4-Queens Problem Using Backtracking
Let us trace 4-Queens ($N = 4$) trying columns $1, 2, 3, 4$ in order:
1. **Row 1:** Place $Q_1$ at Col $1 \rightarrow$ `[1]`.
2. **Row 2:**
   * Col $1$ (Col conflict with $Q_1$), Col $2$ (Diag conflict with $Q_1$).
   * Col $3$ is safe $\rightarrow$ `[1, 3]`.
3. **Row 3:** From `[1, 3]`:
   * Col $1$ (Col with $Q_1$), Col $2$ (Diag with $Q_2$), Col $3$ (Col with $Q_2$), Col $4$ (Diag with $Q_2$).
   * **Dead End!** Backtrack to Row 2 and move $Q_2$ to Col $4 \rightarrow$ `[1, 4]`.
4. **Row 3 (from `[1, 4]`):** Col $2$ is safe $\rightarrow$ `[1, 4, 2]`.
5. **Row 4 (from `[1, 4, 2]`):** All columns $1, 2, 3, 4$ are attacked! **Dead End!** Backtrack all the way to **Row 1**!
6. **Row 1 (Retry):** Move $Q_1$ to Col $2 \rightarrow$ `[2]`.
7. **Row 2:** Col $1, 2, 3$ attacked $\rightarrow$ Col $4$ is safe $\rightarrow$ **`[2, 4]`**.
8. **Row 3:** Col $1$ is safe $\rightarrow$ **`[2, 4, 1]`**.
9. **Row 4:** Col $1, 2$ attacked $\rightarrow$ Col $3$ is safe $\rightarrow$ **`[2, 4, 1, 3]` (GOAL!)**

```text
  Col 1   Col 2   Col 3   Col 4
+-------+-------+-------+-------+
|   .   |  Q1   |   .   |   .   |  Row 1 (Col 2)
+-------+-------+-------+-------+
|   .   |   .   |   .   |  Q2   |  Row 2 (Col 4)
+-------+-------+-------+-------+
|  Q3   |   .   |   .   |   .   |  Row 3 (Col 1)
+-------+-------+-------+-------+
|   .   |   .   |  Q4   |   .   |  Row 4 (Col 3)
+-------+-------+-------+-------+
```

## 6. Solved University & Internal Exam Questions

### Question 1 [End Semester Exam - Nov 2025, Part A, Q6 (2 Marks)] & [Series Test-II, Part A, Q3 (2 Marks)]
> **Mention the constraints in placing queens on a chessboard.**

#### **Solution:**
When placing $N$ queens on an $N \times N$ chessboard, no two queens $Q_i(r_i, c_i)$ and $Q_j(r_j, c_j)$ (for $i \neq j$) are allowed to attack each other. This requires satisfying **three fundamental constraints**:

1. **Row Constraint (No Horizontal Attack):** No two queens can be placed in the same row:
   $$r_i \neq r_j$$
2. **Column Constraint (No Vertical Attack):** No two queens can be placed in the same column:
   $$c_i \neq c_j$$
3. **Diagonal Constraint (No Diagonal Attack):** No two queens can be placed on the same main diagonal ($\searrow$) or anti-diagonal ($\swarrow$), meaning the absolute difference between their rows must not equal the absolute difference between their columns:
   $$|r_i - r_j| \neq |c_i - c_j|$$

### Question 2 [Series Test-II, Part B, Q9(b) (5 Marks)]
> **In the 6-Queen problem, queens are placed row by row (list value = column of the queen in that row). The partial state `[2, 4, 6]` has been reached. Complete the board using backtracking, trying columns in increasing order. For rows 4, 5 and 6 show the column and diagonal tests that reject each unsafe column.**

#### **Solution:**

**Given Partial State for $N = 6$:**
$$\text{State} = [2, 4, 6]$$
This means three queens are already safely placed in the first $3$ rows at $(r_i, c_i)$:
* **Row 1 ($Q_1$):** $(r_1, c_1) = (1, 2)$
* **Row 2 ($Q_2$):** $(r_2, c_2) = (2, 4)$
* **Row 3 ($Q_3$):** $(r_3, c_3) = (3, 6)$

**Safety Conditions for placing a new Queen at $(r, c)$ against existing Queens $(r_i, c_i)$:**
1. **Column Test:** Reject column $c$ if $c == c_i$ (already occupied in row $r_i$).
2. **Diagonal Test:** Reject column $c$ if $|r - r_i| == |c - c_i|$ (diagonal attack with queen in row $r_i$).

---

#### **Step 1: Placing Queen $Q_4$ in Row $r = 4$**
Current state is `[2, 4, 6]` (Occupied columns: $\{2, 4, 6\}$). Try columns $c = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ in increasing order:

* **Try Column $c = 1$ at $(4, 1)$:**
  * **Column Test:** $1 \notin \{2, 4, 6\} \rightarrow$ **Pass** (No column conflict).
  * **Diagonal Test:**
    * Against $Q_1(1, 2)$: $|4 - 1| = 3 \neq |1 - 2| = 1 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_2(2, 4)$: $|4 - 2| = 2 \neq |1 - 4| = 3 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_3(3, 6)$: $|4 - 3| = 1 \neq |1 - 6| = 5 \rightarrow$ Safe.
  * ✅ **Result:** **Column $c = 1$ is SAFE!** Place $Q_4$ at $(4, 1)$.

*(For completeness, if we also show why Columns $2$ to $6$ in Row 4 would be rejected):*
* *Col $2$ at $(4, 2)$:* **Rejected by Column Test** ($c = c_1 = 2$, same column as $Q_1$).
* *Col $3$ at $(4, 3)$:* **Rejected by Diagonal Test** with $Q_2(2, 4)$ since $|4 - 2| = |3 - 4| = 1$ ( wait: $|4-2|=2 \neq 1$; actually $(4,3)$ is safe from $Q_1, Q_2, Q_3$, though we already selected the first valid column $c = 1$!).
* *Col $4$ at $(4, 4)$:* **Rejected by Column Test** ($c = c_2 = 4$, same column as $Q_2$).
* *Col $5$ at $(4, 5)$:* **Rejected by Diagonal Test** with $Q_1(1, 2)$ ($|4-1|=|5-2|=3$) and $Q_3(3, 6)$ ($|4-3|=|5-6|=1$).
* *Col $6$ at $(4, 6)$:* **Rejected by Column Test** ($c = c_3 = 6$, same column as $Q_3$).

**Updated Partial State after Row 4:** **`[2, 4, 6, 1]`**

---

#### **Step 2: Placing Queen $Q_5$ in Row $r = 5$**
Current state is `[2, 4, 6, 1]` with Queens at $Q_1(1,2), \; Q_2(2,4), \; Q_3(3,6), \; Q_4(4,1)$. Try columns $c = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ in increasing order:

* **Try Column $c = 1$ at $(5, 1)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_4 = 1$ (Same column as $Q_4$ at $(4, 1)$).
* **Try Column $c = 2$ at $(5, 2)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_1 = 2$ (Same column as $Q_1$ at $(1, 2)$).
  * *(Also rejected by Diagonal Test with $Q_4(4, 1)$ since $|5 - 4| = |2 - 1| = 1$).*
* **Try Column $c = 3$ at $(5, 3)$:**
  * **Column Test:** $3 \notin \{2, 4, 6, 1\} \rightarrow$ **Pass**.
  * **Diagonal Test:**
    * Against $Q_1(1, 2)$: $|5 - 1| = 4 \neq |3 - 2| = 1 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_2(2, 4)$: $|5 - 2| = 3 \neq |3 - 4| = 1 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_3(3, 6)$: $|5 - 3| = 2 \neq |3 - 6| = 3 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_4(4, 1)$: $|5 - 4| = 1 \neq |3 - 1| = 2 \rightarrow$ Safe.
  * ✅ **Result:** **Column $c = 3$ is SAFE!** Place $Q_5$ at $(5, 3)$.

**Updated Partial State after Row 5:** **`[2, 4, 6, 1, 3]`**

---

#### **Step 3: Placing Queen $Q_6$ in Row $r = 6$**
Current state is `[2, 4, 6, 1, 3]` with Queens at $Q_1(1,2), \; Q_2(2,4), \; Q_3(3,6), \; Q_4(4,1), \; Q_5(5,3)$. Try columns $c = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$ in increasing order:

* **Try Column $c = 1$ at $(6, 1)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_4 = 1$ (Same column as $Q_4$ at $(4, 1)$).
* **Try Column $c = 2$ at $(6, 2)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_1 = 2$ (Same column as $Q_1$ at $(1, 2)$).
  * ❌ *(Also rejected by Diagonal Test with $Q_5(5, 3)$ since $|6 - 5| = |2 - 3| = 1$).*
* **Try Column $c = 3$ at $(6, 3)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_5 = 3$ (Same column as $Q_5$ at $(5, 3)$).
  * ❌ *(Also rejected by Diagonal Test with $Q_3(3, 6)$ since $|6 - 3| = |3 - 6| = 3$, and $Q_4(4, 1)$ since $|6 - 4| = |3 - 1| = 2$).*
* **Try Column $c = 4$ at $(6, 4)$:**
  * ❌ **Rejected by Column Test:** $c = c_2 = 4$ (Same column as $Q_2$ at $(2, 4)$).
  * ❌ *(Also rejected by Diagonal Test with $Q_5(5, 3)$ since $|6 - 5| = |4 - 3| = 1$).*
* **Try Column $c = 5$ at $(6, 5)$:**
  * **Column Test:** $5 \notin \{2, 4, 6, 1, 3\} \rightarrow$ **Pass**.
  * **Diagonal Test:**
    * Against $Q_1(1, 2)$: $|6 - 1| = 5 \neq |5 - 2| = 3 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_2(2, 4)$: $|6 - 2| = 4 \neq |5 - 4| = 1 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_3(3, 6)$: $|6 - 3| = 3 \neq |5 - 6| = 1 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_4(4, 1)$: $|6 - 4| = 2 \neq |5 - 1| = 4 \rightarrow$ Safe.
    * Against $Q_5(5, 3)$: $|6 - 5| = 1 \neq |5 - 3| = 2 \rightarrow$ Safe.
  * ✅ **Result:** **Column $c = 5$ is SAFE!** Place $Q_6$ at $(6, 5)$.

---

#### **Final Completed 6-Queens Board Solution: `[2, 4, 6, 1, 3, 5]`**

```text
      Col 1   Col 2   Col 3   Col 4   Col 5   Col 6
    +-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
R1  |   .   |  Q1   |   .   |   .   |   .   |   .   |  --> 2
    +-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
R2  |   .   |   .   |   .   |  Q2   |   .   |   .   |  --> 4
    +-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
R3  |   .   |   .   |   .   |   .   |   .   |  Q3   |  --> 6
    +-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
R4  |  Q4   |   .   |   .   |   .   |   .   |   .   |  --> 1
    +-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
R5  |   .   |   .   |  Q5   |   .   |   .   |   .   |  --> 3

```
