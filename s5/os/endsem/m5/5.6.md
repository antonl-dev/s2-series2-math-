# 5.6 Disk Scheduling

---

## 1. Introduction: Why do we need Disk Scheduling?

As discussed in Topic 5.5, the major performance bottleneck for a magnetic Hard Disk Drive (HDD) is the **mechanical movement** of its parts. 
- The total disk access time is dominated by the **Seek Time** (the time it takes to move the heavy mechanical actuator arm to the correct cylinder/track).
- To maximize the performance (throughput) of an HDD, the Operating System must **minimize the total seek time**.

**The Problem:** In a multiprogramming system, many processes constantly send I/O requests to the disk. These requests are placed in a queue. If the OS simply processes these requests in the exact order they arrive, the disk arm might swing wildly back and forth across the entire disk, wasting massive amounts of time. 

**The Solution:** The OS uses a **Disk Scheduling Algorithm** to rearrange the order of the pending requests in the queue to minimize the total mechanical head movement.

*(Note: Disk scheduling is utterly irrelevant for SSDs because they have no moving parts and zero seek time. SSDs process requests using simple FCFS or internally optimized parallel access algorithms. The algorithms below apply exclusively to mechanical HDDs).*

---

## 2. Disk Scheduling Algorithms

To evaluate these algorithms, we typically use a request queue of cylinder numbers. 
- *Example Queue:* `98, 183, 37, 122, 14, 124, 65, 67`
- *Current Head Position:* `53`
- *Total Cylinders:* `0 to 199`

The primary metric of efficiency is the **Total Head Movement** (measured in cylinders).

### 1. FCFS (First-Come, First-Served)
- **How it works:** Processes requests in the exact order they arrived in the queue.
- **Example Trace:** $53 \rightarrow 98 \rightarrow 183 \rightarrow 37 \rightarrow 122 \dots$
- **Total Movement:** $|98-53| + |183-98| + |37-183| \dots = 640$ cylinders.
- **Pros:** Completely fair, no starvation. Easy to program.
- **Cons:** Very inefficient. The head swings wildly, resulting in high total head movement.

### 2. SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First)
- **How it works:** From the current head position, select the request in the queue that is **closest** (has the minimum seek distance). 
- **Example Trace:** 
  - Current=53. Closest is 65 (dist 12).
  - Current=65. Closest is 67 (dist 2).
  - Current=67. Closest is 37 (dist 30).
  - Path: $53 \rightarrow 65 \rightarrow 67 \rightarrow 37 \rightarrow 14 \rightarrow 98 \dots$
- **Total Movement:** 236 cylinders.
- **Pros:** Vastly improves performance over FCFS.
- **Cons:** **Starvation!** If a steady stream of requests keeps arriving near the current head position, a request far away at the edge of the disk will never be serviced.

### 3. SCAN (The Elevator Algorithm)
- **How it works:** The disk arm starts at one end of the disk and moves toward the other end, servicing requests as it goes. When it hits the absolute physical end of the disk (e.g., cylinder 0 or 199), it **reverses direction** and services requests moving the other way.
- **Example Trace (Moving towards 0):** 
  - Head moves down: $53 \rightarrow 37 \rightarrow 14 \rightarrow 0$. (Hits the end).
  - Head reverses up: $0 \rightarrow 65 \rightarrow 67 \rightarrow 98 \rightarrow 122 \rightarrow 124 \rightarrow 183$.
- **Total Movement:** 236 cylinders.
- **Pros:** Fairer than SSTF; prevents starvation. 
- **Cons:** If the head just swept past a cylinder, and a new request arrives there, that request must wait for the head to go all the way to the end and come all the way back.

### 4. C-SCAN (Circular SCAN)
- **How it works:** Designed to provide a more uniform wait time. It moves the head in one direction (e.g., UP), servicing requests until it reaches the end. When it reaches the end, it immediately **returns to the beginning (0) without servicing any requests on the return trip**, and starts scanning UP again.
- **Analogy:** A circular list. The disk wraps around from cylinder 199 directly to cylinder 0.
- **Example Trace (Moving UP):** 
  - $53 \rightarrow 65 \rightarrow 67 \rightarrow 98 \rightarrow 122 \rightarrow 124 \rightarrow 183 \rightarrow 199$. 
  - Rapid return to 0 (no servicing).
  - $0 \rightarrow 14 \rightarrow 37$.
- **Pros:** More uniform wait times than standard SCAN.

### 5. LOOK and C-LOOK
- **How they work:** These are the practical versions of SCAN and C-SCAN. Instead of blindly going all the way to the physical ends of the disk (cylinder 0 or 199), the arm only goes as far as the **last request** in that direction, then reverses.

## 3. Exam Questions & Answers

> **Note on Provided Question Papers:** No disk scheduling numericals appeared in the provided papers, but because it is an explicit syllabus topic, numerical problems on FCFS/SSTF/SCAN are almost guaranteed in typical End Semester Exams. Below is a comprehensive expected problem.

### Expected Question 1 (Disk Scheduling Calculation)
**Question:**
*Suppose a magnetic disk drive has 200 cylinders, numbered 0 to 199. The drive is currently serving a request at cylinder 50, and the previous request was at cylinder 45 (meaning the head is moving UP towards 199). The queue of pending requests, in FIFO order, is:*
**82, 170, 43, 140, 24, 16, 190**
*Calculate the total head movement (in cylinders) for the following algorithms:*
*a) FCFS*
*b) SSTF*
*c) SCAN*

**Answer / Solution:**

**a) FCFS (First-Come, First-Served):**
Process exactly in the queue order:
- $50 \rightarrow 82$ (Dist: 32)
- $82 \rightarrow 170$ (Dist: 88)
- $170 \rightarrow 43$ (Dist: 127)
- $43 \rightarrow 140$ (Dist: 97)
- $140 \rightarrow 24$ (Dist: 116)
- $24 \rightarrow 16$ (Dist: 8)
- $16 \rightarrow 190$ (Dist: 174)
- **Total Head Movement:** $32 + 88 + 127 + 97 + 116 + 8 + 174 = \textbf{642 cylinders}$.

**b) SSTF (Shortest Seek Time First):**
Always pick the closest number to the current position. Start at 50.
- Queue: [82, 170, 43, 140, 24, 16, 190]
- From 50, closest is 43. (Dist: 7)
- From 43, closest is 24. (Dist: 19)
- From 24, closest is 16. (Dist: 8)
- From 16, closest is 82. (Dist: 66)
- From 82, closest is 140. (Dist: 58)
- From 140, closest is 170. (Dist: 30)
- From 170, closest is 190. (Dist: 20)
- **Total Head Movement:** $7 + 19 + 8 + 66 + 58 + 30 + 20 = \textbf{208 cylinders}$.

**c) SCAN (Elevator Algorithm):**
The problem states the head is moving UP (from 45 to 50, so towards 199).
- Queue sorted: [16, 24, 43, 82, 140, 170, 190]
- **Move UP:** $50 \rightarrow 82 \rightarrow 140 \rightarrow 170 \rightarrow 190 \rightarrow \textbf{199 (Physical End!)}$
- **Reverse Down:** $199 \rightarrow 43 \rightarrow 24 \rightarrow 16$.
- *Calculation:*
  - Distance UP: $199 - 50 = 149$
  - Distance DOWN: $199 - 16 = 183$
- **Total Head Movement:** $149 + 183 = \textbf{332 cylinders}$.

### Expected Question 2 (Theory)
**Question:**
*Why is disk scheduling necessary for magnetic hard drives but not for Solid-State Drives (SSDs)? What is the major disadvantage of the SSTF algorithm?*

**Answer:**
**Necessity (2 Marks):**
Disk scheduling is entirely focused on rearranging pending I/O requests to minimize **seek time**—the physical, mechanical time it takes to move the heavy magnetic read-write head across the platters of a Hard Disk Drive. Because Solid-State Drives (SSDs) use purely electronic flash memory with absolutely zero moving parts, they have no mechanical seek time. Therefore, complex mechanical scheduling algorithms like SCAN or SSTF provide no benefit to SSDs and only waste CPU overhead.

**SSTF Disadvantage (2 Marks):**
The Shortest Seek Time First (SSTF) algorithm always selects the request closest to the current head position. Its major disadvantage is **starvation**. If a constant stream of new requests keeps arriving clustered near the current head position, the head will stay in that local area indefinitely. Requests located far away at the physical edges of the disk will be ignored forever.

### Quick Self-Check Key Points for Revision
- **Goal:** Minimize mechanical **Seek Time** (Total Head Movement).
- **FCFS:** Dumb, fair, huge movement.
- **SSTF:** Always pick closest. Fast, but causes **Starvation**.
- **SCAN:** Elevator. Go all the way to 199, then reverse.
- **C-SCAN:** Go all the way to 199, then immediately warp back to 0 without doing anything, and go UP again. Gives very uniform wait times.
- **LOOK:** Like SCAN, but don't hit the wall (199). Stop at the last request and turn around.
