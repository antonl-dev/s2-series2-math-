# 2.6 Process Scheduling Algorithms: First-Come, First-Served (FCFS) and Shortest Job First (SJF)

---

## 1. Scheduling Criteria (Metrics)

Before diving into specific algorithms, we need to understand how we measure their performance. The CPU scheduler makes decisions based on the following criteria:

- **CPU Utilization:** Keep the CPU as busy as possible (0% to 100%).
- **Throughput:** The number of processes completed per time unit.
- **Turnaround Time (TAT):** The total time taken from the submission of a process to its completion.
  - $\text{TAT} = \text{Completion Time (CT)} - \text{Arrival Time (AT)}$
- **Waiting Time (WT):** The total time a process spends waiting in the Ready Queue. 
  - $\text{WT} = \text{Turnaround Time (TAT)} - \text{Burst Time (BT)}$
  - *(Alternatively, for non-preemptive algorithms: $\text{WT} = \text{Start Time} - \text{Arrival Time}$)*
- **Response Time (RT):** The time from the submission of a request until the *first* response is produced (first time it gets the CPU).

**Optimization Goal:** Maximize CPU utilization and throughput; Minimize turnaround time, waiting time, and response time.

---

## 2. First-Come, First-Served (FCFS) Scheduling

### How it Works
FCFS is the simplest CPU scheduling algorithm. 
- **Rule:** The process that requests the CPU first is allocated the CPU first.
- **Implementation:** It is easily managed using a FIFO (First-In, First-Out) queue. When a process enters the ready queue, its PCB is linked to the tail of the queue. When the CPU is free, it is allocated to the process at the head of the queue.
- **Preemption:** FCFS is strictly **Non-Preemptive**. Once a process gets the CPU, it holds it until it completes its CPU burst.

### Advantages and Disadvantages
- **Advantages:** Very simple to understand and write code for. No complex starvation issues.
- **Disadvantages:** The average waiting time is often quite long. 
- **The Convoy Effect:** If a very long CPU-bound process arrives first, all other shorter processes (and I/O-bound processes) get stuck waiting behind it. This is called the convoy effect, similar to a line of fast cars stuck behind a slow truck on a one-lane road. It results in poor CPU and device utilization.

## 3. Shortest Job First (SJF) Scheduling

### How it Works
- **Rule:** When the CPU is available, it is assigned to the process that has the **smallest next CPU burst**. If two processes have the same next CPU burst, FCFS is used to break the tie.

### Two Variations of SJF
1. **Non-Preemptive SJF:** Once the CPU is given to a process, it cannot be preempted until it completes its CPU burst.
2. **Preemptive SJF (Shortest Remaining Time First - SRTF):** If a new process arrives in the ready queue with a CPU burst length that is *shorter than what is left* of the currently executing process, the OS will preempt (pause) the current process and give the CPU to the new, shorter process.

### Advantages and Disadvantages
- **Advantages:** SJF is **provably optimal**. It gives the absolute minimum average waiting time for a given set of processes. (Moving a short process before a long one decreases the wait time of the short process more than it increases the wait time of the long process).
- **Disadvantages:** 
  - **Starvation:** A continuous stream of short processes can cause a long process to wait forever in the ready queue.
  - **Predictability Problem:** In a real OS, there is no way to know the exact length of a process's *next* CPU burst in advance. The OS must *predict* the next burst using exponential averaging of past bursts, which adds overhead.

### Question 1 (FCFS Calculation)
**Source:** *End Semester Examination – November 2025 (Module 2, Q3 – Partial: FCFS portion)*
*(Note: The exam question asks for both FCFS and Round Robin. The FCFS solution is detailed here; Round Robin will be solved in `2.7.md`)*

**Question:**
*Assume you have the following jobs shown in the table to execute with one processor. Calculate the average waiting time and average turnaround time if the system uses First Come First Serve (FCFS).*

| Process | Burst Time (ms) | Arrival Time (ms) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **P0** | 11 | 0 |
| **P1** | 13 | 5 |
| **P2** | 6 | 9 |
| **P3** | 9 | 13 |
| **P4** | 12 | 17 |

**Answer / Solution:**

**1. FCFS Gantt Chart:**
Since it is First-Come, First-Served, processes execute purely in the order of their Arrival Time (P0 $\rightarrow$ P1 $\rightarrow$ P2 $\rightarrow$ P3 $\rightarrow$ P4). It is non-preemptive.

- **P0** arrives at `0` and runs for `11`. (Finishes at `11`).
- **P1** arrived at `5`, so it is waiting. It starts at `11` and runs for `13`. (Finishes at $11 + 13 = 24$).
- **P2** arrived at `9`. It starts at `24` and runs for `6`. (Finishes at $24 + 6 = 30$).
- **P3** arrived at `13`. It starts at `30` and runs for `9`. (Finishes at $30 + 9 = 39$).
- **P4** arrived at `17`. It starts at `39` and runs for `12`. (Finishes at $39 + 12 = 51$).

*Gantt Chart Representation:*
`|--P0(0-11)--|--P1(11-24)--|--P2(24-30)--|--P3(30-39)--|--P4(39-51)--|`

**2. Calculation Table:**
*Formulas used:*
- Completion Time (CT) = Time at which process finishes execution in Gantt Chart.
- Turnaround Time (TAT) = CT - Arrival Time (AT)
- Waiting Time (WT) = TAT - Burst Time (BT)

| Process | Arrival Time (AT) | Burst Time (BT) | Completion Time (CT) | Turnaround Time (TAT = CT - AT) | Waiting Time (WT = TAT - BT) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **P0** | 0 | 11 | 11 | 11 - 0 = **11** | 11 - 11 = **0** |
| **P1** | 5 | 13 | 24 | 24 - 5 = **19** | 19 - 13 = **6** |
| **P2** | 9 | 6 | 30 | 30 - 9 = **21** | 21 - 6 = **15** |
| **P3** | 13 | 9 | 39 | 39 - 13 = **26** | 26 - 9 = **17** |
| **P4** | 17 | 12 | 51 | 51 - 17 = **34** | 34 - 12 = **22** |
| **TOTAL** | | | | **111 ms** | **60 ms** |

**3. Final Averages:**
- **Average Turnaround Time:** $\frac{\text{Total TAT}}{\text{Number of Processes}} = \frac{111}{5} =$ **22.2 ms**
- **Average Waiting Time:** $\frac{\text{Total WT}}{\text{Number of Processes}} = \frac{60}{5} =$ **12.0 ms**

### Expected Question 2 (SJF/SRTF Calculation - Crucial for Exam Prep)
*(Note: As SJF is explicitly in the syllabus but absent from the provided past papers, a standard problem is provided to ensure full preparation).*

**Question:**
*Find the Average Waiting Time and Average Turnaround Time for the following processes using the Preemptive Shortest Job First (SRTF) algorithm.*

| Process | Arrival Time | Burst Time |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **P1** | 0 | 8 |
| **P2** | 1 | 4 |
| **P3** | 2 | 2 |
| **P4** | 3 | 1 |

**Answer / Solution:**

**1. Preemptive SJF (SRTF) Execution Walkthrough:**
- **At t=0:** Only P1 is available. It starts executing. (Remaining: P1=8)
- **At t=1:** P1 has executed for 1ms (Remaining: P1=7). P2 arrives (BT=4). 
  Since 4 < 7, P1 is **preempted**, and P2 starts.
- **At t=2:** P2 has executed for 1ms (Remaining: P2=3). P3 arrives (BT=2).
  Since 2 < 3, P2 is **preempted**, and P3 starts.
- **At t=3:** P3 has executed for 1ms (Remaining: P3=1). P4 arrives (BT=1).
  Remaining times: P1=7, P2=3, P3=1, P4=1. Tie between P3 and P4. (Using FCFS tie-breaker, P3 continues).
- **At t=4:** P3 finishes. Next shortest is P4 (BT=1). P4 starts.
- **At t=5:** P4 finishes. Next shortest is P2 (Remaining=3). P2 resumes.
- **At t=8:** P2 finishes. Next shortest is P1 (Remaining=7). P1 resumes.
- **At t=15:** P1 finishes.

*Gantt Chart Representation:*
`| P1(0-1) | P2(1-2) | P3(2-4) | P4(4-5) | P2(5-8) | P1(8-15) |`

**2. Calculation Table:**

| Process | AT | BT | CT | TAT (CT - AT) | WT (TAT - BT) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **P1** | 0 | 8 | 15 | 15 - 0 = 15 | 15 - 8 = 7 |
| **P2** | 1 | 4 | 8 | 8 - 1 = 7 | 7 - 4 = 3 |
| **P3** | 2 | 2 | 4 | 4 - 2 = 2 | 2 - 2 = 0 |
| **P4** | 3 | 1 | 5 | 5 - 3 = 2 | 2 - 1 = 1 |
| **Total** | | | | **26** | **11** |

**3. Final Averages:**
- **Average Turnaround Time:** $26 / 4 = $ **6.5 ms**
- **Average Waiting Time:** $11 / 4 = $ **2.75 ms**

---

### Quick Self-Check Key Points for Revision
- **Formulas:** `TAT = CT - AT` and `WT = TAT - BT`.
- **FCFS:** Simplest, FIFO, non-preemptive. Suffers from the **Convoy Effect** (long process blocks short ones, causing high average WT).
- **SJF:** Picks the shortest burst next. Optimal for minimizing waiting time. Suffers from **Starvation** of long processes and difficulty predicting future CPU bursts.
- **SRTF (Preemptive SJF):** If a new arrival has a shorter burst than the *remaining* burst of the current process, preempt the current process!
