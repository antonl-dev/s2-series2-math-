## 1. Cost of the Project

### Definition
The **Cost of the Project** represents the **total initial capital expenditure (CapEx)** plus the **margin money for working capital** required to conceive, build, equip, test, and bring a project up to the stage of **commercial production/operation**.

Accurate estimation of project cost is essential to avoid mid-project funding shortages (cost overruns) and to determine the exact **Means of Finance** required.

[Click here to see the diagram for Components of Project Cost and Means of Finance](https://www.google.com/search?q=cost+of+project+and+means+of+finance+components+diagram&udm=2)

### Major Components of Cost of the Project
According to standard financial appraisal guidelines (used by banks and financial institutions), the total project cost consists of the following **nine heads**:

1. **Land and Site Development:**
   * Cost of purchasing or leasing land, stamp duty, registration charges, land leveling, boundary walls/fencing, internal roads, drainage, and site water/power connections.
2. **Buildings and Civil Works:**
   * Factory sheds, administrative offices, R&D labs, warehouses, equipment foundations, employee quarters, and architect/structural engineering fees.
3. **Plant and Machinery (Core Technical Assets):**
   * Cost of imported and indigenous machinery, spare parts, freight, transit insurance, customs duty, installation, erection, and commissioning charges.
4. **Technical Know-How and Engineering Fees:**
   * Payments for patents, technology licenses, technical collaboration, detailed engineering drawings, and consultant/DPR fees.
5. **Expenses on Foreign Technicians & Training:**
   * Travel, boarding, and fees for foreign specialists supervising erection, plus training expenses for domestic engineers/operators.
6. **Miscellaneous Fixed Assets:**
   * Supporting equipment not part of core production—furniture, computers, office automation, testing/lab instruments, vehicles, transformers, and backup DG sets.
7. **Preliminary and Capital Issue Expenses:**
   * Legal fees for company incorporation, feasibility reports, market surveys, and expenses for issuing shares/debentures (underwriting commission, brokerage, prospectus printing).
8. **Pre-Operative Expenses:**
   * Expenses incurred **before commercial production starts**: salaries of core staff during construction, rent, administrative overheads, **Interest During Construction (IDC)** on term loans, and trial run/commissioning expenses.
9. **Provision for Contingencies & Margin Money for Working Capital:**
   * **Contingency Provision:** Buffer (typically **5%–10%** of fixed capital) to absorb unforeseen price escalations or minor scope omissions.
   * **Margin Money for Working Capital:** Long-term portion of working capital (typically 25% of initial working capital requirement) that promoters/banks require to support day-to-day inventory and receivables once operations begin.

## 2. Means of Finance

### Definition
**Means of Finance** refers to the various **long-term sources of capital** mobilized to fund the total **Cost of the Project** (ensuring $\text{Total Means of Finance} = \text{Total Cost of Project}$).

Broadly, all sources of project finance fall into **two categories**:
1. **Owned Funds (Equity / Internal Capital)**
2. **Borrowed Funds (Debt / External Capital)**

[Click here to see the diagram for Means of Finance Owned vs Borrowed Funds](https://www.google.com/search?q=means+of+finance+equity+debt+sources+of+project+finance+diagram&udm=2)

### A. Owned Funds (Shareholders' / Promoters' Capital)
Funds contributed by the owners/promoters of the project or generated internally by the company:
1. **Equity Share Capital (Ordinary Shares):**
   * Permanent risk capital raised from promoters and public investors. Equity holders are **owners** with voting rights and receive **variable dividends** only out of residual profits.
2. **Preference Share Capital:**
   * Hybrid capital carrying a **fixed dividend rate** and priority over equity holders for dividends and capital repayment upon winding up, usually without voting rights.
3. **Retained Earnings (Internal Accruals / Ploughed-Back Profits):**
   * Undistributed accumulated profits reinvested by an existing company into expansion or modernization projects—zero flotation cost and no dilution of control.
4. **Capital Subsidies, Grants & Seed Capital:**
   * Non-repayable government subsidies (e.g., backward area incentives, renewable energy subsidies, startup incubation grants) treated like quasi-equity.

### B. Borrowed Funds (Debt Capital)
Funds raised as loans from banks, financial institutions, or debt markets that carry a **fixed repayment obligation**:
1. **Term Loans:**
   * Long-term loans (typically 5–15 years) provided by commercial banks and Development Financial Institutions (e.g., SIDBI, IFCI, IDBI, State Financial Corporations) secured against fixed assets.
2. **Debentures / Corporate Bonds:**
   * Debt certificates issued to investors carrying a fixed coupon (interest) rate—may be **Non-Convertible (NCD)** or **Convertible into equity (CCD)**.
3. **Deferred Credit (Supplier / Vendor Credit):**
   * Machinery suppliers accept payment in installments over 3–7 years against a bank guarantee.
4. **Leasing, Hire Purchase & Foreign Currency Loans:**
   * Equipment financing via lease rentals or External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) from international lenders.

## 3. Owned Funds vs. Borrowed Funds (Detailed Comparison)

| Basis of Comparison | Owned Funds (Equity / Internal) | Borrowed Funds (Debt / External) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Nature & Status** | Represents **ownership capital**; providers are owners/shareholders of the enterprise. | Represents **creditor capital**; providers are lenders/creditors, not owners. |
| **2. Repayment Obligation** | **Permanent capital** (no fixed maturity or mandatory repayment during company life, except redeemable preference shares). | **Temporary/Repayable**; principal must be repaid strictly per the agreed amortization schedule. |
| **3. Return / Servicing Cost** | **Variable dividends** paid only if sufficient profits exist; **not mandatory** during loss years. | **Fixed interest** must be paid regularly regardless of whether the project makes a profit or loss. |
| **4. Tax Treatment** | Dividends are paid out of **after-tax profit** (**no tax shield**). | Interest expense is **tax-deductible**, lowering the effective post-tax cost of debt (**Interest Tax Shield**). |
| **5. Risk to the Firm** | **Low financial risk** (no risk of bankruptcy/default from unpaid dividends). | **High financial risk** (defaulting on principal or interest can trigger insolvency/asset seizure). |
| **6. Voting Rights & Control** | Equity shareholders hold **voting rights** and influence management decisions (issuing new equity dilutes promoter control). | Lenders have **no voting rights** in routine operations (no ownership dilution), though they may impose loan covenants. |
| **7. Security / Collateral** | **Unsecured**; no mortgage of company assets is required. | Usually **secured** by mortgage/hypothecation of land, buildings, and machinery. |
| **8. Cost of Capital** | Higher expected return by investors due to higher risk, plus higher flotation cost (for public issues). | Generally **cheaper** than equity due to fixed lower interest rates and tax deductibility. |
| **9. Sources / Examples** | Equity shares, preference shares, retained earnings, government grants. | Bank term loans, debentures/bonds, deferred supplier credit, lease finance. |

## 4. Key Norms in Choosing the Financing Mix (Capital Structure)

Financial institutions evaluate three key ratios before approving a project's Means of Finance:
1. **Debt–Equity Ratio ($\text{Debt} / \text{Equity}$):** Typically maintained around **1.5:1 to 2:1** for industrial projects (higher for capital-intensive infrastructure like power/roads).
2. **Promoters' Contribution:** Promoters are usually required to bring in **20%–30%** of project cost from their own resources to demonstrate commitment ("skin in the game").
3. **Debt Service Coverage Ratio ($\text{DSCR}$):**
   $$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{Profit After Tax} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Interest on Term Loan}}{\text{Interest on Term Loan} + \text{Loan Principal Installment}}$$
   Banks typically require an average **DSCR $\ge 1.5$ to $2.0$** to ensure the project generates enough cash to repay borrowed funds safely.

### A. Previous Year / Exam Question (From Attached Question Papers)

#### Question 1 (8 Marks — End-Semester Full Question)
**Source:** *Fifth Semester B. Tech. Degree Examination, Regular, November 2025 (23HUT535, QP Code: 5010BHU25T535), Part B – Module 5, Q19*  
**Question:** Distinguish between owned funds and borrowed funds as means of finance.

**Answer:**

**1. Introduction (1 Mark):**  
To fund the total **Cost of a Project**, promoters mobilize long-term capital from various **Means of Finance**, which are broadly classified into **Owned Funds** (ownership/equity capital) and **Borrowed Funds** (debt/loan capital). Balancing these two determines the project's capital structure, financial risk, and cost of capital.

**2. Key Differences Between Owned Funds and Borrowed Funds (6 Marks):**

| Point of Distinction | Owned Funds | Borrowed Funds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Meaning & Status** | Capital contributed by owners/promoters or retained from profits; providers are **owners**. | Capital raised as loans from banks, institutions, or bondholders; providers are **creditors**. |
| **2. Sources / Instruments** | Equity share capital, preference share capital, retained earnings (internal accruals), and capital subsidies. | Bank term loans, debentures/bonds, deferred vendor credit, and foreign currency loans. |
| **3. Return on Capital** | Paid as **dividends**, which fluctuate with profits and are **not compulsory** in loss years. | Paid as **fixed interest**, which is legally **mandatory** regardless of profit or loss. |
| **4. Repayment of Principal** | **Permanent capital** (never returned during the company's normal lifetime, except redeemable preference shares). | **Repayable** over a fixed maturity period (e.g., 5–15 years) according to an installment schedule. |
| **5. Tax Advantage** | Dividends are distributed from **after-tax profits**; there is **no tax shield**. | Interest is a tax-deductible expense, providing a valuable **tax shield** and lowering effective cost. |
| **6. Control & Voting Rights** | Equity holders enjoy **voting rights** and management control; issuing new equity dilutes existing promoter control. | Lenders have **no voting rights** or ownership share, though they impose protective loan covenants. |
| **7. Financial Risk & Collateral** | **Low risk** to the firm and **unsecured** (no asset mortgage required). | **High financial risk** of default/bankruptcy and usually **secured** against fixed assets (land/machinery). |
| **8. Cost of Raising Funds** | **Higher cost of capital** because equity investors demand higher returns for bearing residual risk. | **Cheaper source** due to lower interest rates, lender security, and tax savings. |

**3. Conclusion / Optimal Mix (1 Mark):**  
An ideal project financing plan combines both sources—typically following a **1.5:1 or 2:1 Debt–Equity norm**—using **Owned Funds** to provide stability and risk absorption while leveraging **Borrowed Funds** for tax efficiency and preserving promoter control.

### B. Self-Created Practice Questions (Covering Cost of the Project)

#### Model Question 1 (2 Marks – Part A Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.1 – Cost of the Project)*  
**Question:** What are "Pre-Operative Expenses" and "Margin Money for Working Capital" in estimating the Cost of a Project?

**Answer:**  
* **Pre-Operative Expenses:** Expenditures incurred during the project construction phase *before* commercial production begins—such as salaries of project staff, office rent, trial run expenses, and Interest During Construction (IDC).
* **Margin Money for Working Capital:** The long-term portion of initial working capital (typically 25% of total working capital needs) included in the capital cost of the project to ensure smooth day-to-day operations at launch.
