## 1. Overview of Financial Evaluation Methods (Capital Budgeting)

Before investing capital into a project, management evaluates its financial viability using **Capital Budgeting (Investment Appraisal) Techniques**, which fall into two main categories:

1. **Non-Discounting (Traditional) Methods** — *Ignore Time Value of Money:*
   * **Payback Period (PBP)** *(Topic 5.2)*
   * **Accounting Rate of Return (ARR)** *(Topic 5.3)*
2. **Discounting (Modern / Time-Adjusted) Methods** — *Consider Time Value of Money:*
   * **Net Present Value (NPV)** *(Topic 5.4)*
   * **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** *(Topic 5.5)*
   * **Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR) / Profitability Index (PI)** *(Topic 5.6)*
   * **Discounted Payback Period** *(modified Payback)*

*(Key Financial Rule: **Cash Flow After Tax [CFAT]** = $\text{Profit After Tax [PAT]} + \text{Depreciation}$, because depreciation is a non-cash accounting expense. Payback, NPV, IRR, and BCR all use **Cash Flows**, whereas ARR uses Accounting Profit.)*

## 2. Concept and Definition of Payback Period

### What is the Payback Period?
The **Payback Period (PBP)** is the **length of time (in years) required to recover the original initial capital investment** of a project from its net cash inflows (Cash Flows After Tax).

It answers a simple, risk-focused question: *"How quickly do we get our money back?"*

### Formulas for Calculating Payback Period

#### Case A: Uniform (Equal) Annual Cash Inflows
When a project generates the **same** net annual cash inflow ($CF$) every year:
$$\text{Payback Period} = \frac{\text{Initial Investment } (I_0)}{\text{Annual Net Cash Inflow } (CF)}$$

#### Case B: Uneven (Varying) Annual Cash Inflows
When annual net cash inflows vary from year to year, compute **Cumulative Cash Inflows** year by year until the initial investment is recovered:
$$\text{Payback Period} = E + \frac{B}{C}$$
Where:
* **$E$** = Number of completed years immediately **before** full recovery.
* **$B$** = **Unrecovered balance** remaining at the end of year $E$ ($\text{Initial Investment} - \text{Cumulative Cash Flow at year } E$).
* **$C$** = **Cash inflow during year $(E + 1)$** (the recovery year).

## 3. Decision Rules (Accept / Reject & Ranking)

1. **Single Project (Independent Decision):**
   * **Accept** the project if $\text{Payback Period} \le \text{Target / Maximum Acceptable Payback Period}$ set by management.
   * **Reject** the project if $\text{Payback Period} > \text{Target Payback Period}$.
2. **Mutually Exclusive Projects (Choosing Best Alternative):**
   * **Select the project with the SHORTEST Payback Period** (faster capital recovery = lower risk and higher liquidity).

---

## 4. Discounted Payback Period (Improved Variant)

One major flaw of the simple Payback Period is that it ignores the **Time Value of Money** (treating ₹1 received in Year 4 as equal to ₹1 today).  
The **Discounted Payback Period** fixes this by first **discounting each future cash flow to its Present Value** using the cost of capital ($r$):
$$PV_t = \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r)^t}$$
Then, cumulative **discounted cash flows** are summed until they equal the initial investment $I_0$.  
* *Note:* Discounted Payback Period is always **longer** than Simple Payback Period (for $r > 0$). However, it still ignores cash flows occurring *after* the payback cutoff.

---

## 5. Advantages and Disadvantages of Payback Period Method

### Advantages
1. **Simple to Understand and Compute:** Requires minimal financial math and is easily understood by engineers and non-finance managers.
2. **Emphasizes Liquidity:** Crucial for startups or cash-strapped firms needing fast cash recovery to reinvest or repay short-term debt.
3. **Crude Measure of Risk:** Shorter payback reduces exposure to long-term technological obsolescence, market shifts, and political instability (especially useful in R&D/IT projects).

### Disadvantages
1. **Ignores Time Value of Money (in Simple PBP):** Adds cash flows from Year 1, Year 2, and Year 5 equally without discounting.
2. **Ignores Cash Flows After Payback Period:** Completely disregards high profits generated *after* the recovery year (penalizing long-term infrastructure or R&D projects with slow starts and huge later returns).
3. **Measures Capital Recovery, Not Profitability:** Two projects with a 3-year payback could have vastly different total lifespans and total profits.
4. **Subjective Cut-off Period:** Choosing a "3-year" or "4-year" maximum acceptable payback is arbitrary and not linked to market cost of capital.

## 6. Worked Numerical Examples

### Example 1: Uniform Cash Flows
A project requires an initial investment of **Rs. 5,00,000** and generates an equal net annual cash inflow of **Rs. 1,25,000** for 6 years. Find the Payback Period. Should it be accepted if management's maximum payback threshold is 4.5 years?

**Solution:**
$$\text{Payback Period} = \frac{5,00,000}{1,25,000} = \mathbf{4\text{ years}}$$
Since $4\text{ years} \le 4.5\text{ years}$, the project should be **Accepted**.

---

### Example 2: Uneven Cash Flows (Simple & Discounted Payback)
A project has an initial investment of **Rs. 1,00,000** and yields the following net cash inflows after tax:
* Year 1: Rs. 30,000
* Year 2: Rs. 40,000
* Year 3: Rs. 50,000
* Year 4: Rs. 20,000

Calculate: (a) **Simple Payback Period**, and (b) **Discounted Payback Period** at a discount rate of **10%**.

**Solution:**

**a) Simple Payback Period:**

| Year | Cash Inflow ($CF_t$) | Cumulative Cash Inflow |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 30,000 | 30,000 |
| 2 | 40,000 | **70,000** |
| 3 | **50,000** | 1,20,000 |
| 4 | 20,000 | 1,40,000 |

* Full recovery happens during **Year 3** ($E = 2$ completed years).
* Unrecovered balance after Year 2: $B = 1,00,000 - 70,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 30,000$.
* Cash flow in Year 3: $C = \text{Rs.\; } 50,000$.
$$\text{Simple Payback Period} = 2 + \frac{30,000}{50,000} = 2 + 0.6 = \mathbf{2.6\text{ years}}\text{ (2 years, 7.2 months)}$$

**b) Discounted Payback Period (at $r = 10\\%$):**

| Year ($t$) | Cash Flow ($CF_t$) | PV Factor $\frac{1}{(1.10)^t}$ | Present Value ($PV_t$) | Cumulative $PV$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 30,000 | 0.9091 | 27,273 | 27,273 |
| 2 | 40,000 | 0.8264 | 33,056 | 60,329 |
| 3 | 50,000 | 0.7513 | 37,565 | **97,894** |
| 4 | 20,000 | 0.6830 | **13,660** | 1,11,554 |

* Full recovery occurs during **Year 4** ($E = 3$).
* Unrecovered balance after Year 3: $B = 1,00,000 - 97,894 = \text{Rs.\; } 2,106$.
* Discounted cash inflow in Year 4: $C = \text{Rs.\; } 13,660$.
$$\text{Discounted Payback Period} = 3 + \frac{2,106}{13,660} = 3 + 0.154 = \mathbf{3.15\text{ years}}$$

#### Model Question 3 (5 Marks – Part B Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.2 – Comparative Numerical Problem)*  
**Question:** A company must choose between Machine A and Machine B, each costing **Rs. 4,00,000**. Their net annual cash inflows are:

| Year | Machine A (Rs.) | Machine B (Rs.) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 1,50,000 | 80,000 |
| 2 | 1,50,000 | 1,20,000 |
| 3 | 1,00,000 | 1,50,000 |
| 4 | 50,000 | 2,50,000 |
| 5 | 20,000 | 3,00,000 |

Calculate the Payback Period for both machines and comment on the limitation revealed by this comparison.

**Answer:**

**1. Machine A Cumulative Cash Flows:**
* Year 1: 1,50,000 | Year 2: 3,00,000 | **Year 3: 4,00,000**
$$\text{Payback Period (Machine A)} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ years}}$$

**2. Machine B Cumulative Cash Flows:**
* Year 1: 80,000 | Year 2: 2,00,000 | Year 3: **3,50,000** | Year 4: 6,00,000
* Unrecovered after Year 3 $= 4,00,000 - 3,50,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 50,000$; Year 4 inflow $= \text{Rs.\; } 2,50,000$.
$$\text{Payback Period (Machine B)} = 3 + \frac{50,000}{2,50,000} = \mathbf{3.2\text{ years}}$$

**3. Decision & Limitation Revealed:**
* By the **Payback Period rule**, **Machine A** (3.0 years) is preferred over Machine B (3.2 years).
* **Limitation Exposed:** Machine A earns only **Rs. 4,70,000** total over 5 years (profit Rs. 70,000), whereas Machine B earns **Rs. 9,00,000** total (profit Rs. 5,00,000)! Because Payback ignores cash flows *after* recovery, it wrongly rejects the far more profitable Machine B.

#### Model Question 4 (8 Marks – Part B University Exam Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.2 – Comprehensive Theory + Discounted Payback Numerical)*  
**Question:** Explain the Payback Period method and Discounted Payback Period method of project evaluation, highlighting their merits and demerits. Calculate both Simple Payback Period and Discounted Payback Period (at 10% discount rate) for a project with an initial outlay of **Rs. 2,00,000** and cash inflows of **Rs. 60,000, Rs. 80,000, Rs. 70,000, Rs. 50,000, and Rs. 40,000** over Years 1 to 5.

**Answer:**

**1. Concept, Merits & Demerits (3.5 Marks):**
* **Simple Payback Period:** Measures the number of years needed to recover the initial investment from nominal net cash inflows ($\text{Uniform: } I_0/CF$; $\text{Uneven: } E + B/C$).
* **Discounted Payback Period:** Improves upon simple payback by discounting each cash flow at the cost of capital ($PV_t = CF_t/(1+r)^t$) before summing cumulative recovery, thereby accounting for the **Time Value of Money**.
* **Merits:** Easy to compute/communicate, focuses on liquidity, and acts as a quick risk screen against long-term uncertainty.
* **Demerits:** Simple PBP ignores the Time Value of Money; **both** Simple and Discounted PBP ignore cash inflows occurring *after* the payback period and fail to measure total project profitability.

**2. Numerical Calculation (4.5 Marks):**

| Year ($t$) | $CF_t$ (Rs.) | Cum. $CF$ (Rs.) | $PVF_{10\\%} = \frac{1}{(1.10)^t}$ | Discounted $CF$ ($PV_t$) | Cum. $PV$ (Rs.) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 60,000 | 60,000 | 0.9091 | 54,546 | 54,546 |
| 2 | 80,000 | **1,40,000** | 0.8264 | 66,112 | 1,20,658 |
| 3 | **70,000** | 2,10,000 | 0.7513 | 52,591 | **1,73,249** |
| 4 | 50,000 | 2,60,000 | 0.6830 | **34,150** | 2,07,399 |
| 5 | 40,000 | 3,00,000 | 0.6209 | 24,836 | 2,32,235 |

* **Simple Payback Period:**
  * Recovery occurs in Year 3 ($E = 2$); unrecovered balance $= 2,00,000 - 1,40,000 = 60,000$.
  $$\text{Simple Payback} = 2 + \frac{60,000}{70,000} = 2 + 0.857 = \mathbf{2.86\text{ years}}$$

* **Discounted Payback Period (at 10%):**
  * Recovery occurs in Year 4 ($E = 3$); unrecovered PV balance $= 2,00,000 - 1,73,249 = 26,751$.
  $$\text{Discounted Payback} = 3 + \frac{26,751}{34,150} = 3 + 0.783 = \mathbf{3.78\text{ years}}$$
