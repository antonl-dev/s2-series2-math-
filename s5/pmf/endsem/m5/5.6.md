## 1. Concept and Definition of Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR)

The **Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR)**—also referred to as the **Profitability Index (PI)** in corporate capital budgeting—is a **discounted cash flow (DCF)** appraisal method that measures the **Present Value of future benefits (cash inflows) per rupee of cost (initial investment)** at a specified discount rate.

* **Core Meaning:**  
  BCR indicates the **"bang for the buck"**—how much present value return a project generates for every ₹1 invested. It is especially vital for **public infrastructure projects** (highways, dams, power grids evaluated via Social Cost–Benefit Analysis) and **capital rationing** (allocating a limited budget across competing projects).

## 2. Mathematical Formulas

### 1. Standard Commercial BCR / Profitability Index (PI)
$$\text{BCR} = \frac{\text{Present Value of Future Cash Inflows (Benefits)}}{\text{Initial Capital Investment } (I_0)} = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r)^t}}{I_0}$$

### 2. Net Benefit–Cost Ratio (NBCR)
Measures the **net** present value earned per rupee of initial outlay:
$$\text{NBCR} = \frac{\text{NPV}}{I_0} = \frac{\text{PV of Benefits} - I_0}{I_0} = \text{BCR} - 1$$

### 3. Public-Sector / Engineering Economics B/C Ratio
When annual operating & maintenance (O&M) costs are listed separately from user/social benefits:
$$\text{BCR} = \frac{\text{Present Worth of Benefits}}{\text{Initial Capital Cost } + \text{ Present Worth of O\&M Costs}}$$

## 3. Decision Rules and Relationship with NPV

| BCR Value | NBCR Value | Equivalent NPV | Decision (Independent Project) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **$\text{BCR} > 1.0$** | $\text{NBCR} > 0$ | $\text{NPV} > 0$ | **ACCEPT** *(Benefits exceed costs)* |
| **$\text{BCR} = 1.0$** | $\text{NBCR} = 0$ | $\text{NPV} = 0$ | **INDIFFERENT** *(Break-even in PV terms)* |
| **$\text{BCR} < 1.0$** | $\text{NBCR} < 0$ | $\text{NPV} < 0$ | **REJECT** *(Costs exceed benefits)* |

### Special Decision Rule Under Capital Rationing
When an organization has a **fixed capital budget ceiling** and multiple independent projects with positive NPVs, ranking projects in **descending order of BCR** (highest BCR first) maximizes total wealth (total NPV) generated from the limited budget.

## 4. Advantages and Disadvantages of BCR

### Advantages
1. **Recognizes Time Value of Money:** Discounts all future benefits and costs using the required cost of capital ($r$).
2. **Ideal for Capital Rationing:** Allows fair comparison of projects requiring **unequal initial investments** by measuring return *per rupee invested* (unlike NPV, which favors large projects blindly).
3. **Widely Accepted in Public & Infrastructure Appraisal:** Naturally compares non-commercial social/economic benefits (travel time saved, health gains, clean energy) against government capital and maintenance outlays.
4. **Consistent with NPV for Independent Projects:** Always gives the exact same Accept/Reject decision as NPV ($\text{BCR} > 1 \iff \text{NPV} > 0$).

### Disadvantages
1. **Can Misrank Mutually Exclusive Projects (Scale Problem):** A small ₹1 lakh project with $\text{BCR} = 2.0$ ($\text{NPV} = \text{Rs.\; } 1\text{ lakh}$) has a higher BCR than a ₹50 lakh project with $\text{BCR} = 1.5$ ($\text{NPV} = \text{Rs.\; } 25\text{ lakh}$). For mutually exclusive choices, **NPV** (or **Incremental BCR**) must be used instead.
2. **Sensitive to Cost vs. Negative Benefit Classification:** Subtracting O&M costs from the numerator vs. adding them to the denominator changes the numerical BCR value (though it never changes whether BCR is $> 1$ or $< 1$).
3. **Requires Accurate Discount Rate ($r$):** Results depend heavily on selecting the appropriate cost of capital or social discount rate.

## 5. Worked Numerical Examples

### Example 1: Standard BCR and NBCR Calculation
A project requires an initial outlay of **Rs. 3,00,000** and generates cash inflows of **Rs. 1,20,000 (Year 1)**, **Rs. 1,50,000 (Year 2)**, and **Rs. 1,40,000 (Year 3)**. At a **10% discount rate**, calculate **BCR** and **NBCR**.

**Solution:**

| Year ($t$) | Cash Inflow ($CF_t$) | $PVIF_{10\%,t}$ | Present Value ($PV_t$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 1,20,000 | 0.9091 | 1,09,092 |
| 2 | 1,50,000 | 0.8264 | 1,23,960 |
| 3 | 1,40,000 | 0.7513 | 1,05,182 |
| **Total PV of Benefits** | | | **Rs. 3,38,234** |

* $\text{NPV} = 3,38,234 - 3,00,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 38,234$
* $\text{BCR} = \dfrac{3,38,234}{3,00,000} = \mathbf{1.13}$
* $\text{NBCR} = \text{BCR} - 1 = 1.13 - 1 = \mathbf{0.13}$ *(or $\frac{38,234}{3,00,000} = 0.127$)*
* **Decision:** Since $\text{BCR } (1.13) > 1.0$, **ACCEPT** the project.

---

### Example 2: Capital Rationing Using BCR
A company has a **strict capital budget limit of Rs. 10,00,000** and four independent projects:

| Project | Initial Cost ($I_0$) | PV of Benefits | NPV ($PV - I_0$) | BCR ($PV / I_0$) | Rank by BCR |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **A** | 10,00,000 | 12,00,000 | **2,00,000** | $1.20$ | 3 |
| **B** | 5,00,000 | 6,75,000 | 1,75,000 | **$1.35$** | **1** |
| **C** | 5,00,000 | 6,50,000 | 1,50,000 | **$1.30$** | **2** |
| **D** | 4,00,000 | 4,60,000 | 60,000 | $1.15$ | 4 |

* If selected solely by highest individual **NPV**, Project **A** consumes the full Rs. 10,00,000 and yields **NPV = Rs. 2,00,000**.
* Ranked by **BCR**, selecting **Project B (Rs. 5L) + Project C (Rs. 5L)** stays within the Rs. 10,00,000 budget and generates **Total NPV = $1,75,000 + 1,50,000 = \mathbf{\text{Rs.\; } 3,25,000}$**—creating Rs. 1,25,000 more wealth!

**Question:** Define Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR).

**Answer:**  
The **Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR)** (also called the Profitability Index) is a discounted financial evaluation metric defined as the ratio of the **Present Value of future project benefits (cash inflows)** to the **Present Value of project costs (initial investment)**:
$$\text{BCR} = \frac{\text{Present Value of Benefits}}{\text{Initial Investment (or PV of Costs)}}$$
A project is financially viable and **accepted if $\text{BCR} > 1.0$**, and rejected if $\text{BCR} < 1.0$.

#### Model Question 2 (5 Marks – Part B Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.6 – Theory, Merits & Demerits)*  
**Question:** Explain the Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR) method of project evaluation. Discuss its advantages, limitations, and suitability in public-sector and capital-rationing decisions.

**Answer:**  
The **Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR)** evaluates a project by dividing the Present Value of future cash inflows (or socio-economic benefits) by the Present Value of capital and operating costs at a given discount rate ($\text{Accept if } \text{BCR} > 1$).

* **Advantages:**
  1. Incorporates the **Time Value of Money** across all project years.
  2. Measures efficiency per rupee invested, making it the **best tool under Capital Rationing** when comparing projects of unequal investment sizes.
  3. Ideal for **public infrastructure projects** (highways, irrigation, power grids) where economic/social benefits are weighed against capital and O&M costs.
* **Limitations:**
  1. **Scale Bias on Mutually Exclusive Projects:** A tiny project with a high BCR can generate far less total wealth (NPV) than a larger project with a slightly lower BCR; therefore, **NPV or Incremental BCR** must be used for mutually exclusive choices.
  2. **Classification Sensitivity:** Moving O&M expenses between numerator and denominator changes the exact ratio value.

#### Model Question 3 (8 Marks – Part B University Exam Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.6 – Public Project B/C Numerical + Incremental Evaluation)*  
**Question:** Explain how the Benefit–Cost Ratio (BCR) method is applied to evaluate public engineering projects. Two mutually exclusive highway-lighting alternatives are proposed with a **10-year life** and a **10% discount rate** ($PVIFA_{10\%, 10} = 6.1446$):
* **Design A (Standard LED):** Initial Cost = Rs. 10,00,000; Annual O&M Cost = Rs. 1,00,000; Annual User Safety/Energy Benefit = Rs. 3,00,000.
* **Design B (Smart Solar-LED):** Initial Cost = Rs. 16,00,000; Annual O&M Cost = Rs. 60,000; Annual User Safety/Energy Benefit = Rs. 4,00,000.

Evaluate both designs using BCR and recommend the better alternative using Incremental BCR analysis.

**Answer:**

**1. Application of BCR to Public Engineering Projects (2 Marks):**  
Public projects aim to maximize social welfare rather than corporate profit. BCR compares the **Present Worth of Public Benefits** (energy savings, accident reduction, time saved) against the **Present Worth of Initial Capital Cost plus Annual O&M Costs**. Any alternative with $\text{BCR} > 1$ is viable; however, for **mutually exclusive alternatives** of unequal cost, **Incremental BCR ($\Delta B / \Delta C$)** must be checked to ensure the extra capital invested is justified.

**2. BCR Calculation for Design A & Design B (4 Marks):**  
Given $PVIFA_{10\%,10} = 6.1446$:

* **Design A:**
  * $\text{PV of Benefits}_A = 3,00,000 \times 6.1446 = \text{Rs.\; } 18,43,380$
  * $\text{PV of Total Costs}_A = 10,00,000 + (1,00,000 \times 6.1446) = 10,00,000 + 6,14,460 = \text{Rs.\; } 16,14,460$
  * $\text{BCR}_A = \dfrac{18,43,380}{16,14,460} = \mathbf{1.142} > 1$ *(Acceptable)*

* **Design B:**
  * $\text{PV of Benefits}_B = 4,00,000 \times 6.1446 = \text{Rs.\; } 24,57,840$
  * $\text{PV of Total Costs}_B = 16,00,000 + (60,000 \times 6.1446) = 16,00,000 + 3,68,676 = \text{Rs.\; } 19,68,676$
  * $\text{BCR}_B = \dfrac{24,57,840}{19,68,676} = \mathbf{1.248} > 1$ *(Acceptable)*

**3. Incremental BCR Analysis & Recommendation (2 Marks):**
* $\Delta \text{PV of Benefits } (B - A) = 24,57,840 - 18,43,380 = \text{Rs.\; } 6,14,460$
* $\Delta \text{PV of Costs } (B - A) = 19,68,676 - 16,14,460 = \text{Rs.\; } 3,54,216$
* $\text{Incremental BCR } (\Delta B / \Delta C) = \dfrac{6,14,460}{3,54,216} = \mathbf{1.735} > 1.0$
* *(Check via Net Benefit / NPV: $\text{NPV}_A = 2,28,920$; $\text{NPV}_B = 4,89,164$.)*

**Recommendation:** Both individually and incrementally ($\Delta B/\Delta C = 1.735 > 1$), **Design B (Smart Solar-LED)** is superior and should be selected.
