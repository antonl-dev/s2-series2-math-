## 1. Concept and Definition of Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

### What is IRR?
The **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** is the **discount rate** that makes the **Net Present Value (NPV) of a project equal to zero**—that is, the exact interest rate at which the **Present Value of Cash Inflows equals the Present Value of Cash Outflows (Initial Investment)**.

It represents the project's **true effective annual percentage yield** (or break-even cost of capital).

### Mathematical Equation
Find the rate $r = \text{IRR}$ such that:
$$\sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + \text{IRR})^t} - I_0 = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + \text{IRR})^t} = I_0$$

## 2. How to Calculate IRR

### Case A: Uniform Annual Cash Inflows (Annuity Method)
1. Compute the Present Value Annuity Factor:  
   $$PVIFA_{\text{IRR}, n} = \frac{I_0}{\text{Annual Cash Flow } (CF)}$$
2. Look up this value along the $n$-year row of the **$PVIFA$ table** to find two bounding interest rates $r_L$ and $r_H$, then interpolate.

### Case B: Uneven Annual Cash Inflows (Trial-and-Error + Linear Interpolation)
1. **Trial 1 (Choose a lower discount rate $r_L$):** Compute $\text{NPV}_L$ such that $\text{NPV}_L > 0$.
2. **Trial 2 (Choose a higher discount rate $r_H$):** Compute $\text{NPV}_H$ such that $\text{NPV}_H < 0$. *(If $\text{NPV} > 0$, increase the discount rate; if $\text{NPV} < 0$, decrease the discount rate.)*
3. **Apply Linear Interpolation:**
   $$\text{IRR} = r_L + \left(\frac{\text{NPV}_L}{\text{NPV}_L - \text{NPV}_H}\right) \times (r_H - r_L)$$
   *(Note: Since $\text{NPV}_H$ is negative, $\text{NPV}_L - \text{NPV}_H = \text{NPV}_L + |\text{NPV}_H|$.)*

## 3. Decision Rules for IRR

Compare the calculated **IRR** against the firm's **Required Rate of Return / Cost of Capital ($k$ or Hurdle Rate)**:

| Condition | Financial Meaning | Decision (Independent Project) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **$\text{IRR} > k$** | Project earns **more** than the cost of financing; $\text{NPV} > 0$. | **ACCEPT** |
| **$\text{IRR} = k$** | Project earns **exactly** the cost of capital; $\text{NPV} = 0$. | **INDIFFERENT** |
| **$\text{IRR} < k$** | Project earns **less** than the cost of capital; $\text{NPV} < 0$. | **REJECT** |

## 4. Advantages and Disadvantages of IRR

### Advantages
1. **Considers Time Value of Money & Full Lifespan:** Discounts every cash flow over the project's entire life.
2. **Intuitive Percentage Metric:** Managers easily understand a percentage return (e.g., "18% return vs. 11% bank loan") without needing to pre-determine an exact discount rate before calculation.
3. **Consistent with NPV for Independent Projects:** For normal conventional projects, $\text{IRR} > k$ always agrees with $\text{NPV} > 0$.

### Disadvantages
1. **Multiple IRRs (Non-Conventional Cash Flows):** If cash flows change signs more than once (e.g., negative initial outlay → positive inflows → large negative decommissioning/environmental cleanup cost at the end), the polynomial equation can produce **two or more IRRs**, making decisions ambiguous.
2. **Unrealistic Reinvestment Rate Assumption:** Mathematically assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the **IRR itself** (which may be unrealistically high, e.g., 35%), whereas NPV assumes reinvestment at the realistic **Cost of Capital ($k$)**.
3. **Scale & Timing Conflicts on Mutually Exclusive Projects:** A tiny project with 40% IRR (₹10,000 outlay) may look better than a large project with 22% IRR (₹1,00,00,000 outlay), even though the larger project creates vastly more wealth (higher NPV).
4. **Tedious Manual Calculation:** Requires trial-and-error and interpolation (unless using Excel `=IRR()` or financial calculators).

## 5. NPV vs. IRR: Comparison & Conflict Analysis

### When Do NPV and IRR Agree vs. Disagree?
* **Independent Projects (Conventional Cash Flows):** NPV and IRR **always agree** ($\text{NPV} > 0 \iff \text{IRR} > k$).
* **Mutually Exclusive Projects:** NPV and IRR **can give conflicting rankings** due to:
  1. **Scale Disparity:** Unequal initial capital outlays.
  2. **Timing Disparity:** One project pays early cash flows (high IRR), while the other pays larger cash flows later (high NPV at low $k$).
  3. **Unequal Lifespans.**
* **Fisher's Intersection (Crossover Rate):** The discount rate at which the NPV profiles of two mutually exclusive projects cross ($\text{NPV}_A = \text{NPV}_B$). Below the crossover rate, NPV and IRR rank the two projects in **opposite order**; **NPV is theoretically superior** because it directly maximizes total shareholder wealth.

## 6. Worked Numerical Example (IRR by Interpolation)

**Problem:** A project requires an initial investment of **Rs. 1,00,000** and yields net cash inflows of **Rs. 40,000 (Year 1)**, **Rs. 50,000 (Year 2)**, and **Rs. 40,000 (Year 3)**. Compute the **IRR**. If the cost of capital is **12%**, should the project be accepted?

**Solution:**
* **Trial 1 at $r_L = 12\%$:**
  * Year 1: $40,000 \times 0.8929 = 35,716$
  * Year 2: $50,000 \times 0.7972 = 39,860$
  * Year 3: $40,000 \times 0.7118 = 28,472$
  * Total PV $= 35,716 + 39,860 + 28,472 = \text{Rs.\; } 1,04,048$
  * $\text{NPV}_{12\%} = 1,04,048 - 1,00,000 = \mathbf{+\text{Rs.\; } 4,048}$ *(Positive → try a higher rate!)*

* **Trial 2 at $r_H = 15\%$:**
  * Year 1: $40,000 \times 0.8696 = 34,784$
  * Year 2: $50,000 \times 0.7561 = 37,805$
  * Year 3: $40,000 \times 0.6575 = 26,300$
  * Total PV $= 34,784 + 37,805 + 26,300 = \text{Rs.\; } 98,889$
  * $\text{NPV}_{15\%} = 98,889 - 1,00,000 = \mathbf{-\text{Rs.\; } 1,111}$ *(Negative → IRR lies between 12% and 15%!)*

* **Step 3 — Linear Interpolation:**
  $$\text{IRR} = 12\% + \left(\frac{4,048}{4,048 - (-1,111)}\right) \times (15\% - 12\%) = 12\% + \left(\frac{4,048}{5,159}\right) \times 3\% = 12\% + 2.35\% = \mathbf{14.35\%}$$

* **Decision:** Since $\text{IRR } (14.35\%) > \text{Cost of Capital } (12\%)$, **ACCEPT** the project.

### A. Previous Year / Exam Question (From Attached Question Papers)

#### Question 1 (8 Marks — End-Semester Full Question)
**Source:** *Fifth Semester B. Tech. Degree Examination, Regular, November 2025 (23HUT535, QP Code: 5010BHU25T535), Part B – Module 5, Q20*  
**Question:** Compare NPV and IRR methods of project evaluation.

**Answer:**

**1. Brief Overview of NPV and IRR (1.5 Marks):**  
Both **Net Present Value (NPV)** and **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** are discounted cash flow (DCF) methods that recognize the **Time Value of Money** and evaluate cash flows across the entire project lifespan:
* **NPV** calculates the absolute net monetary wealth added by discounting future cash flows at the firm's cost of capital ($r$): $\text{NPV} = \sum \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} - I_0$.
* **IRR** finds the internal percentage discount rate at which $\text{NPV} = 0$.

**2. Detailed Comparison Table (4.5 Marks):**

| Basis of Comparison | Net Present Value (NPV) | Internal Rate of Return (IRR) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Nature of Output** | Expressed in **absolute monetary terms** (Rs. / $). | Expressed as a **relative percentage rate** (% p.a.). |
| **2. Discount Rate Dependency** | **Requires** Cost of Capital ($r$) as an input beforehand to compute NPV. | **Does not require** $r$ to compute IRR ($r$ is only used afterward as a hurdle rate). |
| **3. Acceptance Rule** | Accept if $\text{NPV} > 0$. | Accept if $\text{IRR} > \text{Cost of Capital } (k)$. |
| **4. Reinvestment Assumption** | Assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the **Cost of Capital ($r$)** *(more realistic)*. | Assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the **IRR itself** *(often unrealistic)*. |
| **5. Non-Conventional Cash Flows** | Always produces a **single unique value** regardless of sign changes. | May produce **multiple IRRs** (or no real IRR) if cash flows change signs more than once. |
| **6. Mutually Exclusive Ranking** | Always selects the project maximizing **total rupee wealth** (satisfies Value Additivity). | Can misrank projects with **unequal investment scales** or **different cash flow timings**. |
| **7. Computational Ease** | Straightforward direct calculation once $r$ is known. | Requires **trial-and-error and interpolation** manually. |

**3. Agreement, Conflict & Conclusion (2 Marks):**  
* **Agreement:** For standalone independent projects with conventional cash flows, NPV and IRR **always yield the same Accept/Reject decision** ($\text{NPV} > 0 \iff \text{IRR} > k$).
* **Conflict & Superiority:** For mutually exclusive projects, NPV and IRR can conflict when investment scales or cash-flow timings differ (below the Fisher Crossover Rate). Whenever a conflict arises, **NPV is theoretically superior** because it assumes realistic reinvestment at the cost of capital and directly maximizes shareholder wealth.

**Question:** Define Internal Rate of Return (IRR) and state its acceptance criterion.

**Answer:**

#### Model Question 2 (5 Marks – Part B Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.5 – Uniform Annuity IRR Numerical)*  
**Question:** A solar rooftop project requires an initial investment of **Rs. 4,00,000** and generates a uniform net annual cash inflow of **Rs. 1,10,000** for **5 years**. Calculate the project's IRR and advise whether it should be accepted if the cost of capital is **10%**.  
*(Given $PVIFA_{11\%,5} = 3.6959$, $PVIFA_{12\%,5} = 3.6048$)*

**Answer:**
1. **Compute Annuity Factor at IRR:**
   $$PVIFA_{\text{IRR},5} = \frac{\text{Initial Investment}}{\text{Annual Cash Flow}} = \frac{4,00,000}{1,10,000} = \mathbf{3.6364}$$
2. **Locate Bounding Rates:**  
   $3.6364$ lies between $11\%$ ($3.6959$) and $12\%$ ($3.6048$).
3. **Interpolate IRR:**
   $$\text{IRR} = 11\% + \left(\frac{3.6959 - 3.6364}{3.6959 - 3.6048}\right) \times (12\% - 11\%) = 11\% + \frac{0.0595}{0.0911} \times 1\% = \mathbf{11.65\%}$$
4. **Decision:** Since $\text{IRR } (11.65\%) > \text{Cost of Capital } (10\%)$, **ACCEPT** the project.
