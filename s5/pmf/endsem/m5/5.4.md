## 1. Foundation: Time Value of Money (TVM)

Money has a **time value**: **₹1 received today is worth more than ₹1 received a year from now** because today's rupee can be invested immediately to earn interest, is not eroded by inflation, and carries zero future collection risk.

* **Compounding (Finding Future Value):**  
  $$FV = PV \times (1 + r)^t$$
* **Discounting (Finding Present Value):**  
  $$PV = \frac{FV}{(1 + r)^t} = FV \times PVIF_{r,t}$$
  Where $r$ (or $k$) = **Discount Rate / Cost of Capital**, and $PVIF_{r,t} = \frac{1}{(1+r)^t}$ is the **Present Value Interest Factor**.

---

## 2. Concept and Formula of Net Present Value (NPV)

### Definition
The **Net Present Value (NPV)** method is a **discounted cash flow (DCF)** capital budgeting technique that measures the **net monetary wealth** added by a project in today's terms. It is defined as the **Present Value of all future Cash Inflows minus the Present Value of all Cash Outflows** (initial investment), discounted at the firm's required rate of return (Cost of Capital, $r$).

### Mathematical Formulas

1. **Standard Formula (Single Initial Outlay $I_0$ at $t = 0$):**
   $$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r)^t} - I_0$$
   Where:
   * $CF_t$ = Net Cash Inflow After Tax (CFAT) at the end of year $t$ (in Year $n$, include any **Salvage Value** and **Working Capital Recovery**).
   * $r$ = Cost of Capital / Discount Rate (in decimals, e.g., $10\% = 0.10$).
   * $n$ = Economic life of the project (in years).
   * $I_0$ = Initial Cash Outflow at $t = 0$.

2. **General Formula (Multi-Year Outflows):**
   $$\text{NPV} = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{\text{Cash Inflow}_t - \text{Cash Outflow}_t}{(1 + r)^t}$$

3. **Uniform Annual Inflows (Annuity Shortcut):**
   If $CF$ is constant every year for $n$ years:
   $$\text{NPV} = \left(CF \times PVIFA_{r,n}\right) - I_0, \quad \text{where } PVIFA_{r,n} = \frac{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}{r}$$

## 3. Decision Rules for NPV

| NPV Value | Financial Meaning | Decision (Independent Project) |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **$\text{NPV} > 0$** | Project earns **more** than the cost of capital ($r$); increases firm/shareholder wealth. | **ACCEPT** |
| **$\text{NPV} = 0$** | Project earns **exactly** the cost of capital ($r$); breaks even in present value terms. | **INDIFFERENT** *(Accept if strategic benefits exist)* |
| **$\text{NPV} < 0$** | Project earns **less** than the cost of capital ($r$); destroys wealth. | **REJECT** |

* **Mutually Exclusive Projects:** Rank projects by NPV and choose the project with the **highest positive NPV**.
* **NPV Profile:** Plotting NPV (Y-axis) against Discount Rate $r$ (X-axis) yields a downward-sloping curve for normal projects; the point where $\text{NPV} = 0$ is the **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)**.

## 4. Advantages and Disadvantages of NPV Method

### Advantages
1. **Recognizes Time Value of Money:** Properly discounts distant cash flows relative to immediate cash flows.
2. **Considers All Cash Flows:** Accounts for every cash inflow/outflow throughout the project's full life, including salvage value.
3. **Value Additivity & Shareholder Wealth Maximization:** Directly measures the exact rupee/dollar wealth created ($\text{NPV}(A+B) = \text{NPV}(A) + \text{NPV}(B)$), aligning strictly with corporate financial goals.
4. **Realistic Reinvestment Assumption:** Assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the realistic **Cost of Capital ($r$)** (unlike IRR, which assumes reinvestment at IRR itself).
5. **Handles Non-Conventional Cash Flows:** Works cleanly even when cash flows change signs multiple times (e.g., major mid-life overhaul or decommissioning cost).

### Disadvantages
1. **Depends on Accurate Discount Rate ($r$):** Calculating the exact Cost of Capital (WACC) is subjective, and NPV is sensitive to changes in $r$.
2. **Absolute Measure (Ignores Project Scale):** Gives an absolute rupee figure rather than a percentage return—making it tricky to rank projects of vastly different initial capital sizes under **capital rationing** (where **BCR/PI** is preferred).
3. **Difficult with Unequal Project Lifespans:** Cannot directly compare a 3-year project against a 6-year mutually exclusive project without adjustments (Equivalent Annual Annuity / Replacement Chain).

## 5. Worked Numerical Examples

### Example 1: Uneven Cash Flows with Salvage Value
A company is evaluating a project costing **Rs. 5,00,000** with a 4-year life and a **Salvage Value of Rs. 50,000** at the end of Year 4. Cost of capital is **10%**. Expected annual Cash Flows After Tax (excluding salvage) are:
* Year 1: Rs. 1,50,000 | Year 2: Rs. 2,00,000 | Year 3: Rs. 2,00,000 | Year 4: Rs. 1,00,000

Compute the NPV and advise whether to accept the project.

**Solution:**
*(Note: In Year 4, Total Cash Inflow = Operating Cash Flow + Salvage Value = $1,00,000 + 50,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 1,50,000$.)*

| Year ($t$) | Net Cash Inflow ($CF_t$) | $PVIF_{10\%,t} = \frac{1}{(1.10)^t}$ | Present Value ($PV_t$) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 1,50,000 | 0.9091 | 1,36,365 |
| 2 | 2,00,000 | 0.8264 | 1,65,280 |
| 3 | 2,00,000 | 0.7513 | 1,50,260 |
| 4 | $1,00,000 + 50,000 = 1,50,000$ | 0.6830 | 1,02,450 |
| **Total PV of Inflows** | | | **Rs. 5,54,355** |

$$\text{NPV} = \text{Total PV of Inflows} - I_0 = 5,54,355 - 5,00,000 = \mathbf{+\text{Rs.\; } 54,355}$$
**Decision:** Since $\text{NPV} > 0$, the project should be **ACCEPTED**.

**Answer:**

#### Model Question 2 (5 Marks – Part B Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.4 – NPV Theory & Merits/Demerits)*  
**Question:** Explain the Net Present Value (NPV) method of project appraisal. Discuss its advantages and limitations.

**Answer:**  
The **Net Present Value (NPV)** method evaluates capital investments by discounting all projected net cash inflows back to the present ($t = 0$) at the firm's required rate of return ($r$) and subtracting the initial capital outlay ($I_0$). A positive NPV indicates that the project generates returns exceeding the cost of capital and directly adds wealth.

**Advantages:**
1. **Time Value of Money:** Accounts for the timing of cash flows by discounting distant cash inflows more heavily.
2. **Full Life Coverage:** Evaluates all cash inflows and outflows over the project's entire life, including salvage value.
3. **Wealth Maximization & Additivity:** Directly measures absolute net wealth added in currency terms and satisfies value additivity.
4. **Realistic Reinvestment Rate:** Assumes intermediate cash inflows are reinvested at the cost of capital ($r$).

**Limitations:**
1. **Difficulty Estimating Discount Rate:** Requires accurate estimation of the firm's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC).
2. **Ignores Project Scale:** An absolute rupee metric can be misleading when comparing projects with unequal initial outlays under capital rationing.
3. **Unequal Lifespan Comparison:** Requires adjustment (e.g., Equivalent Annual Annuity) when comparing mutually exclusive projects of different durations.

#### Model Question 3 (8 Marks – Part B University Exam Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 5.4 – Mutually Exclusive NPV Comparison Numerical)*  
**Question:** Two mutually exclusive projects **X** and **Y** each require an initial outlay of **Rs. 2,00,000** and have a useful life of **4 years**. The company's cost of capital is **12%**.
* **Project X** generates a uniform annual cash inflow of **Rs. 75,000** per year for 4 years.
* **Project Y** generates cash inflows of **Rs. 40,000, Rs. 60,000, Rs. 90,000, and Rs. 1,20,000** in Years 1 to 4 respectively.

*(Given PV factors at 12%: Year 1 = 0.8929, Year 2 = 0.7972, Year 3 = 0.7118, Year 4 = 0.6355)*  
Calculate the NPV of both projects, recommend the better project, and explain why NPV is considered superior to Payback Period and ARR.

**Answer:**

**1. NPV of Project X (2.5 Marks):**  
Since Project X has uniform annual inflows of Rs. 75,000, sum the 4-year PV factors at 12%:
$$PVIFA_{12\%,4} = 0.8929 + 0.7972 + 0.7118 + 0.6355 = 3.0374$$
$$\text{PV of Inflows (X)} = 75,000 \times 3.0374 = \text{Rs.\; } 2,27,805$$
$$\text{NPV}_X = 2,27,805 - 2,00,000 = \mathbf{+\text{Rs.\; } 27,805}$$

**2. NPV of Project Y (3 Marks):**

| Year ($t$) | Cash Inflow ($CF_t$) | $PVIF_{12\%,t}$ | Present Value (Rs.) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | 40,000 | 0.8929 | 35,716 |
| 2 | 60,000 | 0.7972 | 47,832 |
| 3 | 90,000 | 0.7118 | 64,062 |
| 4 | 1,20,000 | 0.6355 | 76,260 |
| **Total PV** | | | **Rs. 2,23,870** |

$$\text{NPV}_Y = 2,23,870 - 2,00,000 = \mathbf{+\text{Rs.\; } 23,870}$$

**3. Recommendation & Superiority of NPV (2.5 Marks):**
* **Recommendation:** Both projects have positive NPVs, so both are financially viable; however, for mutually exclusive selection, **Project X** is recommended because $\text{NPV}_X\; (\text{Rs.\; } 27,805) > \text{NPV}_Y\; (\text{Rs.\; } 23,870)$—even though Project Y has higher total nominal cash inflows (Rs. 3,10,000 vs. Rs. 3,00,000), Project X brings cash earlier!
* **Why NPV is Superior to Payback & ARR:** Unlike Payback Period (which ignores time value of money and post-payback cash flows) and ARR (which uses accounting profits and ignores time value of money), NPV discounts **all cash flows** over the entire project lifespan and directly measures net wealth creation.
