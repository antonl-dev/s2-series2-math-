## 1. Resource Allocation

### Definition
**Resource Allocation** (also called **Resource Loading & Scheduling under Resource Limits**) is the process of assigning available resources (manpower, machinery, tools, materials, and funds) to project activities in the most economical and effective manner, while ensuring that **resource demand never exceeds available supply**.

### Key Objectives of Resource Allocation
1. **Prevent Overallocation:** Ensure no worker, team, or machine is assigned more hours than its maximum daily/weekly capacity.
2. **Optimize Productivity:** Match the right skill/equipment to the right task at the right time.
3. **Prioritize Critical Activities:** Direct scarce resources to critical-path tasks first so overall project delay is minimized.
4. **Control Project Cost:** Avoid emergency hiring, expensive overtime, or idle equipment charges.

### Basic Steps in Resource Allocation
1. **List Resource Requirements:** Identify man, material, and tool requirements for each activity from the WBS.
2. **Generate Resource Loading Profile (Histogram):** Plot daily/weekly demand assuming all tasks start at their Earliest Start ($ES$).
3. **Compare Demand vs. Availability:** Identify time periods where demand exceeds maximum capacity (**overallocation peaks**).
4. **Resolve Conflicts:** Delay lower-priority (non-critical) tasks using heuristic priority rules (like Minimum Total Float) or split/reallocate tasks until demand $\le$ capacity—even if the project end date must extend.

## 2. Resource Leveling

### Definition
**Resource Leveling** is a schedule optimization technique in which the start and finish dates of activities are adjusted based on resource constraints with the goal of **smoothing out extreme peaks and valleys** in resource usage, achieving a relatively uniform and steady demand profile over the project duration.

[Click here to see the diagram for Resource Leveling Histogram Before and After](https://www.google.com/search?q=resource+leveling+histogram+before+and+after+diagram&udm=2)

### Why is Resource Leveling Needed? (Problems with Unlevel Schedules)
If every activity starts at its Earliest Start ($ES$), projects typically show huge demand spikes early on followed by sudden drops:
* **Hiring & Layoff Costs:** Repeatedly hiring temporary staff for short spikes and laying them off later is expensive and hurts morale.
* **Idle Equipment & Mobilization Cost:** Renting extra cranes or test rigs for a 3-day peak wastes capital.
* **Operational Unfeasibility:** A schedule requiring 50 electricians on Tuesday and 5 on Wednesday is impossible to staff smoothly in real life.

### Resource Leveling vs. Resource Smoothing (Important Exam Distinction)

| Feature | Resource Smoothing | Resource Leveling |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Constraint** | **Time-Constrained** (Project finish date **cannot** change). | **Resource-Constrained** (Resource limits **cannot** be exceeded). |
| **How Tasks Shift** | Non-critical activities are shifted **strictly within their available Total Float / Free Float**. | Activities are shifted as needed; **may alter the Critical Path and extend the project end date** if float is insufficient. |
| **Result** | Reduces fluctuations as much as possible without delaying completion. | Guarantees resource caps are respected and creates a flat resource profile. |

*(Note: In general exam terminology, shifting non-critical tasks within their float to reduce peaks is commonly referred to as Resource Leveling; mentioning the Resource Smoothing distinction earns extra credit!)*

## 3. Resource Leveling vs. Resource Allocation (Comparison)

| Parameter | Resource Allocation | Resource Leveling |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Core Purpose** | Assign limited resources to tasks and resolve overallocations. | Minimize sharp fluctuations (peaks & valleys) in resource usage. |
| **2. Focus** | Staying **within maximum resource capacity**. | Achieving a **uniform, steady resource histogram** over time. |
| **3. Impact on End Date** | Frequently extends project duration when resources are scarce. | Aims to keep duration fixed (using float), extending only if unavoidable. |
| **4. When Performed** | During initial schedule creation after CPM/PERT logic. | After initial resource loading exposes spikes and troughs. |
| **5. Typical Method** | Heuristic priority rules (MINSLK, SPT, LFT). | Shifting non-critical tasks within Total Float / Burgess's Sum-of-Squares method. |

## 4. Procedure for Resource Leveling (With Worked Example)

### Standard Procedure (Heuristic / Burgess's Sum-of-Squares Method)
1. **Perform CPM Analysis:** Find $ES, EF, LS, LF$, Total Float ($TF$), and Critical Path.
2. **Plot Initial Resource Histogram:** Schedule all tasks at $ES$ and sum daily resource requirements.
3. **Compute Variance / Sum of Squares ($\sum R_t^2$):** Lower $\sum R_t^2$ indicates a smoother resource profile (for the same total man-days, a perfectly flat line minimizes $\sum R_t^2$).
4. **Shift Non-Critical Tasks:** Shift tasks having positive Total Float ($TF > 0$) forward toward $LS$ so they move out of **peak periods** into **valley (low-demand) periods**.
5. **Re-check Histogram:** Repeat until peak demand is minimized and the histogram is as uniform as possible.

### Worked Numerical Example (Leveling Within Float)
Suppose a 6-day project has the following activities:
* **A (Critical):** $0 \to 3$ (Duration = 3 days, $TF = 0$, 4 workers/day)
* **B (Critical):** $3 \to 6$ (Duration = 3 days, $TF = 0$, 4 workers/day)
* **C (Non-critical):** Duration = 2 days, $ES = 0,\; LF = 6 \Rightarrow TF = 4$ days, 3 workers/day
* **D (Non-critical):** Duration = 2 days, $ES = 0,\; LF = 6 \Rightarrow TF = 4$ days, 3 workers/day

**1. Before Leveling (All tasks at Earliest Start):**
* Days 1–2: $A(4) + C(3) + D(3) = \mathbf{10\text{ workers/day}}$ *(Huge Peak!)*
* Days 3–6: $A\text{ or }B = \mathbf{4\text{ workers/day}}$ *(Valley!)*
* Daily profile: `[10, 10, 4, 4, 4, 4]` $\Rightarrow$ Peak = **10 workers**, $\sum R^2 = 100+100+16+16+16+16 = 264$.

**2. After Resource Leveling (Keep D at Days 1–2; Shift C to Days 3–4 within its 4-day float):**
* Days 1–2: $A(4) + D(3) = \mathbf{7\text{ workers/day}}$
* Days 3–4: $A/B(4) + C(3) = \mathbf{7\text{ workers/day}}$
* Days 5–6: $B(4) = \mathbf{4\text{ workers/day}}$
* Daily profile: `[7, 7, 7, 7, 4, 4]` $\Rightarrow$ Peak drops from **10 to 7 workers**, $\sum R^2 = 49+49+49+49+16+16 = \mathbf{228}$, and the **6-day deadline is still met!**

## 5. Benefits of Resource Leveling

1. **Steady Workforce & Reduced Turnover:** Eliminates wasteful cycles of hiring, training, and laying off workers.
2. **Lower Costs:** Minimizes overtime wages, subcontracting surcharges, and idle equipment rental charges.
3. **Better Supervision & Quality:** A stable, predictable team size is easier to supervise and maintains higher work quality.
4. **Improved Project Feasibility:** Ensures the project schedule fits realistic manpower, equipment, and cash-flow limits before execution starts.

#### Model Question 2 (8 Marks – Part B University Exam Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 2.4 – Comprehensive Theory + Worked Histogram Example)*  
**Question:** Explain Resource Leveling and Resource Smoothing in detail. Illustrate with a numerical example how shifting non-critical activities within their float reduces peak resource demand without delaying project completion.

**Answer:**

**1. Resource Leveling vs. Resource Smoothing (3 Marks):**  
* **Resource Leveling:** Adjusts activity start and finish dates based on resource availability to balance resource demand against supply and smooth out extreme fluctuations. When resource caps cannot be met within existing float, resource leveling **may alter the critical path and extend the project completion date**.
* **Resource Smoothing:** A special case of leveling used when the **project deadline is strictly fixed**. It shifts non-critical activities **strictly within their available Total Float / Free Float** so that resource peaks are reduced without changing the critical path or extending the project finish date.

**2. Steps in Resource Leveling (1.5 Marks):**
1. Perform CPM forward/backward passes to identify critical tasks ($TF=0$) and non-critical tasks ($TF>0$).
2. Plot the initial **Resource Histogram** assuming all tasks start at $ES$.
3. Identify peak demand periods and low-demand ("valley") periods.
4. Shift non-critical tasks forward within their float from peak days into valley days to lower the maximum daily resource requirement and minimize fluctuations.

**3. Worked Numerical Example (3.5 Marks):**  
Consider a 6-day project with four tasks:
* **A (Critical):** Days 1–3 ($0\to 3$), $TF = 0$, requires **4 workers/day**
* **B (Critical):** Days 4–6 ($3\to 6$), $TF = 0$, requires **4 workers/day**
* **C (Non-critical):** Duration = 2 days, $ES = 0,\; LF = 6 \Rightarrow TF = 4$ days, requires **3 workers/day**
* **D (Non-critical):** Duration = 2 days, $ES = 0,\; LF = 6 \Rightarrow TF = 4$ days, requires **3 workers/day**

* **Initial Schedule (All at Earliest Start):**
  * Days 1–2: $A(4) + C(3) + D(3) = \mathbf{10\text{ workers/day}}$
  * Days 3–6: $A\text{ or }B = \mathbf{4\text{ workers/day}}$
  * **Peak Demand = 10 workers**; severe drop from 10 to 4 after Day 2.

```text
Workers (Before Leveling)          Workers (After Leveling)
10 | [C][C]                        10 |
 8 | [D][D]                         8 | [D][D] [C][C]
 6 | [A][A]                         6 | [A][A] [A][B] [B][B]
 4 | [A][A][A][B][B][B]             4 | [A][A] [A][B] [B][B]
   +-------------------> Days         +-------------------> Days
     1  2  3  4  5  6                   1  2  3  4  5  6
```

* **Leveled Schedule (Shift C to Days 3–4 within its 4-day float; keep D at Days 1–2):**
  * Days 1–2: $A(4) + D(3) = \mathbf{7\text{ workers/day}}$
  * Days 3–4: $A/B(4) + C(3) = \mathbf{7\text{ workers/day}}$
  * Days 5–6: $B(4) = \mathbf{4\text{ workers/day}}$

* **Conclusion:** By shifting non-critical task **C** by 2 days within its float, the **peak workforce drops by 30% (from 10 workers to 7 workers)**, the Sum of Squares ($\sum R^2$) drops from $264$ to $228$, and the **6-day project completion deadline remains unchanged**.
