## 1. Project Estimation: Time, Cost, and Resources

Accurate estimation is the backbone of realistic scheduling and budgeting. In project planning, estimates are prepared for **Time**, **Cost**, and **Resources (Man, Material, Tool)** using the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) as the baseline.

### A. Time Estimation
Time estimation determines the duration required to complete each activity considering normal working hours, resource productivity, and risk buffers.

**Common Time Estimation Techniques:**
1. **Expert Judgment:** Consulting experienced engineers or past project managers.
2. **Analogous (Top-Down) Estimation:** Using actual durations from similar past projects (fast, less accurate; used in early initiation).
3. **Parametric Estimation:** Multiplying unit productivity rates by quantity of work (e.g., $\text{1000 m cable laying} \div \text{100 m/day} = \text{10 days}$).
4. **Bottom-Up Estimation:** Estimating each lowest-level WBS work package and rolling them up (most accurate, time-consuming).
5. **Three-Point Estimation (PERT):** Using Optimistic ($t_o$), Most Likely ($t_m$), and Pessimistic ($t_p$) durations to handle uncertainty.

---

### B. Cost Estimation
Project costs are broadly divided into **Direct Costs** and **Indirect Costs**:

1. **Direct Costs:** Costs directly traceable to specific project activities (e.g., labor wages, raw materials, equipment hire, subcontractor fees). **Direct cost increases when a project is accelerated (crashed).**
2. **Indirect (Overhead) Costs:** Ongoing support costs not tied to a single task (e.g., site administration, project manager salary, office utilities, security, interest on capital, delay penalties). **Indirect cost decreases as project duration is shortened.**

**Types / Accuracy Levels of Cost Estimates:**
* **Rough Order of Magnitude (ROM) Estimate:** Made during initiation ($\pm 25\%\text{ to }75\%$ accuracy).
* **Budgetary Estimate:** Made during early planning ($\pm 10\%\text{ to }25\%$ accuracy).
* **Definitive Estimate:** Made from detailed WBS & vendor quotes before execution ($\pm 5\%\text{ to }10\%$ accuracy).

---

### C. Resource Estimation (Man, Material, Tool)
Resources are the physical and human inputs required to execute project tasks. They are classified as **3Ms / Man, Material, and Tool**:

1. **Man (Human / Labor Resources):**
   * Estimating the **number of personnel**, **skill category** (e.g., licensed electricians, civil masons, software architects), and **effort (man-hours / man-days)** needed per task.
   * Accounts for skill availability, shift patterns, and learning curves.

2. **Material (Consumable Resources):**
   * Estimating exact quantities and specifications of physical consumables (cement, steel, cables, switchgear, server licenses) using a **Bill of Materials (BOM)**.
   * Must account for **procurement lead times**, bulk discounts, storage space, and normal wastage allowances (typically 2–5%).

3. **Tool (Equipment, Machinery & Software Tools):**
   * Estimating specialized machinery, testing instruments, vehicles, cranes, CNC machines, or licensed software tools (e.g., Primavera, CAD) required per task.
   * Evaluates **make/buy/rent decisions**, machine capacity/hour, setup time, and preventive maintenance windows.

## 2. Crashing of Projects (Time–Cost Trade-Off Analysis)

### What is Project Crashing?
**Project Crashing** is a schedule compression technique in which **additional resources** (extra labor, overtime, second shifts, faster equipment) are applied to **critical path activities** to reduce the total project duration at the **minimum possible incremental cost**.

* **Crashing vs. Fast-Tracking:**  
  * **Crashing:** Adds cost/resources to shorten activity durations (Time–Cost trade-off).  
  * **Fast-Tracking:** Performs sequential activities in parallel/overlap without adding direct resources (increases rework risk).

[Click here to see the diagram for Project Crashing Time Cost Trade Off Curve](https://www.google.com/search?q=project+crashing+time+cost+trade+off+curve+direct+indirect+total+cost&udm=2)

### Key Terminology and Formulas in Crashing

1. **Normal Time ($NT$):** Standard time required to complete an activity with normal resource deployment.
2. **Normal Cost ($NC$):** Direct cost incurred when completing the activity in Normal Time.
3. **Crash Time ($CT$):** Minimum technically feasible time in which an activity can be completed by deploying maximum extra resources.
4. **Crash Cost ($CC$):** Direct cost associated with completing the activity in Crash Time ($CC \ge NC$).
5. **Maximum Crashable Time:** $\Delta T_{\max} = NT - CT$.
6. **Cost Slope (Incremental Direct Cost per Unit Time):**
   $$\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{\text{Crash Cost} - \text{Normal Cost}}{\text{Normal Time} - \text{Crash Time}} = \frac{CC - NC}{NT - CT}$$
   * **Interpretation:** A lower Cost Slope means it is **cheaper** to speed up that activity by 1 day/week.
7. **Total Project Cost:**
   $$\text{Total Cost} = \text{Total Direct Cost} + (\text{Indirect Cost per unit time} \times \text{Project Duration})$$

### Optimum Project Duration Rule
* As project duration is reduced:
  * **Direct Cost increases** (because expensive overtime/extra resources are added).
  * **Indirect Cost decreases** (because fewer weeks of site overhead/supervision are needed).
* **Stopping Rule for Cost Optimization:** Keep crashing as long as:
  $$\text{Incremental Direct Cost per unit time (Cost Slope)} < \text{Indirect Cost per unit time}$$
  The duration where **Total Cost is minimum** is the **Optimal Project Completion Time**.

## 3. Step-by-Step Procedure of Project Crashing

1. **Step 1 – Compute Normal Schedule & Critical Path:** Draw the network diagram using Normal Times ($NT$), calculate path durations, identify the **Critical Path**, and record Total Normal Direct Cost ($\sum NC$).
2. **Step 2 – Calculate Cost Slope & Crash Limit:** Compute $\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{CC - NC}{NT - CT}$ and crash limit $(NT - CT)$ for every activity.
3. **Step 3 – Calculate Initial Total Cost:** $\text{Total Cost} = \text{Normal Direct Cost} + (\text{Normal Duration} \times \text{Indirect Cost/unit time})$.
4. **Step 4 – Select Critical Activity to Crash:**
   * Only crash activities on the **current critical path(s)** (crashing non-critical activities wastes money without reducing project duration).
   * Pick the critical activity with the **lowest Cost Slope**.
   * *If multiple parallel critical paths exist*, crash **one activity on each parallel critical path simultaneously** (or a single shared/common critical activity) choosing the combination with the **lowest combined Cost Slope**.
5. **Step 5 – Determine How Much to Crash:** Crash by the minimum of:
   * Remaining crash capacity of the chosen activity, OR
   * Time until a non-critical path becomes critical (parallel critical path limit).
6. **Step 6 – Update Costs & Network:** Recompute path durations, Direct Cost, Indirect Cost, and Total Cost.
7. **Step 7 – Repeat Until Stopping Criterion:** Repeat Steps 4–6 until either:
   * All activities on a critical path reach their Crash Time (Crash Limit), OR
   * Minimum combined Cost Slope $\ge$ Indirect Cost per unit time (Optimal Cost reached), OR
   * Target deadline is achieved.

#### Question 1 (4 Marks — Solved Crashing Numerical)
**Source:** *Internal Assessment (Series Test-I), August 2025 (23HUT535 – Class: B. Tech), Part B – Q12(b) [BL: L3, CO2]*  
**Question:** Consider the data of a project as shown in the following table:

| Activity | Normal time (wks) | Normal cost (Rs.) | Crash time (wks) | Crash cost (Rs.) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1–2 | 7 | 700 | 4 | 850 |
| 1–3 | 5 | 500 | 3 | 700 |
| 1–4 | 8 | 600 | 5 | 1200 |
| 2–5 | 9 | 800 | 7 | 1250 |
| 3–5 | 5 | 700 | 3 | 1000 |
| 3–6 | 6 | 1100 | 5 | 1300 |
| 4–6 | 7 | 1200 | 5 | 1450 |
| 5–7 | 2 | 400 | 1 | 500 |
| 6–7 | 3 | 500 | 2 | 850 |

If the indirect cost per week is Rs. 200, find the optimal crashed project completion time.

**Answer:**

**Step 1: Calculate Cost Slope and Max Crashable Weeks for Each Activity**  
$$\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{CC - NC}{NT - CT}$$

| Activity | $NT$ | $NC$ | $CT$ | $CC$ | $\Delta T = NT - CT$ | $\Delta C = CC - NC$ | Cost Slope (Rs./wk) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **1–2** | 7 | 700 | 4 | 850 | 3 | 150 | **50** |
| **1–3** | 5 | 500 | 3 | 700 | 2 | 200 | **100** |
| **1–4** | 8 | 600 | 5 | 1200 | 3 | 600 | **200** |
| **2–5** | 9 | 800 | 7 | 1250 | 2 | 450 | **225** |
| **3–5** | 5 | 700 | 3 | 1000 | 2 | 300 | **150** |
| **3–6** | 6 | 1100 | 5 | 1300 | 1 | 200 | **200** |
| **4–6** | 7 | 1200 | 5 | 1450 | 2 | 250 | **125** |
| **5–7** | 2 | 400 | 1 | 500 | 1 | 100 | **100** |
| **6–7** | 3 | 500 | 2 | 850 | 1 | 350 | **350** |

* **Total Normal Direct Cost** $= 700 + 500 + 600 + 800 + 700 + 1100 + 1200 + 400 + 500 = \mathbf{Rs.\; 6,500}$

**Step 2: Identify Network Paths and Initial Critical Path(s)**
* Path 1: $1 \rightarrow 2 \rightarrow 5 \rightarrow 7 = 7 + 9 + 2 = \mathbf{18\text{ weeks}}$ *(Critical)*
* Path 2: $1 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 5 \rightarrow 7 = 5 + 5 + 2 = 12\text{ weeks}$
* Path 3: $1 \rightarrow 3 \rightarrow 6 \rightarrow 7 = 5 + 6 + 3 = 14\text{ weeks}$
* Path 4: $1 \rightarrow 4 \rightarrow 6 \rightarrow 7 = 8 + 7 + 3 = \mathbf{18\text{ weeks}}$ *(Critical)*

There are **two parallel critical paths** ($1\text{–}2\text{–}5\text{–}7$ and $1\text{–}4\text{–}6\text{–}7$) with **Normal Project Duration = 18 weeks**.
* **Initial Total Cost (18 weeks):**  
  $\text{Direct Cost } (6,500) + \text{Indirect Cost } (18 \times 200 = 3,600) = \mathbf{Rs.\; 10,100}$

**Step 3: Iteration 1 — Crash Parallel Critical Paths**  
Since Paths 1 and 4 share no common activity, we must crash one activity from Path 1 and one activity from Path 4 simultaneously:
* Cheapest on Path 1 ($1\text{–}2\text{–}5\text{–}7$): **1–2** (Slope = Rs. 50/wk, max 3 wks).
* Cheapest on Path 4 ($1\text{–}4\text{–}6\text{–}7$): **4–6** (Slope = Rs. 125/wk, max 2 wks).
* **Combined Cost Slope** $= 50 + 125 = \mathbf{Rs.\; 175/\text{week}}$.
* Since $\text{Rs.\; } 175/\text{wk} < \text{Indirect Cost } (\text{Rs.\; } 200/\text{wk})$, crashing is profitable (net saving of Rs. 25/wk).
* Maximum simultaneous crash possible without hitting Path 3 (14 wks) or exhausting `4–6`: **$\min(3,\; 2,\; 18-14) = 2$ weeks**.

Crash **1–2 by 2 weeks** (7 → 5) and **4–6 by 2 weeks** (7 → 5):
* **New Project Duration = 16 weeks** (Path 1 = 16 wks, Path 4 = 16 wks, Path 3 = 14 wks, Path 2 = 12 wks).
* **New Direct Cost** $= 6,500 + 2 \times (50 + 125) = 6,500 + 350 = \text{Rs.\; } 6,850$
* **New Indirect Cost** $= 16 \times 200 = \text{Rs.\; } 3,200$
* **Total Cost (16 weeks)** $= 6,850 + 3,200 = \mathbf{Rs.\; 10,050}$

**Step 4: Iteration 2 — Check Further Crashing to 15 Weeks**  
* Activity `4–6` is now fully crashed (0 weeks left).
* Cheapest remaining combination on Path 1 and Path 4: **1–2** (Rs. 50/wk) + **1–4** (Rs. 200/wk) $= \mathbf{Rs.\; 250/\text{week}}$.
* Since $\text{Rs.\; } 250/\text{wk} > \text{Indirect Cost } (\text{Rs.\; } 200/\text{wk})$, crashing further **increases** total cost ($6,850 + 250 + 15 \times 200 = \text{Rs.\; } 10,100$).

**Final Answer:**
* **Optimal Crashed Project Completion Time:** **16 weeks**
* **Minimum Total Project Cost:** **Rs. 10,050**

#### Question 2 (8 Marks — End-Semester Full Question)
**Source:** *Fifth Semester B. Tech. Degree Examination, Regular, November 2025 (23HUT535, QP Code: 5010BHU25T535), Part B – Module 2, Q14*  
**Question:** Explain the procedure of project crashing with an example.

**Answer:**

**1. Concept of Project Crashing (1.5 Marks):**  
**Project Crashing** is a network compression technique used in CPM to reduce total project completion time by assigning additional resources (overtime, extra workers, faster equipment) to critical activities at the minimum extra cost.  
* **Normal Time ($NT$) & Normal Cost ($NC$):** Standard duration and direct cost without acceleration.  
* **Crash Time ($CT$) & Crash Cost ($CC$):** Minimum feasible duration and its higher direct cost.  
* **Cost Slope:** Incremental direct cost per unit time saved:
  $$\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{CC - NC}{NT - CT}$$

**2. Step-by-Step Procedure of Project Crashing (3 Marks):**
1. **Determine Normal Schedule:** Draw the project network with Normal Times ($NT$), calculate path lengths, identify the **Critical Path**, and compute Normal Direct Cost ($\sum NC$).
2. **Compute Cost Slopes:** Calculate $\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{CC - NC}{NT - CT}$ and maximum crash limit $(NT - CT)$ for all activities.
3. **Compute Normal Total Cost:** $\text{Total Cost} = \text{Direct Cost} + (\text{Duration} \times \text{Indirect Cost per unit time})$.
4. **Select Critical Activity to Crash:** Identify the activity on the **current critical path** with the **lowest Cost Slope**. (If multiple parallel critical paths exist, select one activity from each path with the lowest combined cost slope, or a common activity shared by them.)
5. **Crash Within Limits:** Reduce duration by 1 unit (or up to the point where either the activity hits its Crash Time or a non-critical path becomes critical).
6. **Recalculate Costs:** Add the crashing cost to Direct Cost, reduce Indirect Cost accordingly, and find the new Total Cost.
7. **Stop at Optimum / Target:** Stop when all critical activities are fully crashed, the target deadline is met, or when **Incremental Crash Cost $\ge$ Indirect Cost saved per unit time** (minimum total cost achieved).

**3. Illustrative Example (3.5 Marks):**  
Suppose a project has 3 activities with **Indirect Cost = Rs. 1,000/day**:

| Activity | Predecessor | $NT$ (days) | $NC$ (Rs.) | $CT$ (days) | $CC$ (Rs.) | Cost Slope (Rs./day) | Max Crash |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| A | – | 5 | 4,000 | 3 | 5,200 | $\frac{5200-4000}{5-3} = \mathbf{600}$ | 2 days |
| B | A | 6 | 6,000 | 4 | 7,800 | $\frac{7800-6000}{6-4} = \mathbf{900}$ | 2 days |
| C | – | 8 | 5,000 | 6 | 7,400 | $\frac{7400-5000}{8-6} = \mathbf{1,200}$ | 2 days |

*(Paths: $A \rightarrow B = 5 + 6 = 11$ days; $C = 8$ days.)*

* **Normal Duration (11 days):**
  * Critical Path = $A \rightarrow B$ (11 days).
  * Normal Direct Cost $= 4,000 + 6,000 + 5,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 15,000$.
  * Indirect Cost $= 11 \times 1,000 = \text{Rs.\; } 11,000 \Rightarrow$ **Total Cost = Rs. 26,000**.

* **Step 1 — Crash A (cheapest on $A \rightarrow B$, Slope = Rs. 600 < Rs. 1,000):**
  * Crash A by **2 days** ($5 \to 3$ days). Now Path $A \rightarrow B = 3 + 6 = 9$ days; Path $C = 8$ days.
  * Direct Cost $= 15,000 + 2(600) = 16,200$; Indirect Cost $= 9 \times 1,000 = 9,000 \Rightarrow$ **Total Cost = Rs. 25,200**.

* **Step 2 — Crash B (next cheapest on $A \rightarrow B$, Slope = Rs. 900 < Rs. 1,000):**
  * Crash B by **1 day** ($6 \to 5$ days) until Path $A \rightarrow B$ equals Path $C$ (**8 days**).
  * Direct Cost $= 16,200 + 900 = 17,100$; Indirect Cost $= 8 \times 1,000 = 8,000 \Rightarrow$ **Total Cost = Rs. 25,100**.

* **Step 3 — Check Further Crashing (at 8 days, both $A \rightarrow B$ and $C$ are critical):**
  * Must crash B (900) + C (1,200) = Rs. 2,100/day, which exceeds Rs. 1,000/day indirect cost. So **stop!**
  * **Optimal Duration = 8 days**, **Minimum Total Cost = Rs. 25,100**.

### B. Self-Created Practice Questions (Covering Time, Cost & Resource Estimates)

#### Model Question 1 (2 Marks – Part A Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 2.2 – Cost Slope)*  
**Question:** Define "Cost Slope" in project crashing and state its formula.

**Answer:**  
**Cost Slope** is the additional direct cost incurred per unit of time saved when crashing (accelerating) an activity. It indicates the cost-effectiveness of shortening a critical task:
$$\text{Cost Slope} = \frac{\text{Crash Cost} - \text{Normal Cost}}{\text{Normal Time} - \text{Crash Time}}$$

#### Model Question 2 (5 Marks – Part B Style)
**Source:** *Self-Created (Syllabus Topic 2.2 – Resource & Cost Estimation)*  
**Question:** Explain how time, cost, and resources (man, material, tool) are estimated in project planning.

**Answer:**  
Effective project planning converts the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) into realistic schedules and budgets by estimating three core elements:

1. **Time Estimation:** Determines task durations using **Expert Judgment**, **Analogous** (historical comparison), **Parametric** (unit productivity rates), **Bottom-Up** (WBS-level rollup), or **Three-Point PERT** estimation.
2. **Cost Estimation:** Quantifies **Direct Costs** (labor, raw materials, subcontracts, equipment directly tied to tasks) and **Indirect Costs** (site overheads, supervision, utilities, penalties). Accuracy improves from Order-of-Magnitude ($\pm 25\%$) to Definitive ($\pm 5\%$) estimates.
3. **Resource Estimation (Man, Material, Tool):**
   * **Man (Manpower):** Estimates required workforce count, skill categories (engineers, technicians, helpers), and man-days per task.
   * **Material:** Prepares a Bill of Materials (BOM) specifying exact quantities, procurement lead times, and wastage allowances.
   * **Tool (Equipment/Software):** Estimates heavy machinery, testing instruments, vehicles, or software tools needed, evaluating machine-hours and rental vs. purchase costs.
