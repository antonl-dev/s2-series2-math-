### 2. Entities: Strong vs. Weak Entities

1. **Strong (Regular) Entity Type:**
   * An entity that has **its own independent Primary Key** (Key Attribute) that uniquely identifies each instance.
   * Drawn with a **Single Rectangle**.
   * *Examples:* `STUDENT` (identified by `Student_ID`), `TEAM` (identified by `Team_ID`).

2. **Weak Entity Type:**
   * An entity that **does NOT have a complete Primary Key of its own**. It can only be uniquely identified by combining its own **Partial Key (Discriminator)** (drawn with a *dashed underline*) with the **Primary Key of its Owner (Identifying) Strong Entity**.
   * Drawn with a **Double Rectangle**, connected to its owner via a **Double Diamond** (*Identifying Relationship*).
   * *Example:* In an `EMPLOYEE` database, `DEPENDENT` (spouse/child of an employee) only has `Dependent_Name` (e.g., `'Raju'`). Two different employees might both have a son named `'Raju'`. So `DEPENDENT` can only be uniquely identified by **`(Employee_ID, Dependent_Name)`**.

### 4. Relationships, Cardinality Ratios & Participation Constraints

A **Relationship** (drawn as a **Diamond**) connects two or more entities. Every binary relationship (between two entities $A$ and $B$) has a **Cardinality Ratio** that tells us how many instances of $B$ can be associated with one instance of $A$:

📸 **[Click here to view Cardinality Ratios (1:1, 1:N, M:N) in ER Diagrams on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=cardinality+ratios+in+er+diagram+1:1+1:N+M:N+dbms&udm=2)**

1. **One-to-One ($1:1$):**
   * One entity in $A$ is associated with at most one entity in $B$, and vice versa.
   * *Example:* One `EMPLOYEE` **manages** one `DEPARTMENT`, and one `DEPARTMENT` has only one manager.
   ```text
   [ EMPLOYEE ] -------- 1 < Manages > 1 -------- [ DEPARTMENT ]
   ```

2. **One-to-Many ($1:N$ or $N:1$):**
   * One entity in $A$ can be associated with **many ($N$)** entities in $B$, whereas each entity in $B$ belongs to **at most one ($1$)** entity in $A$.
   * *Example (from Oct 2025 Q1b):* One `TEAM` **has** many `PLAYERS`, and each `PLAYER` belongs to only one `TEAM`.
   ```text
   [ TEAM ] -------- 1 < Has > N -------- [ PLAYER ]
   ```

3. **Many-to-Many ($M:N$):**
   * One entity in $A$ can be associated with many entities in $B$, AND one entity in $B$ can be associated with many entities in $A$.
   * *Example (from Oct 2026 Q2a):* A `STUDENT` can **register** for many `COURSES`, and a `COURSE` can have many `STUDENTS`.
   ```text
   [ STUDENT ] -------- M < Registers > N -------- [ COURSE ]
   ```

4. **Recursive (Self-Referencing) Relationship:**
   * The **same entity type** participates more than once in a relationship in different **roles**!
   * *Example 1:* An `EMPLOYEE` *supervises* another `EMPLOYEE` (Roles: `Supervisor` [1] and `Subordinate` [N]).
   * *Example 2 (Directly asked in Oct 2025 Q1b!):* `TEAM` plays a `MATCH` where one `TEAM` is the **Host Team** and another `TEAM` is the **Guest Team**!

5. **Participation Constraints (Single Line vs. Double Line):**
   * **Partial Participation (Single Line `---`):** Some instances of the entity do not participate in the relationship *(e.g., not every employee manages a department)*.
   * **Total Participation (Double Line `===`):** Every single instance of the entity *must* participate in the relationship *(e.g., every `DEPARTMENT` must have a manager; every Weak Entity has total participation with its owner)*.

## PART B: Step-by-Step Rules for Converting (Synthesizing) an ER Diagram into a Relational Schema

> ⭐ **MEMORIZE THESE 7 MECHANICAL RULES:** Once you know these 7 rules, you can convert **ANY** ER diagram into Relational Tables in 3 minutes and score 8/8 marks!

📸 **[Click here to view ER-to-Relational Mapping Rules Summary Charts on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=ER+to+relational+mapping+rules+in+dbms+diagram&udm=2)**

---

### Rule 1: Mapping Strong (Regular) Entity Types
* **Action:** For each strong entity $E$ (single rectangle), create a separate table (relation) $R$ that includes all the **simple attributes** of $E$.
* **What if there is a Composite Attribute?** Flatten it! Include only its simple component attributes (e.g., if `Name` is split into `First_Name` and `Last_Name`, put `First_Name` and `Last_Name` as columns).
* **What if there is a Derived Attribute (dashed oval)?** Do **NOT** store it as a column in the table (it is calculated at runtime).
* **Primary Key:** Choose the entity's key attribute as the **Primary Key** of table $R$ (and underline it!).

**Example:**
```text
ER Diagram:    ( _Student_ID_ ) --- [ STUDENT ] --- ( Name ) , ( Branch )

Relational Schema:
STUDENT ( Student_ID (PK), Name, Branch )
```

---

### Rule 2: Mapping Weak Entity Types (Double Rectangle)
* **Action:** For each weak entity $W$ owned by strong entity $E$, create a table $R$ and include all simple attributes of $W$.
* **Foreign Key:** Include the **Primary Key of the Owner Entity $E$** as a **Foreign Key ($FK$)** in table $R$.
* **Primary Key of the Weak Entity Table:** The Primary Key of $R$ is the **Composite Key** formed by:
  $$\text{PK of } R = \underline{(\text{Primary Key of Owner } E, \text{ Partial Key of } W)}$$

**Example (from Nov 2025 End-Sem Q3!):**
* Suppose `Played` (weak entity with partial key `played`) belongs to owner `Track` (with primary key `track_id`):
  $$\text{Played} (\underline{\text{track\_id (FK)}, \text{played}})$$

---

### Rule 3: Mapping Binary $1:1$ Relationships
Suppose Entity $A$ and Entity $B$ have a $1:1$ relationship (e.g., `EMPLOYEE` `1 — <Manages> — 1` `DEPARTMENT`).
* **Action:** Do **NOT** create a third table! Instead, take the **Primary Key of one table** and put it as a **Foreign Key** inside the other table.
* **Which side should receive the Foreign Key?** Put the Foreign Key inside the entity that has **Total Participation** (every `DEPARTMENT` has a manager, so put `Mgr_ENO` as a Foreign Key inside `DEPARTMENT`! That prevents `NULL` values). Also include any attributes of the relationship itself on that same table.

---

### Rule 4: Mapping Binary $1:N$ (One-to-Many) Relationships *(Super Common!)*
Suppose Entity $A$ (the **$1$-side**) and Entity $B$ (the **$N$-side** / Many-side) have a $1:N$ relationship.

> **THE GOLDEN RULE FOR $1:N$:**
> **"Take the Primary Key of the $1$-side and put it as a Foreign Key into the $N$-side (Many-side) table!"**
> *(Why? Because each item on the $N$-side belongs to only ONE item on the $1$-side, so it fits in a single cell without creating multivalued attributes!)*

**Example:**
```text
[ TEAM ] (1) ---------- < Has > ---------- (N) [ PLAYER ]
PK: Team_ID                                    PK: Player_No
```
* **Wrong way:** Putting `Player_No` inside `TEAM` (a team has 15 players, which would put 15 values in one cell!).
* **Right way (Put PK of 1-side into N-side):**
  * $\text{TEAM}(\underline{\text{Team\_ID}}, \text{Name}, \text{City})$
  * $\text{PLAYER}(\underline{\text{Player\_No}}, \text{Name}, \text{DoB}, \textbf{Team\_ID (FK)})$

---

### Rule 5: Mapping Binary $M:N$ (Many-to-Many) Relationships *(Asked in Oct 2026 Q2!)*
Suppose Entity $A$ and Entity $B$ have an $M:N$ relationship (e.g., `STUDENT` `M — <Registers> — N` `COURSE`).
* Can we put `Course_ID` inside `STUDENT`? **No** (a student takes many courses).
* Can we put `Student_ID` inside `COURSE`? **No** (a course has many students).

> **THE GOLDEN RULE FOR $M:N$:**
> **"Every $M:N$ relationship ALWAYS becomes a NEW THIRD TABLE (Relationship / Junction Table)!"**

* **What goes inside this new 3rd table?**
  1. The **Primary Key of Entity $A$** (as a Foreign Key).
  2. The **Primary Key of Entity $B$** (as a Foreign Key).
  3. Any **descriptive attributes** attached to the Relationship diamond itself (e.g., `Semester`, `Grade`).
* **What is the Primary Key of this new 3rd table?**
  * The **Composite Key** made of both Foreign Keys together: $\underline{(\text{PK of } A, \text{PK of } B)}$!

**Example (Oct 2026 Q2):**
* `STUDENT` ($\underline{\text{Student\_ID}}$, `Name`, `Branch`)
* `COURSE` ($\underline{\text{Course\_ID}}$, `Course_Name`, `Credits`)
* `REGISTRATION` ($\underline{\text{Student\_ID (FK)}, \text{Course\_ID (FK)}}$, `Semester`, `Grade`)

---

### Rule 6: Mapping Multivalued Attributes (Double Oval)
Suppose `STUDENT` has a multivalued attribute `(( Phone_No ))`.
* Since a relational table cannot have multiple phone numbers in a single cell (violates 1NF), **every multivalued attribute becomes a NEW SEPARATE TABLE**!
* **Columns of the new table:** $\text{STUDENT\_PHONE}(\underline{\text{Student\_ID (FK)}, \text{Phone\_No}})$ (Both columns together form the Composite Primary Key!).

---

### Quick Cheat-Sheet: How Many Tables Do I Create?
| ER Construct | Does it create a NEW table? | How Keys Work |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strong Entity** | **YES** (1 table) | Own PK |
| **Weak Entity** | **YES** (1 table) | $\text{PK} = \underline{(\text{Owner PK}, \text{Partial Key})}$ |
| **$1:1$ Relationship** | **NO** (0 extra tables) | Put PK of one side as FK in the other side |
| **$1:N$ Relationship** | **NO** (0 extra tables)* | Put **PK of $1$-side** as **FK in $N$-side** |
| **$M:N$ Relationship** | **YES! (1 new junction table)** | $\text{PK} = \underline{(\text{PK}_1\text{ (FK)}, \text{PK}_2\text{ (FK)})}$ + relationship attributes |
| **Multivalued Attribute** | **YES! (1 new table)** | $\text{PK} = \underline{(\text{Entity PK (FK)}, \text{Attribute})}$ |

* One `Track` ($1$) was played at many timestamps ($N$) in the weak entity `Played` (partial key: `played`).
* By the Weak Entity mapping rule (Rule 2), the relation `PLAYED` includes the Primary Key of its owner `TRACK` as a **Foreign Key**, combined with its partial key `played` to form the **Composite Primary Key**:

  * **Primary Key:** `(track_id, played)` *(or `(album_id, track_id, played)`)*
  * **Foreign Key:** `track_id` (and `album_id`) references `TRACK`

### Quick 30-Second Recap of 2.2
* **Strong Entity** = Single Rectangle, has its own PK (underlined oval).
