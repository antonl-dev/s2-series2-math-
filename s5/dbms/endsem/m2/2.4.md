## PART A: What is a JOIN ($\bowtie$) Operation?

In Module 2.3, we saw that combining two tables using a **Cartesian Product ($\times$)** followed by a **Selection ($\sigma$)** is clumsy because $R \times S$ first generates every possible pair of rows (including thousands of meaningless rows) before filtering them.

To solve this, Relational Algebra provides the **JOIN operation**, denoted by the bowtie symbol **$\bowtie$**.

* **Definition:** The **Join** operation combines related tuples from two relations $R$ and $S$ into single, wider tuples **only when they satisfy a specified join condition**.
* **Mathematical Equivalence:**
  $$R \bowtie_{\langle \text{join condition} \rangle} S \;\equiv\; \sigma_{\langle \text{join condition} \rangle}(R \times S)$$
* **Why is $\bowtie$ much better than $\sigma(R \times S)$?**
  1. **Shorter and cleaner** to write in exams.
  2. **Much faster in real DBMSs** because the query engine matches keys directly (using indexes/hashing) without building the giant Cartesian product in memory first.

## PART B: Inner Joins – Theta Join, Equi-Join, and Natural Join

Let's trace all three Inner Joins using two sample tables: **`EMP`** and **`DEPT`**.

**Table 1: `EMP` (3 columns, 3 rows)**
| ENO | NAME | DNO (FK) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 101 | Adhil | 10 |
| 102 | Meera | 20 |
| 103 | Kiran | NULL |

**Table 2: `DEPT` (2 columns, 3 rows)**
| DNO (PK) | DNAME |
| :--- | :--- |
| 10 | EEE |
| 20 | Accounts |
| 30 | Physics |

---

### 1. Theta Join ($R \bowtie_\theta S$)
* **Definition:** A general join operation where the join condition $\theta$ is of the form $R.A_i \;\text{op}\; S.B_j$, and $\text{op}$ can be **any** comparison operator from $\{=, \neq, <, \le, >, \ge\}$.
* **Syntax:**
  $$R \bowtie_{\theta} S$$
* **Example (Using $>$ instead of $=$):**
  Find all pairs of `(Employee, Project)` where the employee's `SALARY` is greater than the project's `PCOST`:
  $$\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{SALARY} > \text{PCOST}} \text{PROJECT}$$

---

### 2. Equi-Join ($R \bowtie_{R.A = S.B} S$)
* **Definition:** The most common type of Theta Join where the **only comparison operator used in the join condition is equality ($=$)**.
* **Crucial Property of Equi-Join (Always mention this in exams!):**
  * In the result of an **Equi-Join**, **both joining attributes** (one from $R$ and one from $S$) are **retained** in the output table!
  * Therefore, the result always has one or more pairs of columns that contain **identical values in every single row**.
  * **Degree of Equi-Join:** $\text{Degree}(R) + \text{Degree}(S)$ columns.

#### Visual Trace of Equi-Join ($\text{EMP} \bowtie_{\text{EMP.DNO} = \text{DEPT.DNO}} \text{DEPT}$):
Notice that **both** `EMP.DNO` and `DEPT.DNO` appear side-by-side in the output ($3 + 2 = \mathbf{5\text{ columns}}$):

| ENO | NAME | **EMP.DNO** | **DEPT.DNO** | DNAME |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 101 | Adhil | **10** | **10** | EEE |
| 102 | Meera | **20** | **20** | Accounts |

*(Notice: Kiran with `DNO = NULL` and Dept `30 (Physics)` with no employees are dropped because Inner Joins only keep matching rows!)*

---

### 3. Natural Join ($R \bowtie S$ or $R * S$)
Why keep two identical columns (`EMP.DNO` and `DEPT.DNO`) in the output when their values are always equal? **Natural Join** was created to eliminate that redundant duplicate column automatically!

* **Definition:** Denoted by **$\bowtie$** (with **no condition subscript**) or **$*$**, a **Natural Join** is a specialized Equi-Join that:
  1. Automatically performs an equality check ($=$) on **all pairs of attributes that have the exact same name** in both relations $R$ and $S$, **AND**
  2. **Automatically projects out (removes) the duplicate joining column(s)** from the resulting relation!
* **Degree of Natural Join:** If $R$ has $n$ columns, $S$ has $m$ columns, and they share $k$ common column names, the result has **$n + m - k$ columns**.

📸 **[Click here to view Equi-Join vs Natural Join Visual Comparison on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=difference+between+equi+join+and+natural+join+in+dbms+with+example&udm=2)**

#### Visual Trace of Natural Join ($\text{EMP} \bowtie \text{DEPT}$):
Since `DNO` has the exact same name in both `EMP` and `DEPT`, $\text{EMP} \bowtie \text{DEPT}$ automatically joins on `EMP.DNO = DEPT.DNO` and keeps **only ONE `DNO` column** ($3 + 2 - 1 = \mathbf{4\text{ columns}}$):

| ENO | NAME | **DNO** | DNAME |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 101 | Adhil | **10** | EEE |
| 102 | Meera | **20** | Accounts |

---

### ⚠️ Important Exam Question: What if the Foreign Key and Primary Key have DIFFERENT Names?
Look at the schema in your **Oct 2025 Q2(b)** and **Nov 2025 Q4** question papers:
* In `EMPLOYEE`, the department number column is named **`DNUM`**.
* In `DEPARTMENT`, the department number column is named **`DNO`**.

Since `DNUM` and `DNO` do **not** have the same column name, what happens if you write $\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie \text{DEPARTMENT}$ without a subscript? Because they share no common column name, a strict Natural Join would turn into a Cartesian Product!

**So how do you join `EMPLOYEE` and `DEPARTMENT` in your exam? You have 2 100%-correct ways:**

1. **Method 1 (Use Equi-Join with explicit subscript – Easiest & Best!):**
   $$\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \text{DEPARTMENT}$$
2. **Method 2 (Rename `DNO` to `DNUM` using $\rho$, then use Natural Join $\bowtie$):**
   $$\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie \rho_{\text{DEPT}(\text{DNUM}, \text{DNAME}, \text{DLOCATION}, \text{DPHONE}, \text{MGRENO})}(\text{DEPARTMENT})$$

*(Always use **Method 1 ($\bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}}$)** in your exam—it is fast, crystal clear, and gets full marks every time!)*

---

### Side-by-Side Comparison Table: Equi-Join vs. Natural Join

*(Frequently asked as a 3-mark distinction question!)*

| Feature | Equi-Join ($R \bowtie_{R.A = S.B} S$) | Natural Join ($R \bowtie S$ or $R * S$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Join Condition** | Must be **explicitly written** as a subscript using $=$ (e.g., $\bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}}$). | **Implicit** (no subscript written; automatically equates attributes with the **same name**). |
| **2. Column Names Required** | Joining columns can have **different names** (e.g., `DNUM` and `DNO`) or the same name. | Joining columns **must have the exact same name** (e.g., `DNO` and `DNO`) in both tables. |
| **3. Duplicate Columns in Output** | **Retains BOTH joining columns** in the result (contains redundant duplicate column). | **Automatically eliminates the duplicate column** (only one copy of the common attribute is kept). |
| **4. Number of Columns (Degree)** | $\text{Degree}(R) + \text{Degree}(S)$ | $\text{Degree}(R) + \text{Degree}(S) - (\text{No. of common attributes})$ |

## PART C: Outer Joins ($\, \, \$)

In all the **Inner Joins** above (Theta, Equi-Join, Natural Join), notice what happened to:
* Employee `103 (Kiran)` whose `DNO` was `NULL`, and
* Department `30 (Physics)` which had zero employees.

Both were **completely lost** from the output! Tuples without a matching counterpart in the other table are called **Dangling Tuples**.

When users want to keep **all rows** from one or both tables—even if they have no match in the other table—we use **Outer Joins**, which pad the missing side with **`NULL`** values:

📸 **[Click here to view Left, Right, and Full Outer Join Diagrams on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=left+outer+join+right+outer+join+full+outer+join+in+relational+algebra+example&udm=2)**

### 1. Left Outer Join ($R \ S$)
* **Rule:** Keeps **every tuple in the LEFT relation $R$**. If a tuple in $R$ has no matching tuple in $S$, the attributes of $S$ are filled with **`NULL`**.
* **Result of $\text{EMP} \ \text{DEPT}$:**
  | ENO | NAME | DNO | DNAME |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | 101 | Adhil | 10 | EEE |
  | 102 | Meera | 20 | Accounts |
  | **103** | **Kiran** | **NULL** | **NULL** *(Kept from Left table!)* |

### 2. Right Outer Join ($R \ S$)
* **Rule:** Keeps **every tuple in the RIGHT relation $S$**. If a tuple in $S$ has no matching tuple in $R$, the attributes of $R$ are filled with **`NULL`**.
* **Result of $\text{EMP} \ \text{DEPT}$:**
  | ENO | NAME | DNO | DNAME |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | 101 | Adhil | 10 | EEE |
  | 102 | Meera | 20 | Accounts |
  | **NULL** | **NULL** | **30** | **Physics** *(Kept from Right table!)* |

### 3. Full Outer Join ($R \ S$)
* **Rule:** Keeps **all tuples from BOTH the left relation $R$ and the right relation $S$**, padding missing values on either side with **`NULL`**.
* **Result of $\text{EMP} \ \text{DEPT}$:**
  | ENO | NAME | DNO | DNAME |
  | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
  | 101 | Adhil | 10 | EEE |
  | 102 | Meera | 20 | Accounts |
  | **103** | **Kiran** | **NULL** | **NULL** |
  | **NULL** | **NULL** | **30** | **Physics** |

## PART D: Complete Relational Algebra PYQs & Master Query Examples

Here is the exact schema given in **both Oct 2025 Internal Test Q2(b) [4 Marks]** and **Nov 2025 End-Sem Exam Q4 [8 Marks]**:

```text
EMPLOYEE   ( ENO, NAME, ADDRESS, DOB, AGE, GENDER, SALARY, DNUM, SUPERENO )
DEPARTMENT ( DNO, DNAME, DLOCATION, DPHONE, MGRENO )
PROJECT    ( PNO, PNAME, PLOCATION, PCOST, CDNO )
```
**Foreign Keys Explained in the Question Paper:**
1. **`DNUM`** in `EMPLOYEE` $\rightarrow$ references **`DNO`** in `DEPARTMENT` *(which department an employee belongs to)*.
2. **`MGRENO`** in `DEPARTMENT` $\rightarrow$ references **`ENO`** in `EMPLOYEE` *(which employee manages the department)*.
3. **`CDNO`** in `PROJECT` $\rightarrow$ references **`DNO`** in `DEPARTMENT` *(which department controls the project)*.
4. **`SUPERENO`** in `EMPLOYEE` $\rightarrow$ references **`ENO`** in `EMPLOYEE` *(the supervisor of each employee)*.

---

### ✅ Full Solved Answer: Nov 2025 End-Sem Exam – Question 4 [8 Marks] (2 Marks × 4)

#### Query 4(a): *"Names of female employees whose salary is more than 20000."*
$$\pi_{\text{NAME}}\Big(\sigma_{\text{GENDER} = \text{'F'} \;\wedge\; \text{SALARY} > 20000}(\text{EMPLOYEE})\Big)$$

---

#### Query 4(b): *"Salaries of employee from 'Accounts' department."*
* **Using Join ($\bowtie$):**
  $$\pi_{\text{SALARY}}\Big(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \sigma_{\text{DNAME} = \text{'Accounts'}}(\text{DEPARTMENT})\Big)$$
* *(Or selecting outside the join—both are 100% equivalent):*
  $$\pi_{\text{SALARY}}\Big(\sigma_{\text{DNAME} = \text{'Accounts'}}\big(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \text{DEPARTMENT}\big)\Big)$$

---

#### Query 4(c): *"List all locations that are either department locations or project locations."*
* **Using Project ($\pi$) and Union ($\cup$):**
  $$\pi_{\text{DLOCATION}}(\text{DEPARTMENT}) \;\cup\; \pi_{\text{PLOCATION}}(\text{PROJECT})$$

---

#### Query 4(d): *"Find the names of employees who are not managers of any department."*
* **Step 1:** Subtract Manager IDs (`MGRENO`) from all Employee IDs (`ENO`) using Set Difference ($-$):
  $$\text{NON\_MGRS}(\text{ENO}) \leftarrow \pi_{\text{ENO}}(\text{EMPLOYEE}) \;-\; \pi_{\text{MGRENO}}(\text{DEPARTMENT})$$
* **Step 2:** Join with `EMPLOYEE` to get their `NAME`:
  $$\text{RESULT} \leftarrow \pi_{\text{NAME}}(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie \text{NON\_MGRS})$$
* *(Or written as a single one-line expression):*
  $$\pi_{\text{NAME}}\Big(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{EMPLOYEE.ENO} = \text{MGR.ENO}} \big(\pi_{\text{ENO}}(\text{EMPLOYEE}) - \rho_{\text{MGR}(\text{ENO})}(\pi_{\text{MGRENO}}(\text{DEPARTMENT}))\big)\Big)$$

---

### ✅ Full Solved Answer: Oct 2025 Internal Test – Question II(b) [4 Marks] (1 Mark × 4)

*(Note: Queries `i` and `ii` in Oct 2025 are identical to `4(a)` and `4(b)` above! Below are `iii` and `iv`):*

#### Query II(b)(iii): *"For each employee return name of the employee along with his department name and the names of projects in which he/she works."*
* **How the 3 tables connect:**
  * `EMPLOYEE` connects to `DEPARTMENT` via **`DNUM = DNO`**.
  * `DEPARTMENT` connects to `PROJECT` via **`DNO = CDNO`**.
* **Step-by-Step Solution using Joins ($\bowtie$):**
  $$\text{EMP\_DEPT} \leftarrow \text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \text{DEPARTMENT}$$
  $$\text{EMP\_DEPT\_PROJ} \leftarrow \text{EMP\_DEPT} \bowtie_{\text{DNO} = \text{CDNO}} \text{PROJECT}$$
  $$\text{RESULT} \leftarrow \pi_{\text{NAME}, \;\text{DNAME}, \;\text{PNAME}}(\text{EMP\_DEPT\_PROJ})$$
* **Single-Line Expression:**
  $$\pi_{\text{NAME}, \;\text{DNAME}, \;\text{PNAME}}\Big((\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \text{DEPARTMENT}) \bowtie_{\text{DNO} = \text{CDNO}} \text{PROJECT}\Big)$$

---

#### Query II(b)(iv): *"Names of employees working in 'physics' department, not in 'chemistry' department."*
* **Using Join ($\bowtie$) and Set Difference ($-$):**
  $$\pi_{\text{NAME}}\Big(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \sigma_{\text{DNAME} = \text{'physics'}}(\text{DEPARTMENT})\Big) \;-\; \pi_{\text{NAME}}\Big(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}} \sigma_{\text{DNAME} = \text{'chemistry'}}(\text{DEPARTMENT})\Big)$$

### Quick 30-Second Recap of 2.4
* **Equi-Join ($R \bowtie_{R.A = S.B} S$):** Uses $=$ condition explicitly; keeps **both** joining columns in the output.
* **Natural Join ($R \bowtie S$):** Joins automatically on columns with the **exact same name**; **removes** the duplicate column.
* **Outer Joins ($\, \, \$):** Preserve unmatched rows by filling missing values with `NULL`.
* **How the 3 Exam Tables Connect:**
  * Employee works in Dept: `EMPLOYEE` $\bowtie_{\text{DNUM} = \text{DNO}}$ `DEPARTMENT`
  * Employee manages Dept: `EMPLOYEE` $\bowtie_{\text{ENO} = \text{MGRENO}}$ `DEPARTMENT`
  * Dept controls Project: `DEPARTMENT` $\bowtie_{\text{DNO} = \text{CDNO}}$ `PROJECT`
  * Supervisor of Employee: `EMPLOYEE` $\bowtie_{\text{SUPERENO} = \text{SENO}}$ `SUP`
