## PART A: Redundancy and Anomalies in Database Design

### 1. Why Bad Table Design Causes Problems
Suppose a university designer decides to store **Student details**, **Course details**, and **Enrollment details** all mixed together inside **one single giant table** called `Course_Enrollment`:

$$\text{Course\_Enrollment} (\underline{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}}, \text{StudentName}, \text{CourseName}, \text{Instructor})$$
*(Notice the Composite Primary Key is **`(StudentID, CourseID)`** because one student can take many courses, and one course can have many students).*

Let's look at the actual data inside this poorly designed table:

📸 **[Click here to view Insertion, Deletion, and Update Anomalies Diagrams on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=insertion+update+and+deletion+anomalies+in+dbms+with+example+table&udm=2)**

**Table: `Course_Enrollment` (Un-normalized / Bad Design)**
| StudentID (PK) | StudentName | CourseID (PK) | CourseName | Instructor | Notice the Problem! |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **S01** | Adhil | **C100** | DBMS | Dr. Smith | `'C100, DBMS, Dr. Smith'` repeated! |
| **S02** | Rahul | **C100** | DBMS | Dr. Smith | `'C100, DBMS, Dr. Smith'` repeated! |
| **S03** | Meera | **C100** | DBMS | Dr. Smith | `'C100, DBMS, Dr. Smith'` repeated! |
| **S01** | Adhil | **C105** | Robotics | Dr. Nair | **Only 1 student** in `'C105 (Robotics)'`! |

Because independent facts about **Students** and **Courses** are combined into one table, we get massive **Data Redundancy** (wasted space). Worse yet, whenever we try to `INSERT`, `DELETE`, or `UPDATE` rows, we suffer from **three serious flaws called Database Anomalies**:

---

### 2. The Three Types of Anomalies (With Exact Examples)

#### 1. Insertion Anomaly *(Directly Asked in Nov 2025 End-Sem Q7b [4 Marks]!)*
* **Definition:** An **Insertion Anomaly** occurs when we are **unable to insert valid information about one entity** into the database without also having information about another unrelated entity.
* **Example on `Course_Enrollment`:**
  * Suppose the university introduces a brand-new course **"Artificial Intelligence"** (`CourseID = 'C101'`, `Instructor = 'Dr. Rao'`), **but no student has registered for it yet**.
  * If we try to insert this new course into `Course_Enrollment`, what do we put for `StudentID` and `StudentName`? We would have to put **`NULL`**:
    $$\text{INSERT: } (\textbf{NULL}, \text{NULL}, \text{'C101'}, \text{'Artificial Intelligence'}, \text{'Dr. Rao'})$$
  * ❌ **Why the DBMS Rejects This:** `StudentID` is part of the **Composite Primary Key $\underline{(\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID})}$**! By the **Entity Integrity Constraint**, **no part of a Primary Key can EVER be `NULL`**!
  * Therefore, **we cannot record the existence of a new course until at least one student enrolls in it!** This is an **Insertion Anomaly**.

---

#### 2. Deletion Anomaly
* **Definition:** A **Deletion Anomaly** occurs when deleting a tuple to remove information about one entity **unintentionally destroys the ONLY copy of valid information about another entity**.
* **Example on `Course_Enrollment`:**
  * Look at row 4 of our table above: `S01 (Adhil)` is the **only student** currently enrolled in course **`C105 (Robotics, taught by Dr. Nair)`**.
  * Suppose Adhil drops out of the `'Robotics'` course, so we delete his enrollment row:
    `DELETE FROM Course_Enrollment WHERE StudentID = 'S01' AND CourseID = 'C105';`
  * ❌ **The Disaster:** The moment we delete that row, we **also completely lose the information that Course `C105` is named `'Robotics'` and is taught by `'Dr. Nair'`**!

---

#### 3. Update (or Modification) Anomaly
* **Definition:** An **Update Anomaly** occurs when the same piece of information is duplicated across many rows, so changing a single fact requires updating **multiple rows**—and failing to update even one of those rows causes **Data Inconsistency**.
* **Example on `Course_Enrollment`:**
  * Course `C100 (DBMS)` has 60 students enrolled, so the fact that `C100` is taught by `'Dr. Smith'` is repeated across **60 different rows**.
  * If `'Dr. Smith'` leaves and `'Dr. Mathew'` takes over `C100`, we must update all 60 rows. If a system crash or buggy query updates only 59 rows and misses 1 row, the database now shows two different instructors for the exact same course!

> 💡 **How Do We Fix All 3 Anomalies?**
> By **Normalizing (Decomposing)** the single bad table into smaller, independent tables (`STUDENT`, `COURSE`, and `ENROLLMENT`) using **Functional Dependencies**!

## PART B: Functional Dependencies (FD) and Their Types

> ⭐ **DIRECT EXAM QUESTION:** Asked in **Oct 2025 Internal Test Q4(a) (3.5 Marks)**: *"Explain Functional dependency and its types with suitable examples."*

### 1. What is a Functional Dependency ($X \rightarrow Y$)?

* **Notation:** Written as **$X \rightarrow Y$** (read as *"$X$ functionally determines $Y$"* or *"$Y$ is functionally dependent on $X$"*).
  * **$X$ (Left-Hand Side / LHS):** Called the **Determinant**.
  * **$Y$ (Right-Hand Side / RHS):** Called the **Dependent**.
* **Intuitive Meaning:** If you know the value of $X$, there is **only ONE possible value of $Y$** associated with it!
* **Formal Mathematical Definition (Always write this in the exam!):**
  > *"In a relation schema $R$, a Functional Dependency **$X \rightarrow Y$** holds if and only if, for any two tuples $t_1$ and $t_2$ in any legal instance of $R$:*
  > $$\text{If } t_1[X] = t_2[X], \text{ then we MUST have } t_1[Y] = t_2[Y]."$$
  > *(In plain English: Whenever two rows agree on their **$X$** values, they **must also agree** on their **$Y$** values!)*

📸 **[Click here to view Functional Dependency Concept and Examples Diagrams on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=functional+dependency+in+dbms+with+example+diagram&udm=2)**

#### Visual Example: How to Check if $X \rightarrow Y$ Holds in a Table
Look at `Course_Enrollment` above:
1. **Does `CourseID -> CourseName` hold?**
   * Every time `CourseID` is `'C100'`, is `CourseName` always `'DBMS'`? **YES!** So **`CourseID -> CourseName` HOLDS**.
2. **Does `StudentID -> CourseID` hold?**
   * Look at `StudentID = 'S01'` (Adhil): in Row 1 his `CourseID` is `'C100'`, and in Row 4 his `CourseID` is `'C105'`. Same `StudentID`, **different** `CourseID`! So **`StudentID -> CourseID` DOES NOT HOLD**.

---

### 2. The 4 Major Types of Functional Dependencies (With Examples)

When an exam asks *"Explain the types of Functional Dependencies with suitable examples"*, write these **4 pairs of types**:

```text
                +---------------------------------------------+
                |       TYPES OF FUNCTIONAL DEPENDENCIES      |
                +----------------------+----------------------+
                                       |
     +-----------------+---------------+---------------+-----------------+
     |                 |                               |                 |
     v                 v                               v                 v
1. Trivial vs.    2. Full Functional           3. Transitive        4. Multivalued
   Non-Trivial       Dependency vs.               Functional           Dependency
   FD                Partial Dependency           Dependency           (X ->> Y)
                     (Used in 2NF!)               (Used in 3NF!)
```

---

#### Type 1: Trivial vs. Non-Trivial Functional Dependency

* **a) Trivial Functional Dependency:**
  * A functional dependency $X \rightarrow Y$ is **Trivial** if the right-hand side **$Y$ is a subset of the left-hand side $X$ ($Y \subseteq X$)**.
  * It is called "trivial" because it is automatically satisfied in every possible table!
  * *Examples:*
    * $\{\text{Roll\_No}, \text{Name}\} \rightarrow \text{Roll\_No}$ *(since $\{\text{Roll\_No}\} \subseteq \{\text{Roll\_No}, \text{Name}\}$)*
    * $\text{Roll\_No} \rightarrow \text{Roll\_No}$

* **b) Non-Trivial Functional Dependency:**
  * A functional dependency $X \rightarrow Y$ is **Non-Trivial** if $Y$ is **not** a subset of $X$ ($Y \not\subseteq X$).
  * It is **Completely Non-Trivial** if $X$ and $Y$ share **zero common attributes ($X \cap Y = \emptyset$)**.
  * *Examples:*
    * $\text{Roll\_No} \rightarrow \text{Name}$ *(Completely Non-Trivial)*
    * $\{\text{Roll\_No}, \text{Name}\} \rightarrow \{\text{Name}, \text{Branch}\}$ *(Semi-Trivial, since `Branch` is not in LHS)*

---

#### Type 2: Full Functional Dependency vs. Partial Functional Dependency *(Crucial for 2NF!)*

This distinction applies when the left-hand side $X$ is a **Composite Key** (made of 2 or more attributes, like $\underline{(\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID})}$):

📸 **[Click here to view Full vs Partial Functional Dependency Diagrams on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=full+functional+dependency+vs+partial+dependency+in+dbms+diagram&udm=2)**

* **a) Full Functional Dependency:**
  * An FD $X \rightarrow Y$ is a **Full Functional Dependency** if $Y$ depends on the **ENTIRE set $X$**, and removing **any single attribute** from $X$ causes the dependency to no longer hold!
  * *Formal Condition:* For any proper subset $X' \subset X$, $X' \not\rightarrow Y$.
  * *Example:* In $\text{ENROLLMENT}(\underline{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}}, \text{Grade})$:
    $$\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\} \rightarrow \text{Grade}$$
    * Can `StudentID` alone determine `Grade`? **No** (a student gets different grades in different courses).
    * Can `CourseID` alone determine `Grade`? **No** (different students get different grades in the same course).
    * You need **BOTH `StudentID` AND `CourseID` together** to determine `Grade`. Hence, it is a **Full Functional Dependency**!

* **b) Partial Functional Dependency:**
  * An FD $X \rightarrow Y$ has a **Partial Dependency** if a non-prime attribute $Y$ can be determined by **just a part (proper subset) of a Composite Candidate Key $X$**!
  * *Example:* In $\text{Course\_Enrollment}(\underline{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}}, \text{StudentName}, \text{CourseName}, \text{Grade})$:
    * The Primary Key is **`(StudentID, CourseID)`**.
    * However, **`StudentID -> StudentName`** (you only need `StudentID` to know the student's name; `CourseID` is completely unnecessary!).
    * Similarly, **`CourseID -> CourseName`** (depends only on `CourseID`, part of the key!).
    * Therefore, `StudentName` and `CourseName` have a **Partial Dependency** on the Primary Key `(StudentID, CourseID)`. *(As we will see in Module 4.3, eliminating Partial Dependencies gives **Second Normal Form [2NF]**!)*

```text
VISUALIZING FULL vs. PARTIAL DEPENDENCY:

         [Full Dependency -> Good!]
            +------------------+
            |                  v
  ( _StudentID_ , _CourseID_ , Grade , StudentName )
        |                                  ^
        +----------------------------------+
         [Partial Dependency -> Bad! Only depends on StudentID!]
```

---

#### Type 3: Transitive Functional Dependency *(Crucial for 3NF!)*

* **Definition:** A functional dependency $X \rightarrow Z$ is a **Transitive Functional Dependency** if $X$ determines $Z$ **indirectly through another non-prime attribute $Y$**:
  $$\text{If } X \rightarrow Y \;\text{ and }\; Y \rightarrow Z \quad (\text{where } Y \not\rightarrow X), \quad \text{then } X \rightarrow Z \text{ is a Transitive Dependency!}$$
* **Example:**
  Consider $\text{EMPLOYEE}(\underline{\text{ENO}}, \text{EName}, \text{DNO}, \text{DName})$:
  1. `ENO -> DNO` *(Employee ID determines which Department Number they work in)*.
  2. `DNO -> DName` *(Department Number determines the Department Name)*.
  3. Therefore, **`ENO -> DName` holds transitively via the non-key attribute `DNO`**!
  *(As we will see in Module 4.4, eliminating Transitive Dependencies gives **Third Normal Form [3NF]**!)*

```text
VISUALIZING TRANSITIVE DEPENDENCY:
  [ ENO (PK) ] --------> [ DNO (Non-Key) ] --------> [ DName (Non-Key) ]
        \                                                   ^
         \_________________________________________________/
                 Transitive Dependency: ENO -> DName
```

---

#### Type 4: Multivalued Dependency ($X \twoheadrightarrow Y$)
* Occurs when an entity has **two or more independent multivalued attributes** in the same table (e.g., a `Student` has multiple `Hobbies` AND multiple `Phone_Numbers` stored in the same table). Denoted as $X \twoheadrightarrow Y$.

## PART C: Armstrong’s Axioms (Inference Rules for FDs)

> ⭐ **DIRECT EXAM QUESTION:** Asked in **Nov 2025 End-Sem Exam Q7(a) (4 Marks)**: *"Given the FDs ... write the sequence of Armstrong's Axioms needed to arrive at the following FDs and justify."*

Given a set of known Functional Dependencies $F$ on a table, how can we mathematically deduce/prove **other new Functional Dependencies** that must also hold on that table?
**W. W. Armstrong (1974)** proposed a set of inference rules known as **Armstrong's Axioms**.

* Armstrong's Axioms are **Sound** (every FD derived using them is 100% true) and **Complete** (every true FD implied by $F$ can be derived using them).

📸 **[Click here to view Armstrong's Axioms Primary and Secondary Rules Chart on Google Images](https://www.google.com/search?q=Armstrong%27s+axioms+in+dbms+primary+and+secondary+rules+chart&udm=2)**

---

### 1. The 3 Primary Rules (Core Armstrong's Axioms)

| Rule Name | Formal Rule | Easy Meaning & Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Reflexivity Rule (IR1)** | **If $Y \subseteq X$, then $X \rightarrow Y$** | Any set of attributes always determines its own subsets *(generates Trivial FDs)*.<br>*Example:* $AB \rightarrow A$, or $QR \rightarrow Q$. |
| **2. Augmentation Rule (IR2)** | **If $X \rightarrow Y$, then $XZ \rightarrow YZ$** *(or $XZ \rightarrow Y$)* | You can attach the **same attribute(s) $Z$** to **both sides** (or just the LHS) of a valid FD.<br>*Example:* If $P \rightarrow R$, augmenting with $P$ gives $PP \rightarrow PR \implies \mathbf{P \rightarrow PR}$!<br>If $Q \rightarrow T$, augmenting with $R$ gives $\mathbf{QR \rightarrow TR}$. |
| **3. Transitivity Rule (IR3)** | **If $X \rightarrow Y$ and $Y \rightarrow Z$, then $X \rightarrow Z$** | Chain two FDs together when the RHS of the first matches the LHS of the second!<br>*Example:* If $P \rightarrow Q$ and $Q \rightarrow T$, then $\mathbf{P \rightarrow T}$! |

---

### 2. The 3 Secondary (Derived) Rules *(Massive Time-Savers in Proofs!)*

These 3 rules are derived from the 3 primary axioms above and are **always allowed** in exam proofs:

| Rule Name | Formal Rule | Easy Meaning & Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **4. Decomposition Rule (IR4)** *(Projectivity)* | **If $X \rightarrow YZ$, then $X \rightarrow Y$ and $X \rightarrow Z$** | You can **split the Right-Hand Side (RHS)** into separate FDs!<br>*Example:* If $A \rightarrow BC$, then $A \rightarrow B$ and $A \rightarrow C$.<br>*(⚠️ **WARNING:** You can **NEVER** split the Left-Hand Side! $AB \rightarrow C$ does **NOT** mean $A \rightarrow C$!)* |
| **5. Union / Additive Rule (IR5)** | **If $X \rightarrow Y$ and $X \rightarrow Z$, then $X \rightarrow YZ$** | If two FDs have the **exact same LHS ($X$)**, you can combine their RHSs together!<br>*Example:* If $QR \rightarrow S$ and $QR \rightarrow U$, then $\mathbf{QR \rightarrow SU}$! |
| **6. Pseudo-Transitivity Rule (IR6)** | **If $X \rightarrow Y$ and $WY \rightarrow Z$, then $WX \rightarrow Z$** | Since $X$ gives $Y$, you can **replace $Y$ with $X$** inside the LHS of $WY \rightarrow Z$!<br>*Example:* If $P \rightarrow Q$ and $QR \rightarrow S$, replace $Q$ with $P$ to get $\mathbf{PR \rightarrow S}$! |

### PYQ 1: Nov 2025 End-Sem Exam – Question 7(a) [4 Marks]

> **Question 7(a) [4 Marks]:**
> *"Given the FDs: $P \rightarrow Q$, $P \rightarrow R$, $QR \rightarrow S$, $Q \rightarrow T$, $QR \rightarrow U$, $PR \rightarrow U$, write the sequence of Armstrong's Axioms needed to arrive at the following FDs and justify. (4 marks)*
> *i. $P \rightarrow T$*
> *ii. $PR \rightarrow S$*
> *iii. $QR \rightarrow SU$"*

---

#### ✅ Model Exam Answer [4 / 4 Marks]:

**Given Set of Functional Dependencies:**
1. $P \rightarrow Q$
2. $P \rightarrow R$
3. $QR \rightarrow S$
4. $Q \rightarrow T$
5. $QR \rightarrow U$
6. $PR \rightarrow U$

---

#### i. Derivation of $P \rightarrow T$:
| Step | Functional Dependency | Justification (Armstrong's Axiom Used) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. | $P \rightarrow Q$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 2. | $Q \rightarrow T$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 3. | **$P \rightarrow T$** | By **Transitivity Rule (IR3)** on Step 1 ($P \rightarrow Q$) and Step 2 ($Q \rightarrow T$). |

---

#### ii. Derivation of $PR \rightarrow S$:
*(We write both the **Direct 3-Primary-Axiom Proof** and the **Pseudo-Transitivity Proof** so you get full marks under any grading key!)*

| Step | Functional Dependency | Justification (Armstrong's Axiom Used) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. | $P \rightarrow Q$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 2. | $PR \rightarrow QR$ | By **Augmentation Rule (IR2)**: Augmenting Step 1 ($P \rightarrow Q$) with $R$ on both sides. |
| 3. | $QR \rightarrow S$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 4. | **$PR \rightarrow S$** | By **Transitivity Rule (IR3)** on Step 2 ($PR \rightarrow QR$) and Step 3 ($QR \rightarrow S$). |

*(Alternative 1-Step Justification: Applying the **Pseudo-Transitivity Rule [IR6]** directly on $P \rightarrow Q$ and $QR \rightarrow S$ yields **$PR \rightarrow S$**).*

---

#### iii. Derivation of $QR \rightarrow SU$:
*(Again, we show both the **Union Rule** and the **Augmentation + Transitivity** proof!)*

**Method A (Using Union Rule – Fastest):**
| Step | Functional Dependency | Justification (Armstrong's Axiom Used) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. | $QR \rightarrow S$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 2. | $QR \rightarrow U$ | Given in the problem statement. |
| 3. | **$QR \rightarrow SU$** | By **Union / Additive Rule (IR5)** on Step 1 ($QR \rightarrow S$) and Step 2 ($QR \rightarrow U$). |

**Method B (Using Only Primary Axioms – Augmentation & Transitivity):**
1. $QR \rightarrow S$ *(Given)*
2. $QR \rightarrow QRS$ *(By **Augmentation Rule** on Step 1 with $QR$, since $QR \cdot QR = QR$)*
3. $QR \rightarrow U$ *(Given)*
4. $QRS \rightarrow SU$ *(By **Augmentation Rule** on Step 3 with $S$ on both sides)*
5. **$QR \rightarrow SU$** *(By **Transitivity Rule** on Step 2 [$QR \rightarrow QRS$] and Step 4 [$QRS \rightarrow SU$])*.

### PYQ 2: Nov 2025 End-Sem Exam – Question 7(b) [4 Marks]

> **Question 7(b) [4 Marks]:**
> *"Consider the following relation: `Course_Enrollment(StudentID, StudentName, CourseID, CourseName, Instructor)`. Suppose a new course "Artificial Intelligence" (`CourseID: C101`, `Instructor=Dr.Rao`) is introduced in the university, but no student has registered for it yet. Explain how this situation leads to an Insertion anomaly. (4 marks)"*

---

#### ✅ Model Exam Answer [4 / 4 Marks]:

**1. Identifying the Primary Key of `Course_Enrollment`:**
* In the given relation `Course_Enrollment(StudentID, StudentName, CourseID, CourseName, Instructor)`, a student can enroll in multiple courses and a course can have multiple students.
* Therefore, the **Primary Key** of this single combined relation is the composite key **`(StudentID, CourseID)`**.

**2. Why Introducing the New Course Causes an Insertion Anomaly:**
* An **Insertion Anomaly** occurs when certain valid attributes cannot be inserted into the database without the presence of other unrelated attributes.
* Here, the university wants to insert the details of a newly introduced course:
  * `CourseID = 'C101'`
  * `CourseName = 'Artificial Intelligence'`
  * `Instructor = 'Dr. Rao'`
* However, since **no student has registered for this course yet**, there are no values available for `StudentID` and `StudentName`. If we attempt to insert this row, the tuple would look like:
  $$(\textbf{NULL}, \;\text{NULL}, \;\text{'C101'}, \;\text{'Artificial Intelligence'}, \;\text{'Dr. Rao'})$$

**3. Violation of Entity Integrity Constraint:**
* According to the **Entity Integrity Constraint** of the relational model, **no attribute participating in a Primary Key can be `NULL`**.
* Because `StudentID` is a part of the composite Primary Key `(StudentID, CourseID)`, the DBMS will **reject** the insertion of `(NULL, NULL, 'C101', 'Artificial Intelligence', 'Dr. Rao')`.
* Consequently, **it is impossible to record the details of a new course in the database until at least one student enrolls in it**. This design flaw is known as an **Insertion Anomaly**.

**4. How to Resolve It:**
* Decompose the relation into separate normalized relations:
  * $\text{STUDENT}(\underline{\text{StudentID}}, \text{StudentName})$
  * $\text{COURSE}(\underline{\text{CourseID}}, \text{CourseName}, \text{Instructor})$ $\leftarrow$ *Now `'C101'` can be inserted here immediately!*
  * $\text{ENROLLMENT}(\underline{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}})$

### PYQ 3: Oct 2025 Internal Test – Question IV(a) [3.5 Marks]

> **Question IV(a) [3.5 Marks]:**
> *"Explain Functional dependency and its types with suitable examples."*

#### ✅ Model Exam Answer [3.5 / 3.5 Marks]:

**1. Definition of Functional Dependency (FD):**
* A **Functional Dependency**, denoted as **$X \rightarrow Y$**, is a constraint between two sets of attributes $X$ and $Y$ in a relation $R$.
* It states that for any two tuples $t_1$ and $t_2$ in $R$, **if $t_1[X] = t_2[X]$, then $t_1[Y] = t_2[Y]$**. That is, the value of the determinant $X$ uniquely determines the value of the dependent $Y$.
* *Example:* In a `STUDENT` relation, $\text{Roll\_No} \rightarrow \text{Name}$ holds because every unique `Roll_No` is associated with one specific `Name`.

**2. Types of Functional Dependencies:**

**i) Trivial and Non-Trivial Functional Dependency:**
* **Trivial FD:** An FD $X \rightarrow Y$ is trivial if $Y$ is a subset of $X$ ($Y \subseteq X$).
  * *Example:* $\{\text{Roll\_No}, \text{Name}\} \rightarrow \text{Name}$.
* **Non-Trivial FD:** An FD $X \rightarrow Y$ is non-trivial if $Y$ is not a subset of $X$ ($Y \not\subseteq X$).
  * *Example:* $\text{Roll\_No} \rightarrow \text{Branch}$.

**ii) Full Functional Dependency vs. Partial Functional Dependency:**
* **Full Functional Dependency:** In a relation with a composite key $X$, $X \rightarrow Y$ is a Full FD if $Y$ depends on the **entire** composite set $X$ and not on any proper subset of $X$.
  * *Example:* $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\} \rightarrow \text{Grade}$ (both `StudentID` and `CourseID` are required to determine `Grade`).
* **Partial Functional Dependency:** Occurs when a non-prime attribute $Y$ is functionally dependent on **only a part (proper subset)** of a composite candidate key $X$.
  * *Example:* In `ENROLL(StudentID, CourseID, StudentName)`, where `(StudentID, CourseID)` is the primary key, $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$ is a **Partial Dependency**.

**iii) Transitive Functional Dependency:**
* Occurs when an attribute $X$ determines $Z$ indirectly through another non-prime attribute $Y$ (i.e., $X \rightarrow Y$ and $Y \rightarrow Z$, where $Y \not\rightarrow X$, which implies $X \rightarrow Z$).
* *Example:* In `EMPLOYEE(ENO, EName, DNO, DName)`, $\text{ENO} \rightarrow \text{DNO}$ and $\text{DNO} \rightarrow \text{DName}$, so $\text{ENO} \rightarrow \text{DName}$ is a **Transitive Dependency**.

### Quick 30-Second Recap of 4.1
* **3 Anomalies in Bad Tables:**
  * **Insertion Anomaly:** Can't insert a new Course without a Student because `StudentID` is part of the PK (can't be `NULL`).
  * **Deletion Anomaly:** Deleting the last Student in a Course accidentally deletes the Course details too.
  * **Update Anomaly:** Updating a duplicated value (like Instructor name) across 60 rows risks inconsistency if 1 row is missed.
* **Key FD Types:**
  * **Partial FD:** Non-key attribute depends on **part** of a composite PK *(Violates 2NF)*.
  * **Transitive FD:** Non-key attribute depends on another **non-key attribute** ($X \rightarrow Y \rightarrow Z$) *(Violates 3NF)*.
* **Armstrong's Axioms:** Reflexivity ($Y \subseteq X \implies X \rightarrow Y$), Augmentation ($X \rightarrow Y \implies XZ \rightarrow YZ$), Transitivity ($X \rightarrow Y, Y \rightarrow Z \implies X \rightarrow Z$), Union ($X \rightarrow Y, X \rightarrow Z \implies X \rightarrow YZ$), Decomposition ($X \rightarrow YZ \implies X \rightarrow Y, X \rightarrow Z$).
