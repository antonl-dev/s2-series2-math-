# 4.2 Characteristics of Municipal Solid Waste & CPHEEO Guidelines

## 1. Sources of Solid Waste
Solid waste is generated from various sectors of society, categorized as follows:
1.  **Municipal Solid Waste (MSW):** Includes waste from households, commercial sectors (hotels/restaurants), institutional buildings (schools/offices), and municipal services (street sweepings, landscaping, and construction debris).
2.  **Industrial Waste:** Arises from industrial activities (e.g., pulp, paper, and chemical plants). Can be toxic or recyclable (metals/plastics).
3.  **Agricultural Waste:** Spoiled food grains and animal waste from farms and dairies.
4.  **Plastic Waste:** A major part of the "use and throw" lifestyle; India generates approx. 56 lakh tonnes annually.
5.  **Construction and Demolition (C&D) Waste:** Steel, concrete, wood, and glass from building sites.
6.  **E-Waste:** Electronic gadgets like computers and mobile phones containing hazardous circuitry.

## 2. Characteristics of Municipal Solid Waste
Understanding waste characteristics is critical for the design of disposal facilities and the selection of equipment.

### I. Physical Characteristics
*   **Density:** Mass per unit volume ($kg/m^3$). Compacted waste occupies 75% less volume than un-compacted waste. High density improves transportation efficiency.
*   **Moisture Content:** The ratio of water weight to total wet weight. Typical range is 20% to 40%. High moisture increases collection costs and affects the feasibility of incineration.
*   **Particle Size:** Important for the design of mechanical separators (trammel screens) and shredders used in material recovery.
*   **Field Capacity:** The total amount of moisture waste can retain against gravity. Excess water forms **Leachate**, a major landfill pollutant.
*   **Permeability:** Governs the movement of liquids and gases within a landfill.

### II. Chemical Characteristics
*   **Volatile Solids:** Indicates the weight loss upon ignition at 950°C.
*   **Ash Content:** The residue remaining after burning.
*   **CHNSO Content:** Measurement of Carbon, Hydrogen, Nitrogen, Sulphur, and Oxygen using elemental analyzers. 
*   **Calorific Value:** The energy content (measured in $kJ/kg$ via a bomb calorimeter). High calorific value is required for incineration.
*   **Ultimate Analysis:** Determines the proportion of elements to define the proper mix for biological conversion ($C/N$ ratios).
*   **Proximate Analysis:** Aims to determine moisture, volatile matter, ash, and fixed carbon to evaluate combustion properties.

### III. Biological Characteristics
*   Determines the **biodegradability** of the organic fraction.
*   Organic waste can be converted biologically into gases and inert solids. 
*   The putrescible nature of these materials is directly related to the generation of flies and odors.

## 3. Waste Compositional Analysis
Waste is separated and weighed to be represented as a fraction of the total. 
*   **Categories:** Dry, Wet, Paper, Glass, Metal, and Household Hazardous.
*   **Variation Factors:** Composition depends on **Location** (urban vs. rural), **Season**, and the local **Economy**.

---

## 4. CPHEEO Guidelines for Solid Waste Management (Overview)
The Central Public Health and Environmental Engineering Organisation (CPHEEO) provides the technical manual for SWM in India.

### Key Implementation Steps:
1.  **Segregation at Source:** Dividing waste into biodegradable (wet) and non-biodegradable (dry) categories at the household level.
2.  **Door-to-Door Collection:** Systematic gathering of waste from households rather than community bins.
3.  **Intermediate Storage:** Use of covered storage centers and secondary collection points.
4.  **Transportation:** Use of closed vehicles to prevent littering and odor during transit to processing sites.
5.  **Waste Processing:** Prioritizing composting (for wet waste) and recycling (for dry waste) before landfilling.
6.  **Scientific Landfilling:** Only non-recyclable and inert waste should be sent to engineered sanitary landfills.
