# 4.7 Biomedical Waste and its Management

## 1. Introduction to Biomedical Waste
**Biomedical waste** is defined as any waste generated during the diagnosis, treatment, or immunization of human beings or animals, or in related research activities.

*   **Risks:** If scattered around hospitals, it attracts flies, rodents, and stray animals, playing a major role in spreading communicable diseases like plague and rabies.
*   **Regulations:** In India, **Biomedical Waste Management Rules (1998)** were notified under the Environment Protection Act (EPA), 1986, to ensure safe handling.

---

## 2. Types of Biomedical Waste
1.  **Human Anatomical Waste:** Human tissues, organs, and body parts.
2.  **Animal Waste:** Animal tissues, carcasses, and fluids used in research or from veterinary colleges.
3.  **Microbiology & Biotechnology Waste:** Laboratory cultures, stocks of live microorganisms, and toxins.
4.  **Waste Sharps:** Needles, syringes, scalpels, blades, and broken glass that may cause punctures/cuts.
5.  **Discarded Medicines:** Outdated or contaminated drugs (including cytotoxic drugs).
6.  **Soiled Waste:** Items contaminated with blood or body fluids (cotton, dressings, plaster casts).
7.  **Solid Waste:** Disposable items like tubing, catheters, and intravenous (IV) sets.
8.  **Liquid Waste:** Generated from laboratory washing, cleaning, and disinfecting activities.
9.  **Incineration Ash:** Ash resulting from the incineration of any biomedical waste.
10. **Chemical Waste:** Chemicals used in biological production or disinfection.

---

## 3. Management and Treatment Technologies
Biomedical waste must be treated to render it non-infectious before final disposal.

*   **Incineration:** A high-temperature thermal process that burns waste under controlled conditions, converting it into inert ash and gases.
*   **Autoclaving:** A physical method of sterilization using steam. An autoclave typically runs at **121°C for at least 30 minutes** to kill bacteria, viruses, and spores.
*   **Microwave Irradiation:** Using high-frequency waves (2450 MHz) to destroy the majority of microorganisms within the waste.
*   **Chemical Disinfection:** Using 1% hypochlorite solution or other chemicals to kill pathogens.

---

## 4. Colour Coding for Segregation
Proper segregation at the source is the most critical step in biomedical waste management.

| Colour | Type of Waste | Treatment/Disposal Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Yellow** | Anatomical waste, soiled waste, dressings, linen. | Incineration or Deep Burial. |
| **Red** | Tubing, catheters, IV sets, gloves (plastic/rubber). | Autoclaving / Microwaving followed by Shredding. |
| **Blue/White** | Waste sharps (Needles, scalpels, blades). | Autoclaving / Chemical treatment & Shredding. |
| **Black** | Discarded medicines, cytotoxic drugs, incineration ash. | Disposal in a Secured Landfill. |

## 5. Benefits of Proper Segregation
*   Minimizes the total amount of potentially hazardous waste.
*   Facilitates proper packing and labeling.
*   Reduces occupational health and safety risks for healthcare workers and rag pickers.
*   Improves infection control within the hospital.
*   Establishes a uniform waste management practice across the healthcare industry.
