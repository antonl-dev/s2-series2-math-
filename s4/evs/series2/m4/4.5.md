# 4.5 Hazardous and E-Waste Identification and Management

## 1. Identification of Hazardous Waste
**Hazardous wastes** are materials that pose a substantial danger, either immediately or over time, to human beings, animals, plants, and the environment.

### Categories for Identification:
1.  **Listed Waste (US-EPA Standard):**
    *   **F-List:** Wastes from non-specific sources (e.g., spent solvents like methylene chloride used for degreasing).
    *   **K-List:** Wastes from specific industrial sources (e.g., organic chemical manufacturing, wood preservation).
    *   **P-List and U-List:** Discarded commercial chemical products, unused or expired (P-list is acutely toxic).
2.  **Characterized Waste (The "D" Wastes):**
    *   **Ignitability (D001):** Wastes that can readily catch fire and sustain combustion (Flash point < 60°C).
    *   **Corrosivity (D002):** Highly acidic or alkaline wastes (pH ≤ 2 or pH ≥ 12.5) that dissolve metal or flesh.
    *   **Reactivity (D003):** Wastes that are unstable, react violently with water, or can explode.
    *   **Toxicity (D004):** Determined using the **Toxicity Characteristic Leaching Procedure (TCLP)**. If a leached contaminant exceeds specific limits (e.g., Lead > 5.0 mg/L), it is hazardous.

## 2. E-Waste (Electronic Waste)
E-waste refers to surplus, broken, or obsolete electronic devices (computers, mobile phones, televisions, ACs).

*   **Hazard:** Contains circuits and electrical components made of toxic materials (lead, mercury, cadmium) that are released into the environment if not disposed of properly.
*   **Ranking:** India ranks among the **top 10 countries** globally in solid waste and e-waste generation.

### Steps for E-waste Management:
1.  **Point of Generation:** Management should start where the waste is created.
2.  **3R Technique:** Prioritize reducing the use of electronic gadgets and reusing parts.
3.  **Sustainable Design:** Designing electronics to be easily disassembled and recycled.
4.  **Identification:** Determining whether specific components or the entire item is hazardous.

---

## 3. Hazardous Waste Management Techniques

### I. Treatment Methods
Hazardous waste must be treated before final disposal to reduce its hazard level:
*   **Chemical Treatment:** Includes ion exchange, precipitation, oxidation-reduction, and **neutralization** of acids/bases.
*   **Thermal Treatment:** **High-temperature incineration** can detoxify and destroy organic hazardous wastes.
*   **Physical/Chemical Stabilization:** Processes used to "solidify" waste or reduce the mobility of toxic chemicals (e.g., pH adjustment to prevent lead leaching).

### II. Final Disposal
*   **Sanitary Landfills (Double Liner):** Wastes are buried in highly engineered pits with double liners and leachate collection to protect groundwater.
*   **Surface Impoundments (Lagoons):** Temporary storage for hazardous liquids in lined pits.
*   **Deep Well Injection:** Liquid waste is injected into deep, contained geological formations.

## 4. Obligations for Society

1.  **Producer Responsibility:** Producers must take responsibility for product design and life-cycle management (**Extended Producer Responsibility - EPR**).
2.  **Political Responsibility:** Governments must bridge the gap by enforcing laws that protect human health and the environment.
3.  **Community Responsibility:** Individuals must take responsibility for sustainable consumption and proper waste segregation.

### Generator Types (By Volume):
*   **Large Quantity Generators (LQG):** > 1000 kg hazardous waste per month.
*   **Small Quantity Generators (SQG):** 100 kg to 1000 kg per month.
*   **Conditionally Exempt SQG:** < 100 kg per month.

## 5. Case Study: Love Canal (Historical Problem)
*   **Incident:** In the 1940s-50s, Hooker Chemical Company buried 22,000 tons of toxic waste in a canal in Niagara Falls, NY.
*   **Outcome:** The land was sold and built upon (schools/homes). Heavy rain caused the chemicals to leak, resulting in a national health emergency in 1978. It highlighted the extreme dangers of improper hazardous waste disposal.
