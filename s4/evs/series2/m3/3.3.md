# 3.3 Water Quality Index & Overview of Water Treatment Plant

## 1. Water Quality Index (WQI)
The **Water Quality Index (WQI)** is a tool used to simplify complex water quality data into a single, easy-to-understand number (an index number).

*   **Definition:** It is a rating that reflects the composite influence of various different water quality parameters (physical, chemical, and biological) taken into consideration for calculation.
*   **Purpose:**
    *   It represents the overall quality of water for any intended use (most commonly for human consumption).
    *   It is one of the most effective ways to communicate water quality trends to the general public, policy makers, and water managers.
*   **Formulation:** The relative importance (weightage) of various parameters depends on the intended use of the water. If the WQI is high, it generally indicates poor water quality, whereas a lower index number often represents cleaner water (depending on the specific WQI scale used).

## 2. Characteristics of Sewage (Wastewater)
Before treatment, wastewater is analyzed for its physical and chemical properties:

### Physical Characteristics
*   **Colour:** Fresh sewage is light brown; decomposing sewage is dark brown; septic sewage is black.
*   **Odour:** Fresh sewage has no odour; septic sewage has an offensive odour (due to $H_2S$ and other gases).
*   **Temperature:** Slightly higher than the temperature of the surrounding water supply.
*   **Solids:** Sewage typically consists of **99.9% water and 0.1% solids** (suspended, dissolved, or colloidal).

### Chemical Characteristics
*   **pH:** Fresh sewage is alkaline; putrefied (septic) sewage is acidic.
*   **Nitrogen:** Indicates the presence of organic matter.
    *   *Free Ammonia:* Indicates stale or old sewage.
    *   *Nitrites/Nitrates:* Indicate partially or fully decomposed organic matter.
*   **Fats, Oil, and Grease:** Hinder biological oxidation processes.
*   **Sulphides:** Produced by anaerobic decomposition; $H_2S$ is corrosive to sewer pipes (known as **Crown Corrosion**).

## 3. Oxygen Demand
To understand how much pollution is in water, we measure how much oxygen is needed to clean it:

1.  **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD):** The amount of dissolved oxygen required by microorganisms to decompose organic matter under aerobic conditions.
    *   **Ultimate Carbonaceous BOD:** The total oxygen needed to decompose all biodegradable carbon-based materials.
    *   **Total BOD:** Carbonaceous BOD + Nitrogenous oxygen demand (NBOD).
2.  **Chemical Oxygen Demand (COD):** The amount of oxygen required to chemically oxidize both biodegradable and non-biodegradable organic matter.
3.  **Relative Stability:** The ratio of oxygen available in sewage to the oxygen required to satisfy the first stage of BOD. It helps predict when aerobic decomposition ends and anaerobic decomposition starts.

## 4. Overview of Water Treatment Plant (WTP) / Sewage Treatment Plant (STP)

Treatment is divided into several stages to remove different types of contaminants.

### I. Preliminary Treatment
The goal is to remove large floating objects and heavy inorganic solids.
*   **Screens:** To remove large floating objects like rags, wood, and plastics.
*   **Grit Chamber:** A long tank that slows the flow so heavy solids like sand, grit, and stones can settle while organic matter stays in suspension.

### II. Primary Treatment
*   **Primary Sedimentation Tank (PST):** Wastewater sits in a large tank. Suspended organic solids settle to the bottom as "primary sludge," and floating materials (oil/grease) are skimmed off the top.

### III. Secondary Treatment (Biological Treatment)
The goal is to remove dissolved and colloidal organic matter using microbes.
*   **Biological Reactors:** Microorganisms consume organic matter as food.
*   **Secondary Settling Tank (SST):** Microbes (biomass) are allowed to settle out. A portion of this sludge is recycled back to the reactor.

### IV. Tertiary Treatment (Advanced Treatment)
The final "polishing" stage to make water safe for reuse or discharge into sensitive areas.
*   **Coagulation & Flocculation:** Adding chemicals to clump tiny particles together.
*   **Filtration:** Using sand or membranes to remove remaining solids.
*   **Disinfection:** Using Chlorine, Ozone, or UV radiation to kill pathogenic microorganisms (bacteria/viruses).

## 5. Sludge Management
Sludge is the solid byproduct of treatment. It must be treated before disposal:
*   **Anaerobic Digester:** Sludge is decomposed by bacteria without oxygen, producing **Biogas** ($CH_4$ and $CO_2$).
*   **Dewatering:** Methods like centrifugation, filter presses, or sludge drying beds are used to reduce volume before landfilling or use as manure.
