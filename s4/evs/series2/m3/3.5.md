# 3.5 Wastewater Sources and Quality

## 1. Introduction to Wastewater
Wastewater is used water that has been affected by domestic, industrial, or commercial use. It is primarily characterized by the source from which it originates.

### Domestic Wastewater Categories
In a household setting, wastewater is divided based on its level of contamination:
1.  **Sullage / Grey water:** Wastewater from household sinks, showers, and baths. It does **not** contain human excreta. It is relatively easier to treat and reuse.
2.  **Black water:** Wastewater containing human waste (excreta and urine) from toilets. It contains high levels of pathogens and organic matter.
3.  **Combined Sewage:** A mixture of domestic sewage, industrial waste, and sometimes storm water.

---

## 2. Key Sources of Wastewater

### I. Household (Domestic) Sources
*   **Kitchen:** High in organic matter, oils, and detergents.
*   **Bathroom (Shower/Wash):** Contains soaps, hair, and grey water.
*   **Toilets:** The primary source of "Black water" and pathogens.
*   **Laundry:** Contains high levels of phosphates and synthetic detergents.

### II. Industrial Sources
The nature and composition of industrial wastewater vary widely depending on the industry (e.g., textiles, tanneries, paper mills).
*   **Cooling Systems:** Usually thermal pollution (high-temperature water).
*   **Process Water:** Contains specific toxic chemicals, heavy metals, and high acids or alkalis.

### III. Agricultural Sources
*   **Run-off:** Rich in Nitrogen, Phosphates, and Pesticides.
*   **Animal Husbandry:** High concentrations of organic matter and ammonia.

### IV. Storm Water
*   Rainwater that washes off streets, roofs, and parking lots. It often picks up oils, heavy metals, and debris, becoming a source of non-point pollution.

## 3. Wastewater Terminology
*   **Sewage:** A mixture of water and waste (domestic and industrial).
*   **Sewerage System:** The entire infrastructure (pipes, pumps, etc.) used to collect and transport sewage.
*   **Septage:** The settled solids (sludge) and liquid removed from a septic tank.
*   **Night Soil:** A historical term for human excreta collected without a water-carriage system.

---

## 4. Wastewater Quality (Characteristics)

Wastewater quality is determined by analyzing three sets of characteristics:

### I. Physical Quality
*   **Solids:** Total solids include suspended, dissolved, and colloidal matter. (Sewage is 99.9% water and 0.1% solids).
*   **Turbidity:** Measurement of water clarity; depends on the quantity of suspended solids.
*   **Odour:** Fresh sewage has little odour; septic sewage smells like rotten eggs ($H_2S$).
*   **Temperature:** Usually higher than the water supply, which speeds up biological activity but lowers dissolved oxygen.

### II. Chemical Quality
*   **pH:** Used to determine the acidity or alkalinity.
*   **Nitrogen Forms:**
    *   *Ammonia Nitrogen:* Indicates fresh/recent pollution.
    *   *Nitrites/Nitrates:* Indicate older, oxidized pollution.
*   **Sulphates/Sulphides:** Anaerobic bacteria convert sulphates to sulphides ($H_2S$), which causes **Crown Corrosion** in concrete pipes.
*   **Chlorides:** High levels indicate possible industrial waste or saltwater intrusion.

### III. Biological Quality
*   **Pathogens:** Bacteria, viruses, and protozoa that cause diseases (e.g., *E. Coli*).
*   **Microorganisms:** Divided into those that help degrade organic matter and those that cause disease.

---

## 5. Population Equivalent (P.E.)
Population Equivalent is a parameter used to express the "strength" of industrial wastewater in terms of domestic sewage. It helps in designing the capacity of treatment plants.

**Formula:**
$$P_e = \frac{\text{Total } BOD_5 \text{ of Industrial Wastewater (Kg/day)}}{\text{Average } BOD_5 \text{ value per capita per day}}$$
