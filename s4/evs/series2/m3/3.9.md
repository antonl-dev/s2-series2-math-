# 3.9 Natural Methods of Wastewater Treatment

## 1. Introduction
Natural wastewater treatment systems utilize biological, physical, and chemical processes that occur in the environment (soil, plants, and microorganisms) rather than relying solely on mechanical and chemical energy. These methods are cost-effective, energy-efficient, and ideal for decentralized applications.

---

## 2. Constructed Wetlands
Constructed wetlands are engineered systems designed to mimic natural wetlands. They use wetland vegetation, soils, and their associated microbial assemblages to treat wastewater.

### Types of Constructed Wetlands:
1.  **Surface Flow Wetlands:** Wastewater flows over the soil surface through a thick growth of aquatic plants.
2.  **Subsurface Flow Wetlands:**
    *   **Horizontal Flow:** Wastewater stays below the surface, flowing through a porous gravel/sand bed.
    *   **Vertical Flow:** Wastewater is applied to the surface and drains down through the filter media.

### Components:
*   **Inlet Structure:** Distributes the inflow evenly.
*   **Substrate/Media:** Gravel, sand, or soil that supports plants and filters solids.
*   **Vegetation:** Macrophytes (like reeds and cattails) that provide surface area for microbial growth and transfer oxygen to the roots.
*   **Rhizosphere (Root Zone):** The area where most biological activity occurs.

## 3. Lagoons (Waste Stabilization Ponds)
Lagoons are large, shallow earthen basins where wastewater is treated through the natural interaction of sunlight, bacteria, and algae.

### Types of Lagoons:
1.  **Aerated Lagoons:** Use mechanical aerators to introduce oxygen, promoting rapid aerobic decomposition.
2.  **Anaerobic Lagoons:** Function without oxygen; used primarily for high-strength industrial or animal waste.
3.  **Facultative Lagoons:** Have an aerobic upper layer (due to algae) and an anaerobic bottom layer.
4.  **Maturation Ponds:** Final stage ponds used mainly to kill pathogens via UV radiation from sunlight.

### Advantages and Disadvantages:
*   **Advantages:** Highly resistant to shock loads, low energy consumption, and high BOD/pathogen reduction.
*   **Disadvantages:** Requires a very large land area and may produce odors if not maintained.

---

## 4. On-Site Treatment Systems
For individual houses or small clusters, the following natural methods are common:

*   **Septic Tank:** A sedimentation tank where organic solids settle and undergo anaerobic digestion.
*   **Anaerobic Baffled Reactor (ABR):** An improved septic tank with multiple compartments that force water through sludge, increasing treatment efficiency.
*   **Anaerobic Filter:** A tank filled with filter media where bacteria grow and treat the water as it passes through.

## 5. PHYTORID Technology
Developed by **CSIR-NEERI**, Phytorid is a patented technology that uses a subsurface flow constructed wetland system.
*   It utilizes specific plants and a gravel/sand media.
*   It works on the principle of combined physical, chemical, and biological processes.
*   It is widely used in India for treating sewage in parks, housing societies, and industrial campuses.

---

## 6. Treatment Process Summary
1.  **Pre-treatment:** Screening and grit removal.
2.  **Primary Treatment:** Sedimentation (settling of solids).
3.  **Secondary Treatment:** Natural biological degradation in wetlands or lagoons.
4.  **Tertiary/Post-treatment:** Final polishing in maturation ponds or sand filters to reach reuse standards.
