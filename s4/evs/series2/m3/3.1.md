# 3.1 Sources and Availability of Freshwater & Water Conservation Strategies

## 1. Introduction to Water
A single water molecule is composed of two atoms of hydrogen attached to one atom of oxygen ($H_2O$) by covalent bonds. 
* About **71 percent** of the Earth's surface is water-covered.
* Water exists in the air as water vapor, in rivers and lakes, in icecaps and glaciers, in the ground as soil moisture and in aquifers, and even in living organisms.

### Where is Earth's Water?
* **97%** of the water on Earth is **salt water** (oceans and seas).
* **Only 3% is fresh water**, which is the water required for virtually all human uses (agricultural, industrial, household, recreational, and environmental activities).
    * Of this 3% fresh water, **slightly over two-thirds (68.7%)** is frozen in glaciers and polar ice caps.
    * **30.1%** is found underground as groundwater.
    * Only about **1.2%** is easily accessible surface water (rivers, lakes, swamps) and other freshwater.

## 2. Sources of Fresh Water

Water resources are sources of water that are useful or potentially useful to humans. There are 5 primary sources of fresh water:

### I. Surface Water
Surface water is water found in a river, lake, or fresh water wetland. 
* It is naturally replenished by **precipitation** (rain/snow).
* It is naturally lost through discharge to the oceans, evaporation, evapotranspiration, and groundwater recharge.

### II. Under River Flow (Hyporheic Zone)
Under river flow is the water that flows through the rocks and sediments *below* a river. 
* This area is called the **hyporheic zone**.
* It can be a significant part of a river's flow, especially in large valleys.
* The hyporheic zone exchanges water with aquifers (large underground water-storing areas).

[click here udm2 link to gogoel images](https://www.google.com/search?tbm=isch&q=hyporheic+zone+diagram)

### III. Groundwater
Groundwater is fresh water located in the subsurface pore spaces of soil and rocks.
* It is water flowing within aquifers below the water table.
* A distinction is often made between shallow groundwater (closely associated with surface water) and deep groundwater in an aquifer (sometimes called "fossil water").

### IV. Frozen Water
Frozen water, primarily in the form of glaciers and ice caps, holds about 68% of the Earth's fresh water.
* In regions like the Himalayas and the Andes, glacial meltwater sustains agriculture and drinking supplies for millions.
* **Impact of Climate Change:** Accelerated ice melt temporarily becomes a significant source of freshwater for rivers, but excessive melting leads to long-term water scarcity as glaciers shrink.

### V. Desalination
Desalination is an artificial process by which saline water (generally sea water) is converted to fresh water.
* The most common processes are **distillation** and **reverse osmosis**.
* It is currently very expensive compared to alternative sources, so it satisfies only a very small fraction of total human water use globally.

## 3. Water Conservation
With the continuous increase in population, both the quality and quantity of water have declined. **Water conservation** is the process of saving water for future utilization.

### Key Water Conservation Strategies

1. **Reducing Evaporation Loss:** 
   * Commonly an issue in humid regions.
   * Can be reduced by placing horizontal barriers (like asphalt) below the soil surface to increase water availability and crop yield.
2. **Reducing Irrigation Loss:**
   * Growing hybrid crop varieties that require less water.
   * Irrigating fields in the early morning or late evening to minimize sun evaporation.
   * Using highly efficient methods like **drip irrigation** or **sprinkler irrigation**.
3. **Re-use of Water:**
   * Treated wastewater can be used for ferti-irrigation.
   * **Greywater** (from sinks, showers) can be reused for washing cars, watering gardens, etc.
   * Water used for washing fruits and vegetables can be diverted to water household plants.
4. **Preventing Wastage of Water:**
   * Closing taps when not in active use.
   * Promptly repairing any leakages in pipes.
   * Using small capacity, low-flow taps.
5. **Decrease Run-off Loss:**
   * Run-off can be reduced by allowing water more time to infiltrate into the soil.
   * This is achieved through agricultural techniques like **contour cultivation** or **terrace farming**.
6. **Avoid Discharge of Sewage:**
   * Preventing the discharge of untreated sewage into natural water resources to maintain existing freshwater quality.

## 4. Methods of Water Conservation

### A. Rainwater Harvesting
Rainwater harvesting is a technique of collecting and storing rainwater in natural reservoirs or tanks for future utilization. 

**Objectives:**
* To meet the increasing demands for water.
* To reduce groundwater contamination and minimize water crises.
* To reduce soil erosion and flooding.
* To raise the water table by recharging groundwater.

**Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting:** 
A low-cost and highly effective method for urban buildings where rainwater from the roof is collected, filtered, and stored or directed into a recharge pit.

### B. Watershed Management
A **Watershed** is a land area from which water drains under the influence of gravity into a common stream, lake, reservoir, or other body of surface water.
**Watershed management** refers to the management of rainfall and the resultant runoff.

**Factors affecting watersheds negatively:** Overgrazing, Deforestation, Mining, Construction activities, and Droughty climates.

**Objectives of Watershed Management:**
* To minimize the risks of floods, droughts, and landslides.
* To prevent soil erosion by runoff.
* To raise the groundwater level.
* To generate employment opportunities and develop rural areas.

**Watershed Management Techniques:**
1. **Trenches:** Dug at equal intervals to trap water and improve groundwater storage.
2. **Earthen Dams:** Constructed in the catchment area to check the rapid water run-off.
3. **Farm Ponds:** Built to improve the surface water storage capacity of the catchment area.
4. **Dykes:** Built along nullahs (watercourses) to raise the local water table.
