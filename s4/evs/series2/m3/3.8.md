# 3.8 Decentralized Wastewater Treatment (DWWM)

## 1. Definition and Concept
**Decentralized Wastewater Management (DWWM)** refers to the collection, treatment, and disposal/reuse of wastewater at or near the point of generation.
*   Unlike centralized systems that transport waste over long distances to one massive plant, DWWM involves smaller facilities serving individual homes, apartment blocks, or small isolated communities.
*   In these systems, both solid and liquid fractions are often utilized near their origin.

## 2. When to Use Decentralized Systems
DWWM is highly suitable in the following situations:
*   **Low Population Density:** Where houses are scattered over a large area.
*   **Topography Issues:** In areas with low elevations or obstructions (like railway tracks) that make laying large sewer lines difficult.
*   **Water Scarcity:** Where there is a high possibility and need for localized reuse of treated wastewater.
*   **Cost Constraints:** When the cost of extending a centralized system to a specific locality is exorbitant.
*   **New Developments:** For newly developed clusters like industrial parks or rural townships.

---

## 3. Advantages and Disadvantages

### Advantages
1.  **Small Flows:** Environmental damage from mishaps is small because the volume at any point is low.
2.  **Flexible Routing:** Smaller pipes are installed at shallower depths, causing less disturbance during construction.
3.  **Easier Expansion:** New treatment units can be added as the community grows without overloading existing centers.
4.  **Local Reuse:** Treated water can be used directly for toilet flushing, landscape irrigation, or cooling towers.
5.  **Lower Investment:** Significant savings on the cost of the sewer collection network (which usually accounts for 80% of total system costs).

### Disadvantages
1.  **Operation & Maintenance (O&M):** Local residents or small associations must bear the responsibility for maintenance.
2.  **Standardization:** It is difficult to standardize designs as they must vary based on local geography and climate.
3.  **Site Availability:** Finding a site for even a small treatment unit can be difficult in densely built-up urban sections.

---

## 4. Components of a DWWM System
Similar to centralized systems, a decentralized system consists of three main components:

### I. Collection System
*   **Small Bore Sewer System:** Uses small diameter pipes to carry settled sewage (after primary treatment in a septic tank).
*   **Shallow Sewer System:** Pipes laid at very shallow depths to reduce excavation costs.
*   **Twin Drain System:** Separates septic tank effluent/greywater from storm water.

### II. Treatment System
Various technologies can be employed depending on the scale:
*   **Septic Tanks:** Basic onsite primary treatment.
*   **Anaerobic Baffled Reactors (ABR):** A series of chambers that force wastewater through a "sludge blanket" for high treatment efficiency.
*   **Moving Bed Bio-film Reactor (MBBR):** Uses plastic carriers to provide a high surface area for microbial growth.

### III. Reuse / Disposal System
*   Treated water is typically used for horticulture, agriculture, or groundwater recharge.

## 5. Planning and Site Selection
The selection of sites for decentralized units depends on:
*   **Population Density** and land availability.
*   **Topography** (gravitational flow).
*   **Reuse Potential** of the treated water.
*   **Hydrogeology** (soil absorption capacity).
