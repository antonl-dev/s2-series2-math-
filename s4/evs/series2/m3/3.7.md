# 3.7 Applicable Wastewater Discharge Standards and Typical Flow Schemes for STP

## 1. General Standards for Discharge (Schedule VI)
In India, the Environment (Protection) Rules, 1986, prescribe limits for environmental pollutants in effluents. These standards vary based on where the treated water is being discharged.

### Key Parameter Limits (General Effluents)
| S.No | Parameter | Inland Surface Water | Public Sewers | Land for Irrigation | Marine Coastal Areas |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | **Suspended Solids (mg/l)** | 100 | 600 | 200 | 100 |
| 2 | **pH Value** | 5.5 to 9.0 | 5.5 to 9.0 | 5.5 to 9.0 | 5.5 to 9.0 |
| 3 | **Oil and Grease (mg/l)** | 10 | 20 | 10 | 20 |
| 4 | **BOD (3 days at 27°C)** | 30 | 350 | 100 | 100 |
| 5 | **COD (mg/l)** | 250 | -- | -- | 250 |
| 6 | **Arsenic (mg/l)** | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.2 |
| 7 | **Lead (mg/l)** | 0.1 | 1.0 | -- | 2.0 |
| 8 | **Cadmium (mg/l)** | 2.0 | 1.0 | -- | 2.0 |

## 2. Waste Water Generation Standards (Industry Specific)
Different industries have specific "quantum" limits based on their production capacity:
*   **Integrated Iron & Steel:** $16\, m^3/tonne$ of finished steel.
*   **Sugar:** $0.4\, m^3/tonne$ of cane crushed.
*   **Pulp & Paper (Large):** $175\, m^3/tonne$ of paper produced.
*   **Dairy:** $3\, m^3/KL$ of milk.
*   **Tanneries:** $28\, m^3/tonne$ of raw hide.

## 3. Typical Flow Scheme for Sewage Treatment Plant (STP)
A conventional sewage treatment plant involves a series of units designed to remove pollutants progressively.

### I. Preliminary Treatment (Physical)
*   **Bar Screens:** Traps large floating debris (plastics, rags, wood).
*   **Grit Chamber:** Settles heavy inorganic solids (sand, gravel) by reducing flow velocity.

### II. Primary Treatment (Physical/Chemical)
*   **Primary Sedimentation Tank (PST):** Heavy organic solids settle to the bottom as primary sludge. Scum (oils/fats) is removed from the surface.

### III. Secondary Treatment (Biological)
This is the heart of the plant where dissolved organic matter is removed by microorganisms.
*   **Biological Reactor:** Could be an **Activated Sludge Process (ASP)**, **Moving Bed Bio-film Reactor (MBBR)**, or **Sequential Batch Reactor (SBR)**.
*   **Secondary Settling Tank (SST):** Microbes (biomass) settle out. Some are recycled back to the reactor (**RAS - Return Activated Sludge**), and the rest is wasted.

### IV. Tertiary Treatment (Polishing)
*   **Rapid Sand Filtration:** Removes very fine suspended particles.
*   **Disinfection:** Chlorination, UV, or Ozonation to kill harmful pathogens before final discharge or reuse.

## 4. Sludge Treatment Scheme
The solids collected from PST and SST must be stabilized:
1.  **Thickening:** Reducing water content.
2.  **Anaerobic Digestion:** Breaking down organic matter in the absence of oxygen to produce **Biogas ($CH_4$)**.
3.  **Drying/Dewatering:** Using sludge drying beds or mechanical presses.
4.  **Disposal:** Used as soil conditioner/manure or disposed of in landfills.

---

## 5. Load-Based Standards (Part-C)
For industries like Petroleum Oil Refineries, limits are set in terms of quantity per 1,000 tonnes of crude processed:
*   **BOD:** $6.0\, kg$
*   **Oil & Grease:** $2.0\, kg$
*   **Suspended Solids:** $8.0\, kg$
