# 3.2 Water Pollution and its Impacts – Water Quality Standards (IS 10500)

## 1. Introduction to Water Pollution
Water pollution is defined as the presence of foreign substances or impurities in water in such a quantity that it constitutes a health hazard, thus making the water unfit for use. 
* It affects both **surface water** (lakes, rivers, oceans) and **groundwater** (subsurface water stored in aquifers).

### The Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
* This is the first Indian law focused on preventing the discharge of untreated domestic and industrial wastewater into water bodies.
* It established Pollution Control Boards at the Centre and State levels.
* **Definition of Pollution under the Act:** Contamination or alteration of physical, chemical, or biological properties of water, or discharge of sewage/trade effluent that renders water harmful to:
  * Public health or safety.
  * Domestic, commercial, industrial, agricultural, or other uses.
  * Life and health of plants, animals, or aquatic organisms.

---

## 2. Sources of Water Pollution

Water pollution sources are primarily classified into two categories:

### I. Point Sources
* Sources that can be readily identified at a **single location** (direct discharges from a single pipe).
* *Examples:* Wastewater discharge from industries, domestic sewage pipes.
* *Control:* Easy to identify, control, and treat to acceptable levels before disposal.

### II. Non-point Sources (Diffused Sources)
* Sources diffused across a broad area where the exact point of origin cannot be easily traced.
* *Examples:* Run-off from agricultural lands, mining areas, city streets.
* *Control:* Very difficult to control and monitor.

### Natural vs. Anthropogenic Sources
* **Natural Sources:** Rain water, atmosphere, underground rocks, volcanoes.
* **Anthropogenic (Human-made) Sources:** Oil spills, industrial wastewater, solid-waste disposal, agricultural runoff, automobile garages.

### Major Sources of Water Pollution in India
1. **Domestic Wastewater:** Generated from households, commercial, and public places. It consists of 99.9% water and 0.1% solids (mostly highly putrescible organic matter). Divided into **Black water** (contains human excreta/urine) and **Grey water** (bathroom/kitchen wastewater).
2. **Agricultural Runoff:** Run-off from fields and animal farms. It is rich in Nitrogen, Phosphates, Organic matter, and Pesticides. Leads to **Eutrophication** (rapid algal growth that depletes dissolved oxygen).
3. **Industrial Effluent:** Complex waste from industrial operations (approx. 400 billion tons generated globally each year). Composition varies widely based on raw materials and processes.
4. **Groundwater Pollution:** Harmful materials (fertilizers, pesticides, solid waste leachates) dissolve in rainwater and percolate down into the soil, contaminating underground aquifers.

## 3. Common Water Pollutants

1. **Inorganic Salts:** Nitrates, phosphates, chlorides, sulphates of Ca, Mg, Fe. Causes water "hardness", scaling in pipes, stains on fabrics (Iron), and Eutrophication.
2. **Acids/Alkalis:** Alters natural pH (6 to 8). High acidity (pH < 5) causes fish death, eye irritation, and corrosion of ships. Alkalies often come from soap manufacturing, textile dyeing, and tanneries.
3. **Organic Matter:** Acts as food for microbes. Microbes decompose organics and consume Dissolved Oxygen (DO), causing oxygen depletion. *(Note: Minimum DO for fish survival is 3 to 4 mg/L).*
4. **Suspended Solids:** Increases water turbidity, settles at the bottom, and decomposes to cause odour nuisances.
5. **Floating Matter:** Oils, greases, etc. Makes rivers unsightly, obstructs sunlight penetration, and hinders the natural self-purification process.
6. **Thermal Discharges:** Hot water from nuclear/industrial cooling systems. Warm water is lighter, causing stratification. It forces aquatic life to the bottom, increases bacterial action, and accelerates oxygen depletion.
7. **Colouring Materials:** From textiles, paper mills, tanneries. Invites public attention and interferes with sunlight transmission, reducing natural disinfection.
8. **Toxic Chemicals:** Cyanides, sulphides, heavy metals (e.g., Hexavalent Chromium causes allergic skin reactions). These have a highly cumulative and toxic effect on aquatic life.
9. **Micro-organisms:** Pathogenic bacteria from domestic/slaughterhouse waste causing diseases like Diarrhoea and Pneumonia.
10. **Radioactive Materials:** Emits ionizing radiation leading to mutations and cancer. Hard to detect by standard methods.
11. **Foam Producing Matter:** Discharged by textile and paper mills. Leads to offensive odours, unchecked weed growth, bad taste, and death of aquatic life.

## 4. Impacts of Water Pollution
* **Human Health Problems:** Consumption of contaminated water leads to water-borne diseases like cholera, typhoid, and jaundice.
* **Heavy Metal Poisoning:** Excess fluoride, lead, or copper can cause general body weakness, mental retardation, organ failure, cancer, and death.
* **Destruction of Aquatic Life:** Due to DO depletion, pH changes, and toxins.
* **Eutrophication:** Nutrient-rich water (nitrogen & phosphate) causes abundant, suffocating algal blooms.
* **Bio-magnification:** Toxic pollutants enter the food chain and their concentration increases from one link (trophic level) to the next.

---

## 5. Control Measures of Water Pollution
1. **At the Consumer Level:** Drinking water should be boiled, cooled, and filtered.
2. **Disinfection:** Use chemicals like bleaching powder or adopt the chlorination process for drinking water.
3. **Agricultural Controls:** Prevent the use of pesticides and insecticides near lakes and ponds. Use phosphate-free detergents and reduce fertilizer usage to control eutrophication.
4. **Drainage Management:** Ensure drainage water does not mix with drinking water supplies. Maintain drainage systems properly.
5. **Wastewater Treatment:** Provide appropriate industrial and domestic wastewater treatment *before* discharging into natural bodies.
6. **Legislation & Awareness:** Increase public awareness. Enforce strict legislative measures, including the **PPP (Polluter Pays Principle)**, where the polluter bears the cost of cleanup operations.

## 6. Water Quality Standards (IS 10500)

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) sets the acceptable and permissible limits for drinking water under the **IS 10500** standard.

| Characteristics | Parameters | Acceptable Limit | Permissible Limit (in absence of alternate source) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Physical** | Temperature | - | - |
| | Colour | 5 Hazen | 15 Hazen |
| | Taste | Agreeable | Agreeable |
| | Odour | Agreeable | Agreeable |
| | Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) | 500 mg/L | 2000 mg/L |
| **2. Chemical** | pH | 6.5 - 8.5 | No relaxation |
| | Alkalinity | 200 mg/L | 600 mg/L |
| | Hardness | 200 mg/L | 600 mg/L |
| | Chloride (Cl) | 250 mg/L | 1000 mg/L |
| | Nitrate (NO₃) | 45 mg/L | No relaxation |
| | Iron (Fe) | 0.3 mg/L | No relaxation |
| | Chromium (Cr) | 0.05 mg/L | No relaxation |
| **3. Biological** | *E. Coli* or thermotolerant coliform bacteria | Shall not be detectable in any 100 ml sample | Shall not be detectable in any 100 ml sample |
