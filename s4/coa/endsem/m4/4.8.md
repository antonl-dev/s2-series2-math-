### 4.8 Classification of Pipeline Processors

Pipeline processors are classified based on the nature of the tasks they perform and the flexibility of their configuration. There are three primary classification levels.

#### 1. Level-Based Classification
This classifies pipelines based on where the overlapping of operations occurs in the computer system:

*   **Instruction Pipeline:** It overlaps the various phases of the instruction cycle (Fetch, Decode, Execute, etc.). Most modern CPUs use this to execute multiple instructions simultaneously.
*   **Arithmetic Pipeline:** It divides a complex arithmetic operation (like floating-point addition or multiplication) into sub-operations that can be executed in overlapping stages.
*   **Processor Pipeline:** (Also called Macro-pipelining) This overlaps the execution of different programs or large tasks across multiple processors.

#### 2. Configuration-Based Classification
This describes how the hardware segments are connected:

*   **Static Pipeline:** Can perform only one specific functional operation at a time (e.g., only addition). To perform a different operation, the pipeline must be drained and reconfigured.
*   **Dynamic Pipeline:** Can perform different functional operations simultaneously by following different paths through the segments. This requires more complex control logic.

#### 3. Linearity-Based Classification
*   **Linear Pipeline:** The sequence of stages is fixed and there are no feedback or feed-forward loops. Data flows strictly from the first segment to the last.
*   **Non-Linear Pipeline:** Contains feedback or feed-forward paths. A task might visit the same segment more than once during its execution. This is common in complex arithmetic operations where intermediate results are reused.

#### 4. Instruction Pipelining (Deep Dive)
Most exam questions focus on the **Instruction Pipeline**. A typical four-segment instruction pipeline consists of:
1.  **FI (Fetch Instruction):** Read instruction from memory.
2.  **DA (Decode Instruction & Address):** Identify the operation and calculate the effective address of operands.
3.  **FO (Fetch Operand):** Read operands from memory or registers.
4.  **EX (Execute):** Perform the operation and store the result.
