# 4.8 Classification of Pipeline Processors

### Overview
Pipeline processors are classified based on the types of tasks they perform and how their internal hardware is configured to handle those tasks. According to the notes (PDF 1, Page 14), pipelines are primarily categorized by their function.

### 1. Functional Classification
The most common way to classify pipelines is by the level at which the concurrency (simultaneous work) occurs:

#### **A. Arithmetic Pipeline**
*   **Purpose:** Designed to perform complex arithmetic operations (Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division) on floating-point or fixed-point numbers.
*   **Mechanism:** It breaks a complex math problem into smaller steps like exponent comparison, mantissa alignment, and normalization.
*   **Benefit:** Greatly improves performance for mathematical computations and signal processing.

#### **B. Instruction Pipeline**
*   **Purpose:** Designed to execute multiple instructions concurrently.
*   **Mechanism:** It overlaps different phases of instruction execution (Fetch, Decode, Execute, etc.).
*   **Benefit:** Increases the "throughput" of the CPU (how many instructions it can finish per second).

---

### 2. Configuration Classification
While not detailed in the notes, pipeline processors are also standardly classified by their structure:

*   **Unifunctional Pipeline:** A pipeline designed to perform only one specific operation (e.g., a dedicated floating-point adder). The hardware connections are fixed.
*   **Multifunctional Pipeline:** A pipeline that can perform different operations at different times (e.g., it could perform addition at one time and multiplication at another) by reconfiguring the connections between its segments.
*   **Static vs. Dynamic Pipelines:**
    *   **Static:** Can only perform one type of function at a time for all data flowing through. To switch functions, the pipe must be emptied.
    *   **Dynamic:** Can perform different functions on different data simultaneously within the same pipeline (highly complex).

---

### 3. Level Classification
*   **Arithmetic level:** Pipelining inside an ALU (Arithmetic Logic Unit).
*   **Instruction level:** Pipelining the execution of a program's instructions.
*   **Processor level:** Pipelining multiple processors to work together on a single task.
