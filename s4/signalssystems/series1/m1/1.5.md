# 1.5 Classification: Power, Energy, Deterministic, and Random Signals

## 1. Deterministic and Random Signals

### Deterministic Signals
*   **Definition:** A signal whose value can be determined exactly by a mathematical equation or formula at any instant of time. There is no uncertainty.
*   **Examples:** 
    *   `x(t) = 2t`
    *   `x(n) = 5 * sin(2 * pi * n)`
    *   `x(n) = 2` for `n >= 0`

### Random Signals
*   **Definition:** A signal whose value cannot be predicted or represented by a mathematical equation. It is characterized by statistical properties (mean, variance, etc.).
*   **Examples:** Thermal noise in electronic circuits, wind speed fluctuations, or "unwanted signals" (interference).

## 2. Energy and Power Signals

Signals are classified based on their total energy or average power. A signal can be an **Energy Signal**, a **Power Signal**, or **Neither**. It cannot be both.

### A. Energy Signals
*   **Property:** Has finite energy and zero average power.
*   **Condition:** `0 < E < infinity` and `P = 0`.
*   **Usually:** Aperiodic/Transient signals (signals that die out over time).

### B. Power Signals
*   **Property:** Has finite average power and infinite energy.
*   **Condition:** `0 < P < infinity` and `E = infinity`.
*   **Usually:** Periodic signals and constant amplitude signals.

### C. Neither
*   Signals that grow infinitely (like the ramp signal `r(t) = t`) have both infinite energy and infinite power.

## 3. Formulas for Calculation (Plain Text)

### Continuous Time (CT):
*   **Energy (E):** `Integral from -infinity to +infinity of |x(t)|^2 dt`
*   **Average Power (P):** `Limit as T -> infinity [ (1 / T) * Integral from -T/2 to +T/2 of |x(t)|^2 dt ]`

### Discrete Time (DT):
*   **Energy (E):** `Summation from n = -infinity to +infinity of |x(n)|^2`
*   **Average Power (P):** `Limit as N -> infinity [ (1 / (2N + 1)) * Summation from n = -N to +N of |x(n)|^2 ]`

## Practice & Homework Problems (Detailed Solutions)

### Q1. Check if `x(t) = e^(j * (2t + pi/4))` is an Energy or Power signal.
1.  **Find Energy:** First, note that `|e^(j * theta)| = 1`.
    *   `E = Integral from -infinity to +infinity of (1)^2 dt = [t]` from `-infinity` to `+infinity`.
    *   **E = infinity**. (Not an energy signal).
2.  **Find Power:**
    *   `P = (1/T) * Integral from -T/2 to +T/2 of (1)^2 dt`
    *   `P = (1/T) * [T/2 - (-T/2)] = (1/T) * T = 1`.
3.  **Result:** Since Power is finite (1) and Energy is infinite, it is a **Power Signal**.

### Q2. Check if `x(t) = e^(-5t) * u(t)` is an Energy or Power signal.
1.  **Find Energy:** Since `u(t)` is only active for `t >= 0`:
    *   `E = Integral from 0 to infinity of (e^(-5t))^2 dt`
    *   `E = Integral from 0 to infinity of e^(-10t) dt`
    *   `E = [ e^(-10t) / -10 ]` from `0` to `infinity`.
    *   `E = (0) - (-1/10) = 1/10`.
2.  **Find Power:**
    *   `P = Limit as T -> infinity (1/T * Energy)`.
    *   `P = Limit ( (1/10) / T ) = 0`.
3.  **Result:** Since Energy is finite (1/10) and Power is zero, it is an **Energy Signal**.

### Q3. Check if `x(n) = e^(j * (pi/2 * n + pi/8))` is Energy or Power (HW).
1.  **Magnitude:** `|x(n)| = 1` for all `n`.
2.  **Energy:** `Sum of (1)^2` from `-infinity` to `+infinity` is **infinity**.
3.  **Power:**
    *   `P = Limit [ (1 / (2N+1)) * Sum from -N to +N of 1 ]`
    *   `P = Limit [ (2N+1) / (2N+1) ] = 1`.
4.  **Result:** **Power Signal**.
