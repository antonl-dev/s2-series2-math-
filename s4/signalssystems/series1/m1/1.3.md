# 1.3 Classification of Signals: CT vs DT and Even/Odd

## 1. Continuous Time (CT) vs. Discrete Time (DT) Signals

### Continuous Time Signals (CT)
*   **Definition:** These signals are defined for every possible instant of time within an interval.
*   **Representation:** Denoted by `x(t)`, where `t` is the independent variable (time).
*   **Graph:** Represented by a smooth, continuous curve.

### Discrete Time Signals (DT)
*   **Definition:** These signals are defined only at specific, isolated points in time (discrete instants).
*   **Representation:** Denoted by `x(n)`, where `n` is an integer representing the sample index.
*   **Graph:** Represented by "stems" or vertical lines at integer values of `n`.

## 2. Even and Odd Signals
This classification is based on the symmetry of the signal around the vertical axis (time `t = 0` or index `n = 0`).

### Even Signals (Symmetrical)
*   A signal is even if it is identical to its mirror image across the vertical axis.
*   **Condition:** `x(t) = x(-t)` or `x(n) = x(-n)`.
*   **Example:** `cos(t)` is an even signal.

### Odd Signals (Anti-Symmetrical)
*   A signal is odd if it is the negative of its mirror image across the vertical axis.
*   **Condition:** `x(-t) = -x(t)` or `x(-n) = -x(n)`.
*   **Example:** `sin(t)` is an odd signal.

## 3. Signal Decomposition (Even and Odd Parts)
Any arbitrary signal `x(t)` or `x(n)` can be expressed as the sum of an even part and an odd part.

*   `x(t) = xe(t) + xo(t)`

### Formulas for Decomposing:
1.  **Even Part:** `xe(t) = [x(t) + x(-t)] / 2`
2.  **Odd Part:** `xo(t) = [x(t) - x(-t)] / 2`

## Practice & Homework Problems (from Notes)

### Q1. Prove the following properties of Signal Products (HW):
**1. Product of two even signals is an EVEN signal.**
*   *Proof:* Let `y(t) = x1(t) * x2(t)`. If both are even, `x1(-t) = x1(t)` and `x2(-t) = x2(t)`.
*   `y(-t) = x1(-t) * x2(-t) = x1(t) * x2(t) = y(t)`.
*   Result: Even.

**2. Product of two odd signals is an EVEN signal.**
*   *Proof:* Let `y(t) = x1(t) * x2(t)`. If both are odd, `x1(-t) = -x1(t)` and `x2(-t) = -x2(t)`.
*   `y(-t) = (-x1(t)) * (-x2(t)) = x1(t) * x2(t) = y(t)`.
*   Result: Even.

**3. Product of one even and one odd signal is an ODD signal.**
*   *Proof:* Let `y(t) = x1(t) * x2(t)`. Let `x1` be even and `x2` be odd.
*   `y(-t) = x1(-t) * x2(-t) = x1(t) * (-x2(t)) = -y(t)`.
*   Result: Odd.

### Q2. Graphical Problem: Decompose a Ramp Signal (HW)
*   **Given:** `x(t)` is a ramp starting at `t=0` and reaching height `2` at `t=2`.
*   **Step 1:** Draw `x(-t)`. This is a ramp starting at `t=0` and reaching height `2` at `t=-2`.
*   **Step 2 (Even Part):** Calculate `xe(t) = [x(t) + x(-t)] / 2`.
    *   Result: A triangle shape centered at 0 with height 1.
*   **Step 3 (Odd Part):** Calculate `xo(t) = [x(t) - x(-t)] / 2`.
    *   Result: At `t=2`, value is `(2-0)/2 = 1`. At `t=-2`, value is `(0-2)/2 = -1`.
    *   Shape: A line passing through the origin from `(-2, -1)` to `(2, 1)`.

### Q3. Find even and odd parts of `x(t) = e^(j*t)` (HW)
*   **Formula:** `e^(j*t) = cos(t) + j*sin(t)`.
*   Using symmetry properties:
    *   **xe(t)** = `cos(t)` (The even part of a complex exponential is the cosine component).
    *   **xo(t)** = `j*sin(t)` (The odd part is the imaginary sine component).
