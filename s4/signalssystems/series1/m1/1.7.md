# 1.7 Combination of Operations on Signals

When a signal undergoes multiple transformations (e.g., it is both shifted and folded), the sequence in which you perform the operations is critical. 

## 1. Priority/Order of Operations
According to your notes (Page 30), when dealing with combined time-domain operations:
1.  **Folding (Time Reversal):** Perform `x(-n)` first.
2.  **Shifting:** Perform the translation (`+k` or `-k`) second.

*Note: Amplitude scaling can be done at any point because it does not change the position of the samples on the horizontal (time) axis.*

## 2. The "Zero-Point" Method for Shifting
A helpful trick found in your notes to find where the new origin (the center of the signal) moves is to set the entire argument of the function to zero.

*   **For `x(-n + 2)`:** Set `-n + 2 = 0`. Solving for `n` gives `n = 2`. This means the original sample at `n=0` moves to `n=2`.
*   **For `x(-n - 2)`:** Set `-n - 2 = 0`. Solving for `n` gives `n = -2`. This means the original sample at `n=0` moves to `n=-2`.

## 3. Step-by-Step Examples (From Notes)

### Example 1: Transform `x(n)` into `x(-n + 2)`
Given a discrete signal `x(n)` with samples at `n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4`.

*   **Step 1: Folding**
    Perform `x(-n)`. The samples now exist at `n = 0, -1, -2, -3, -4`.
*   **Step 2: Shifting**
    Since we found `n = 2` from the zero-point method, we shift the folded signal 2 units to the **RIGHT**.
    *   Sample at `0` moves to `2`.
    *   Sample at `-4` moves to `-2`.
*   **Final Result:** The signal `x(-n + 2)` exists between `n = -2` and `n = 2`.

### Example 2: Transform `x(n)` into `x(-n - 2)`
*   **Step 1: Folding**
    Perform `x(-n)`. Samples are at `0, -1, -2, -3, -4`.
*   **Step 2: Shifting**
    Since we found `n = -2` from the zero-point method, we shift the folded signal 2 units to the **LEFT**.
    *   Sample at `0` moves to `-2`.
    *   Sample at `-4` moves to `-6`.
*   **Final Result:** The signal `x(-n - 2)` exists between `n = -6` and `n = -2`.

## Practice & Homework Problems (from Notes)

### Q1. Perform Time Scaling: `y(n) = x(2n)` (Page 31 HW)
*   **Type:** Down-sampling (Compression).
*   **Logic:** Keep only every 2nd sample from the original signal.
*   `y(0) = x(0)`
*   `y(1) = x(2)`
*   `y(2) = x(4)`
*   **Observation:** The signal appears "thinner" or faster.

### Q2. Perform Time Scaling: `y(n) = x(n/2)` (Page 31 HW)
*   **Type:** Up-sampling (Expansion).
*   **Logic:** Stretch the signal by a factor of 2.
*   `y(0) = x(0)`
*   `y(1) = x(0.5) = 0` (Since there is no sample at 0.5)
*   `y(2) = x(1)`
*   **Observation:** The signal appears "wider" or slower, with zeros inserted in between.
