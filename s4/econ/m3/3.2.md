### Module 3: Section 3.2 - Elementary Techniques in Business Forecasting

Forecasting methods are broadly classified into **Qualitative (Opinion-based)** and **Quantitative (Statistical)** techniques.

---

### 1. Survey Techniques (Opinion Polling)
These methods rely on the opinions of buyers, experts, or sales personnel. They are best suited for **short-term forecasting** or for **new products** where historical data is unavailable.

*   **A) Consumer Survey Method:** 
    *   **Complete Enumeration Survey:** Contacting almost all potential buyers to ask about their future purchase plans. Reliable but very costly and time-consuming.
    *   **Sample Survey:** Selecting a representative subset of consumers. It is cost-effective but carries the risk of sampling error.
    *   **End-Use Method:** Focuses on the demand for **intermediate goods** (e.g., forecasting the demand for milk by looking at the production of ice cream and cheese).
*   **B) Sales Force Opinion (Grassroots Approach):** 
    *   Individual sales representatives estimate demand for their specific territories.
    *   **Advantage:** Salespeople have direct contact with the market. 
    *   **Disadvantage:** May be biased if sales targets are linked to these forecasts.

### 2. Expert Opinion (Delphi Method)
*   **Process:** A group of experts is asked for their opinions anonymously (often via mail/questionnaire). 
*   **Iteration:** Their opinions are summarized and shared back with the group. Experts are allowed to revise their estimates based on the group's feedback.
*   **Goal:** This process continues until a **unanimity (consensus)** is reached.
*   **Advantage:** Prevents "groupthink" and the dominance of a single loud personality because the identity of experts is kept secret.

### 3. Statistical Techniques: Least Square Method
The Least Square Method is a formal mathematical technique used for **Trend Projection**.

*   **Concept:** It is used to fit a "line of best fit" to a set of historical data points.
*   **Logic:** It minimizes the sum of the squares of the vertical deviations between each data point and the fitted line.
*   **Assumption:** It assumes that the past trend (time-series) will continue into the future.
*   **Formula:** Typically follows the linear equation $Y = a + bX$, where $Y$ is the predicted demand and $X$ is the time period.

### 4. Other Statistical Methods (Overview)
*   **Graphical Method:** Plotting data on a graph and drawing a free-hand curve to identify trends. (Simple but lacks mathematical precision).
*   **Moving Average:** Taking the average of the most recent periods to forecast the next one.
*   **Regression Analysis:** Measuring the relationship between a dependent variable (demand) and independent variables (like price, income, or population).
