# Module 3: Section 3.4 - Business Financing: Sources of Capital, Capital and Money Markets

### 1. Sources of Capital
Companies raise funds for their operations through various domestic and foreign sources. These are categorized based on their nature and duration:

*   **i) Internal Self-Finance:** Funds generated within the unit, such as retained earnings (undistributed profits) and depreciation funds. It reduces the cost of borrowing.
*   **ii) Equity (Shares):** Represents ownership in the company. Investors buy shares and get a claim on earnings (dividends). These are perpetual and do not need to be repaid.
*   **iii) Debentures and Bonds:** Debt instruments used to raise long-term finance. The company agrees to pay back the principal on a fixed date and make regular interest payments.
*   **iv) Public Deposits:** A source of short-term finance where the public keeps money with companies for a fixed period (6 months to 3 years) at a fixed interest rate.
*   **v) Loans from Banks:** Commercial banks provide funds for short-term needs or working capital via overdrafts and credit limits.
*   **vi) Development Finance Institutions (DFIs):** Specialized institutions like IDBI and IFCI that provide large-scale industrial finance for setting up new industries.

### 2. Money Market vs. Capital Market
Financial markets are broadly divided into two components based on the maturity period of the assets traded.

| Feature | Money Market | Capital Market |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maturity** | Short-term (Up to 1 year) | Long-term (More than 1 year/Perpetual) |
| **Instruments** | Treasury bills, Certificate of Deposits, Bills of Exchange | Shares, Bonds, Debentures |
| **Purpose** | To maintain liquidity and meet working capital needs | To finance fixed investments (land, machinery) |
| **Risk** | Relatively Low | High |
| **Regulator** | Central Bank (e.g., RBI) | Securities Exchange Board (e.g., SEBI) |

### 3. Primary vs. Secondary Markets (Capital Market)
*   **Primary Market:** The "New Issue Market" where fresh securities (shares/bonds) are offered to the public for the first time.
*   **Secondary Market:** The "Stock Market" where already issued securities are traded among investors. It provides liquidity to existing investors.

### 4. Important Stock Indices (India)
*   **NSE (NIFTY):** Benchmark index of the National Stock Exchange consisting of the top 50 equity stocks.
*   **BSE (SENSEX):** Index of the Bombay Stock Exchange consisting of 30 well-established "Blue chip" companies.
