# Module 3: Section 3.5 - International Financing: FDI, FPI, and FII

### 1. Introduction to International Financing
International financing refers to the flow of capital across national borders. It allows businesses to access global funds and helps developing countries bridge the gap between their domestic savings and investment requirements. The three major forms are **FDI**, **FPI**, and **FII**.

---

### 2. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)
FDI occurs when a company or individual from one country makes a physical investment into building a factory or setting up a business in another country.
*   **Nature:** Long-term and stable. It involves a "lasting interest" in the management of the enterprise.
*   **Examples:** Opening a new branch, manufacturing plant, or acquiring a significant stake (usually >10%) in a foreign company.
*   **Benefits:** Brings not only capital but also technology, management expertise, and skills to the host country.
*   **Exit:** Difficult and slow; involves selling physical assets or entire business units.

---

### 3. Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)
FPI involves the purchase of financial assets like shares, bonds, or mutual funds by foreign investors in a foreign stock exchange.
*   **Nature:** Short-term and volatile. The investor does not seek direct control or management of the company.
*   **Objective:** To earn high returns through dividends, interest, or capital appreciation.
*   **Exit:** Very easy; can be sold "at the click of a button" on the stock market. It is often called **"Hot Money"** because it can leave the country quickly during economic instability.

---

### 4. Foreign Institutional Investor (FII)
FII refers to large institutional entities (like hedge funds, insurance companies, or pension funds) that are registered in one country but invest in the financial markets of another.
*   **Role:** FIIs are the major players in FPI. They bring massive liquidity to the stock markets of developing economies.
*   **Impact:** Their entry boosts stock prices and the value of the local currency, while their sudden exit can cause a market crash.

---

### 5. Summary Comparison

| Feature | FDI (Direct Investment) | FPI/FII (Portfolio Investment) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Asset Type** | Physical Assets (Factories, Land) | Financial Assets (Shares, Bonds) |
| **Duration** | Long-term | Short-term |
| **Entry/Exit** | Difficult & Slow | Easy & Fast ("Hot Money") |
| **Control** | Direct management control | No management control |
| **Impact** | Increases productive capacity | Increases market liquidity |
