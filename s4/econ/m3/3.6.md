# Module 3: Section 3.6 - Basic Principles of Taxation: Direct, Indirect, GST, and Tax Evasion

### 1. Definition of Taxation
Taxation is a compulsory contribution imposed by the government on individuals and businesses to fund public services (infrastructure, defense, health, education). It is a **transfer payment**, meaning it does not result in a direct exchange of goods or services for the payer.

### 2. Classification of Taxes
Taxes are primarily classified into two types:

*   **i) Direct Tax:**
    *   The burden of the tax is borne by the person on whom it is imposed. It cannot be shifted to others.
    *   **Examples:** Income Tax, Corporate Tax.
    *   **Nature:** Usually **progressive**, meaning the tax rate increases as the income of the taxpayer increases.
*   **ii) Indirect Tax:**
    *   The burden of the tax can be shifted from the original taxpayer to the final consumer.
    *   **Examples:** GST, Customs Duty, Excise Duty.
    *   **Nature:** Usually **regressive** in impact, as a rich person and a poor person pay the same amount of tax on a specific product (e.g., a loaf of bread).

### 3. Goods and Services Tax (GST)
GST is a comprehensive, multi-stage, destination-based indirect tax that replaced many other indirect taxes in India (VAT, Service Tax, Excise Duty, etc.).

*   **One Nation, One Tax:** Aims to create a unified common market.
*   **Input Tax Credit (ITC):** Allows taxpayers to claim credit for taxes paid on inputs, which helps in avoiding the **"Cascading Effect"** (tax on tax).
*   **Components:** 
    *   **CGST:** Collected by Central Govt for intra-state sales.
    *   **SGST:** Collected by State Govt for intra-state sales.
    *   **IGST:** Collected by Central Govt for inter-state sales.

### 4. Tax Evasion vs. Tax Avoidance
*   **Tax Evasion:** The **illegal** non-payment or underpayment of taxes. It involves deliberately misrepresenting or concealing income (e.g., "black money"). It is a punishable offense.
*   **Tax Avoidance:** The **legal** utilization of the tax regime to one's advantage to reduce the amount of tax that is payable by means that are within the law (e.g., claiming deductions or exemptions).
