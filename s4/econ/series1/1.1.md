# Module 1: Introduction to Engineering Economics

## 1.1 Engineering and Economics: Definition, Scope, and Basic Concepts

### 1.1.1 What is Engineering Economics?
**Definition:** Engineering Economics is a branch of economics that applies economic principles to engineering problems. It involves the systematic evaluation of the costs and benefits of technical projects to make **cost-effective decisions**.

*   **The Goal:** To ensure that technical designs are not just functional, but also economically viable.
*   **The Core Question:** "Will the project save more money than it costs over its lifetime?"

### 1.1.2 Scope of Engineering Economics
Based on your Course Outcomes (CO1), the scope includes:
1.  **Resource Allocation:** Applying economic techniques to decide how to use limited resources (money, time, labor).
2.  **Decision Making:** Choosing between alternative technical designs based on financial performance.
3.  **Performance Enhancement:** Analyzing revenue and cost components to improve a firm's profitability.
4.  **Forecasting:** Using techniques to predict future demand and economic conditions.

### 1.1.3 Basic Concepts in Economics

#### A. The Foundations
*   **Father of Economics:** Adam Smith.
*   **The "Invisible Hand":** A concept by Adam Smith stating that market supply and demand naturally lead to equilibrium. Decisions are based on demand.
*   **Government Intervention:** While markets often manage themselves, certain sectors are managed under the government (Govt.), such as:
    *   Defense
    *   Police
    *   Social Welfare

#### B. The Theory of Value (Karl Marx)
Your notes highlight Karl Marx’s "Labor Theory of Value":
*   **Importance of Labor:** The value of a product comes mainly from the labor used to produce it, not just from raw materials or machines.
*   **Profit Calculation:** Profit is defined as the value produced by workers minus the wages paid to them.

> **Example from Class Notes:**
> *   Production: 10 chairs.
> *   Selling Price: ₹250 per chair.
> *   Total Revenue: $10 \times 250 = ₹2500$
> *   Labor Wage: ₹80 per chair.
> *   Total Wage: $10 \times 80 = ₹800$
> *   **Profit for Company:** $250 - 80 = ₹170$ per chair (or ₹1700 total).
> *   *Conclusion:* Economic fairness ensures labor receives a "fair wage."

#### C. Economy as an Evolving System
*   **Stages of Evolution:** An economy does not stay static; it evolves through different stages.
*   **The Chess Analogy:** The economy is like a chess game; every move (decision) depends on the opponent's (competitor/market) move.

#### D. Economics as a Social Science
Economics is the study of how resources can be put into the **best use** in the most **efficient way**. 
*   **Technology’s Role:** To achieve maximum efficiency in resource use, advanced technology is required.
