# Module 1.6: Supply and the Law of Supply

## 1.6.1 What is "Supply"?
**Definition:** Supply refers to the quantity of a commodity that a producer is **willing and able to offer for sale** at a given price during a specific period of time. (From page 12 of your notes).

*   **Stock vs. Supply:** Stock is the total volume of goods available; Supply is only the part of the stock actually brought to the market for sale.

---

## 1.6.2 The Law of Supply
**The Law:** "Other things being equal, the higher the price, the higher the quantity supplied; and the lower the price, the lower the quantity supplied."

*   **Relationship:** Direct (Positive) relationship between Price and Quantity Supplied.
*   **Supply Curve:** Slopes **upward** from left to right.
*   **Logic:** Higher prices mean higher potential profit, which encourages producers to produce and sell more.

## 1.6.3 Determinants of Supply (Factors)
Why does the supply of a product change? (From pages 12-13 of your notes):

1.  **Price of the Commodity:** The most important factor. (Price $\uparrow$, Supply $\uparrow$).
2.  **Technology:** Better technology leads to more output at lower costs, which increases supply.
3.  **Economic Policy:** Government policies (like taxes or subsidies) affect production. 
    *   *Example:* If the government reduces taxes on production, supply increases.
4.  **Price of Resources (Input Costs):** If the price of raw materials or labor increases, the cost of production goes up, and supply decreases.
5.  **Number of Sellers:** More firms in the market = higher total market supply.
6.  **Goal of the Firm:** 
    *   If the goal is **Profit Maximization**, supply is adjusted to maximize gain.
    *   If the goal is **Social Welfare** (Public Sector), supply might be kept high even at low prices.
7.  **Agreements among Producers (Market Structure):**
    *   **Monopoly:** A single seller controls the entire supply.
    *   **Oligopoly:** A few large sellers (e.g., Airlines like IndiGo, or Oil producing countries) influence the market supply.

---

## 1.6.4 Shifters of Supply
A "Shift" happens when factors **other than price** change (From page 13):
*   **Increase in Supply (Rightward Shift):** Caused by better technology, lower input costs, or government subsidies.
*   **Decrease in Supply (Leftward Shift):** Caused by old technology, higher taxes, or higher resource prices.

## 1.6.5 Market Structures mentioned in your notes:
*   **Monopoly:** Single seller $\rightarrow$ Price Maker.
*   **Duopoly:** Two sellers.
*   **Oligopoly:** Few sellers (Secure profit through agreements).
*   **Monopolistic:** Many sellers selling slightly different products.

---

### 📝 Study Tip:
Keep in mind the difference between **Demand** (Consumer side) and **Supply** (Producer side). 
*   Demand: Price $\uparrow$, Qty $\downarrow$ (Inverse).
*   Supply: Price $\uparrow$, Qty $\uparrow$ (Direct).
