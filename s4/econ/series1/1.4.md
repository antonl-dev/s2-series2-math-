# Module 1.4: Demand and the Law of Demand

## 1.4.1 What is "Demand"?
In economics, demand is not just a "wish." For demand to exist, three things must be present simultaneously (From page 23 of your notes):
1.  **Desire:** The wish to own a commodity.
2.  **Power (Purchasing Power):** Having the money to buy it.
3.  **Willingness:** The readiness to spend that money.

> **Key Equation:** Demand = Desire + Ability to Pay + Willingness to Pay.

---

## 1.4.2 Determinants of Demand (Factors)
Why does the demand for a product change? Your notes (page 10, 11, 23) list several factors:

1.  **Price of the Commodity:** Usually, if Price $\uparrow$, Demand $\downarrow$.
2.  **Income of the Consumer:** 
    *   For normal goods: Income $\uparrow$, Demand $\uparrow$.
3.  **Tastes and Preferences:** If a product becomes "trendy," demand increases.
4.  **Population:** A larger population generally means higher demand.
5.  **Climate/Weather:** (Example from notes: Demand for umbrellas in rain; "Volcanic/Ethiopia flights cancelled" context).
6.  **Prices of Related Goods:**
    *   **Substitute Goods:** Goods used in place of each other (e.g., Tea and Coffee). If the price of Tea $\uparrow$, the demand for Coffee $\uparrow$.
    *   **Complementary Goods:** Goods used together (e.g., Petrol and Cars, Bread and Jam). If the price of Petrol $\uparrow$, the demand for Cars $\downarrow$.
7.  **Expectations of Future Prices:** If people think the price will rise tomorrow, they buy more today.
8.  **Advertisement:** Better ads lead to higher demand.

---

## 1.4.3 The Law of Demand
**The Law:** "Other things being equal (Ceteris Paribus), as the price of a commodity decreases, the quantity demanded increases; and as the price increases, the quantity demanded decreases."

*   **Relationship:** Inverse (Opposite) relationship between Price and Quantity.
*   **Demand Curve:** Slopes **downward** from left to right.

> **Visual Aid:** [Click here to see the Demand Curve Graph](https://www.google.com/search?q=Demand+curve+downward+sloping+graph&tbm=isch)

### Assumptions of the Law:
For the law to work, we assume:
*   No change in consumer income.
*   No change in tastes or preferences.
*   No new substitutes available.
*   Prices of related goods remain constant.

---

## 1.4.4 Exceptions to the Law (Where Price $\uparrow$ leads to Demand $\uparrow$)
Sometimes, the law fails. These are called "Exceptional Cases":

1.  **Giffen Goods (Inferior Goods):** Very basic goods (like staple starch) where people buy more as the price rises because they can no longer afford better food.
2.  **Veblen Goods (Luxury/Status Goods):** Diamonds, luxury cars, or gold. People buy them *because* they are expensive to show status.
3.  **Necessities:** Medicines or salt. You must buy them regardless of price.
4.  **Future Price Changes:** If people fear a massive price hike in the future, they buy more now even if the current price is high.

### 📝 Exam Prep Tip:
**Question from Paper:** "State the Law of Demand" (2 marks).
*   *Answer:* The Law of Demand states that, keeping other factors constant, the quantity demanded of a commodity is inversely proportional to its price. When Price falls, Demand rises, and vice versa.
