# Module 1.3: Types of Firm and Utility Theory

## 1.3.1 Types of Business Organizations
Firms are classified based on ownership, liability, and objectives. (From pages 18-20 & 24 of your notes).

### 1. Sole Proprietorship (Individual Business)
*   **Definition:** Owned and managed by a single person.
*   **Key Features:** Single profit, quick decision-making, small capital requirements, and business secrets remain private.
*   **Disadvantage:** **Unlimited Liability** (Owner is personally responsible for all debts).
*   **Example:** A local repair shop or grocery store.

### 2. Partnership
*   **Definition:** Owned by two or more persons (usually up to 20).
*   **Key Features:** Shared risks and profits, higher investment capacity than sole proprietorship.
*   **Disadvantage:** Conflicts between partners can occur; shared liability.

### 3. Joint Stock Company
*   **Definition:** A company owned by shareholders and managed by a Board of Directors.
*   **Key Features:** **Limited Liability** (Shareholders only lose what they invested), large capital through shares.
*   **Example:** Tata Steel, L&T.
> **Visual Aid:** [Click here to see Joint Stock Company Structure](https://www.google.com/search?q=Joint+Stock+Company+structure+diagram&tbm=isch)

### 4. Public Sector Undertakings (PSU)
*   **Definition:** Owned by the Government.
*   **Key Objective:** **Public Welfare**, not just profit.
*   **Example:** ONGC, Railways.

### 5. Co-operative Societies
*   **Motto:** "One for all and all for one."
*   **Key Features:** Managed by members, focus on providing service to members.
*   **Exam Alert (4 Marks):** Advantages include democratic management, elimination of middlemen, and lower costs for members.
*   **Example:** AMUL (Dairy co-operative).

## 1.3.2 Concept of Utility
**Definition:** Utility is the power of a commodity to satisfy a human want. It is the "satisfaction" derived from consumption.

### Two Ways to Measure Utility:
1.  **Cardinal Utility:** Assumes utility can be measured in numbers called **"Utils"**. (Used in the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility).
2.  **Ordinal Utility:** Assumes utility cannot be measured in numbers, only **ranked** (e.g., "I like an apple more than an orange").

### Key Terms:
*   **Total Utility (TU):** The total satisfaction received from consuming a specific quantity of a good.
*   **Marginal Utility (MU):** The **additional** utility gained from consuming **one more unit** of a good.
    *   *Formula:* $MU = \Delta TU / \Delta Q$ (Change in Total Utility divided by change in Quantity).

---

## 1.3.3 Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (LDMU)
**The Law:** As a person consumes more units of a good, the extra satisfaction (Marginal Utility) derived from each additional unit **decreases**.

### The Relationship between TU and MU:
1.  When **MU is positive**, Total Utility (TU) increases.
2.  When **MU is Zero**, Total Utility (TU) is at its **Maximum** (Point of Satiety).
3.  When **MU becomes negative**, Total Utility (TU) begins to **decline**.

### Assumptions for this Law:
*   **Homogeneous units:** All units of the good must be identical in size, quality, and taste.
*   **Continuous consumption:** There should be no time gap between consuming units.
*   **Reasonable units:** You shouldn't consume tiny drops of water; it must be a standard unit (like a glass).
