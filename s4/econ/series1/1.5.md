# Module 1.5: Elasticity of Demand

## 1.5.1 Definition
While the Law of Demand tells us **direction** (price up, demand down), **Elasticity** tells us **how much** (the extent of the change). 

**Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_p$ or $C_p$):** It is the measurement of the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the price of that commodity.

---

## 1.5.2 The 5 Types of Price Elasticity
(From pages 25-27 of your notes)

1.  **Perfectly Elastic ($E_p = \infty$):** A tiny change in price leads to an infinite change in demand. (Horizontal Curve).
2.  **Perfectly Inelastic ($E_p = 0$):** Demand does not change at all, regardless of price. 
    *   *Example from notes:* **Insulin**. People need it to live, so they buy the same amount even if the price rises. (Vertical Curve).
3.  **Relatively Elastic ($E_p > 1$):** Demand changes **more** than the price. (Flatter Curve).
4.  **Relatively Inelastic ($E_p < 1$):** Demand changes **less** than the price. 
    *   *Example from notes:* **Petrol**. Even if the price goes from ₹100 to ₹150, people still need it for transport, so demand only drops slightly. (Steep Curve).
5.  **Unitary Elastic ($E_p = 1$):** Percentage change in price is exactly equal to the percentage change in demand.

## 1.5.3 Measurement of Elasticity (The Formula)
The most common method is the **Percentage Method**.

$$E_p = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}}$$

**Expanded Formula:**
$$E_p = \frac{\Delta Q}{Q_1} \times \frac{P_1}{\Delta P}$$
*   $P_1, Q_1$ = Original Price and Quantity.
*   $\Delta P, \Delta Q$ = Change in Price and Quantity.

---

## 1.5.4 Numerical Examples

### Example 1 (From your Class Notes - Page 26)
*   **Original:** $P_1 = ₹10$, $Q_1 = 2000$ kg
*   **New:** $P_2 = ₹9$, $Q_2 = 2500$ kg
*   **Changes:** $\Delta P = 1$, $\Delta Q = 500$
*   **Calculation:** $\frac{500}{2000} \times \frac{10}{1} = 0.25 \times 10 = \mathbf{2.5}$
*   **Result:** $E_p > 1$, so demand is **Elastic**.

### Example 2 (From your Question Paper - Page 3, Q7b)
*   **Scenario:** Initial price of ice cream is ₹4, demand is 1 cup. Price falls to ₹2, demand increases to 4 cups.
*   **Data:** $P_1 = 4, Q_1 = 1, P_2 = 2, Q_2 = 4$
*   **Changes:** $\Delta P = 2, \Delta Q = 3$
*   **Calculation:** $\frac{3}{1} \times \frac{4}{2} = 3 \times 2 = \mathbf{6}$
*   **Result:** $E_p = 6$ (Highly Elastic).

## 1.5.5 Other Types of Elasticity
1.  **Income Elasticity ($E_y$):** Measures how demand changes when the **consumer's income** changes.
    *   *Formula:* $\frac{\% \Delta Q}{\% \Delta \text{Income}}$
2.  **Cross Elasticity ($E_c$):** Measures how demand for Good X changes when the **Price of Good Y** changes.
    *   *Substitutes:* Positive elasticity (Price of Coffee $\uparrow$, Demand for Tea $\uparrow$).
    *   *Complements:* Negative elasticity (Price of Petrol $\uparrow$, Demand for Cars $\downarrow$).

---

## 1.5.6 Why does Elasticity matter? (Applications)
*   **For Producers:** Helps in fixing prices. If demand is inelastic, they can raise prices to increase revenue.
*   **For Government:** Helps in taxation. Govt. usually taxes inelastic goods (like cigarettes or alcohol) because people will keep buying them even if the price is high.
*   **For International Trade:** Helps in determining terms of trade for imports/exports (e.g., India's reliance on crude oil).
