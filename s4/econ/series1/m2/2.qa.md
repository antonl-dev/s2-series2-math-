# Module 2: Question and Answer Bank

## Part A: Short Answer Questions (2 Marks)

### Q1. Define "Short-run".
**Answer:** The short-run is a period of time in which at least one factor of production (usually capital or land) is **fixed**, while other factors (like labor) are **variable**. In this period, a firm can only increase production by increasing variable inputs.

### Q2. Describe "Production Function".
**Answer:** The production function expresses the technical relationship between physical inputs (factors of production like land, labor, capital) and the resulting physical output. 
*   **Equation:** $Q = f(L, K, R...)$ where $Q$ is output, $L$ is labor, $K$ is capital.

### Q3. Distinguish between Explicit and Implicit cost.
**Answer:** 
*   **Explicit Costs:** Actual "out-of-pocket" cash payments made to outsiders for resources (e.g., wages, rent, raw material costs).
*   **Implicit Costs:** The opportunity costs of using resources **owned by the firm** itself (e.g., interest on own capital, salary of the owner if they worked elsewhere).

### Q4. Define "Sunk Cost".
**Answer:** A sunk cost is a cost that has already been incurred and cannot be recovered or changed by any future decision. 
*   **Example:** Money spent on specialized research or old, non-resalable machinery.

### Q5. What is the "Shut Down Point"?
**Answer:** The shut-down point is the minimum price at which a firm can cover its **Average Variable Cost (AVC)**. If the price falls below AVC, the firm should stop production in the short run to minimize losses.
*   **Condition:** $Price < AVC$

## Part B: Short Essay Questions (4 - 6 Marks)

### Q6. "Economies of scale are associated with large scale operations." Explain.
**Answer:** Economies of scale are the cost advantages a firm gains as it increases its scale of production.
1.  **Internal Economies:** Advantages specific to the firm, such as:
    *   **Technical:** Using bigger, better machines.
    *   **Managerial:** Hiring specialized department heads.
    *   **Financial:** Getting lower interest rates on loans.
    *   **Risk-Bearing:** Diversifying products (e.g., ITC selling both books and sugar).
2.  **External Economies:** Benefits shared by the whole industry, like improved regional transport or a pool of skilled local labor.

### Q7. Distinguish between Social Cost and Private Cost with an example.
**Answer:**
*   **Private Cost:** The direct cost incurred by the producer to manufacture a product (Raw materials + Labor + Rent).
*   **External Cost:** The cost imposed on society (Pollution, health issues) for which the firm doesn't pay.
*   **Social Cost:** The sum of both ($Social = Private + External$).
*   **Example:** If a factory produces a unit for ₹10 (Private Cost) but causes ₹3 worth of pollution damage (External Cost), the Social Cost is ₹13.

### Q8. Numerical: Calculate BEP (From Internal Assessment).
**Question:** Calculate the Break-Even Point (BEP) if Total Fixed Cost = ₹30,000; Variable cost per unit = ₹25; Selling price per unit = ₹35.
**Answer:**
*   $TFC = 30,000$
*   $Price (P) = 35$
*   $Variable Cost (V) = 25$
*   **Formula:** $Q_B = \frac{TFC}{P - V}$
*   **Calculation:** $30,000 / (35 - 25) = 30,000 / 10 = \mathbf{3,000 \text{ units}}$.

## Part C: Long Essay / Numerical Questions (8 - 10 Marks)

### Q9. Explain the Law of Variable Proportion using a suitable diagram.
**Answer:**
*   **Definition:** This law states that in the short run, as we add more units of a variable factor (labor) to a fixed factor (land), the marginal product initially rises, then falls, and eventually becomes negative.
*   **The Three Stages:**
    1.  **Stage I (Increasing Returns):** Total Product (TP) rises at an increasing rate. Marginal Product (MP) is rising.
    2.  **Stage II (Diminishing Returns):** TP rises at a decreasing rate. MP is falling but positive. **The firm should operate here.** TP is max when MP = 0.
    3.  **Stage III (Negative Returns):** TP begins to fall. MP becomes negative.

### Q10. Numerical: Detailed BEA (From Regular Exam Paper).
**Question:** A firm pays ₹10,000 monthly rent and ₹10,000 interest. Monthly expenditure on raw materials is ₹40,000. Monthly sales revenue is ₹80,000. Price of one unit is ₹40. 
**Estimate:** (i) P/V Ratio, (ii) BE Sales, (iii) BE Output, (iv) Profit, (v) Margin of Safety.

**Answer:**
1.  **Extract Data:**
    *   $TFC = 10k + 10k = ₹20,000$
    *   $TVC = ₹40,000$
    *   $Total Revenue (TR) = ₹80,000$
    *   $Price (P) = ₹40$
    *   $Qty (Q) = 80,000 / 40 = 2,000 \text{ units}$
    *   $Variable Cost per unit (V) = 40,000 / 2,000 = ₹20$
2.  **Calculations:**
    *   **(i) P/V Ratio:** $\frac{P - V}{P} = \frac{40 - 20}{40} = 0.5$ or $\mathbf{50\%}$
    *   **(ii) BE Output ($Q_B$):** $\frac{TFC}{P - V} = \frac{20,000}{20} = \mathbf{1,000 \text{ units}}$
    *   **(iii) BE Sales ($S_B$):** $1,000 \times 40 = \mathbf{₹40,000}$
    *   **(iv) Profit:** $TR - (TFC + TVC) = 80,000 - (20,000 + 40,000) = \mathbf{₹20,000}$
    *   **(v) Margin of Safety:** $Actual Sales - BE Sales = 80,000 - 40,000 = \mathbf{₹40,000}$

### Q11. Complete the following Cost Table.
**Question:** If TFC is ₹60 and TVC for units 1, 2, 3 are 30, 40, 45... find AC and MC.
**Answer Strategy:**
*   **Total Cost (TC):** $60 + TVC$
*   **Average Cost (AC):** $TC / Output$
*   **Marginal Cost (MC):** $TC_{new} - TC_{old}$
*   *Key Logic:* At Output 0, $TC = TFC$ (60). For Output 1, $TC = 60 + 30 = 90$, $MC = 90 - 60 = 30$.
