# Module 2.6: Break-Even Analysis (BEA)

## 2.6.1 What is the Break-Even Point (BEP)?
**Definition:** The Break-Even Point is the level of sales or output where **Total Revenue (TR) equals Total Cost (TC)**. (From page 55 of your notes).
*   At this point, the firm makes **No Profit and No Loss**.
*   **TR = TC**

---

## 2.6.2 The Break-Even Formulas
To find the number of units you need to sell to "break even," use these components:

1.  **Contribution Margin (C):** The amount of money left over from each sale after paying the variable costs.
    *   **Formula:** $Contribution = Price (P) - Variable Cost per unit (AVC)$
    *   *Logic:* This "contribution" goes toward paying off the Fixed Costs.

2.  **Break-Even Quantity ($Q_B$):**
    *   **Formula:** $Q_B = \frac{Total Fixed Cost (TFC)}{Price (P) - Variable Cost per unit (AVC)}$

3.  **Break-Even Sales ($S_B$):** The total money (revenue) value at the break-even point.
    *   **Formula:** $S_B = Q_B \times Price$  OR  $S_B = \frac{TFC}{1 - \frac{AVC}{P}}$

---

## 2.6.3 The Profit-Volume (P/V) Ratio
(From pages 59-60). This ratio shows the relationship between Contribution and Sales. It is expressed as a percentage.
*   **Formula:** $P/V \text{ Ratio} = \frac{Contribution}{Sales} \times 100 = \frac{S - V}{S} \times 100$
*   **Significance:** A higher P/V ratio means the firm is more profitable for every unit sold.

---

## 2.6.4 The Break-Even Chart
(From page 57). Graphing the BEP provides a clear visual of the business's health.

*   **X-axis:** Quantity (Output).
*   **Y-axis:** Revenue and Cost (Money).
*   **Lines to draw:**
    1.  **TFC Line:** A horizontal line (costs stay same regardless of output).
    2.  **TC Line:** Starts from the TFC point and slopes upward.
    3.  **TR Line:** Starts from zero (0,0) and slopes upward.
*   **The Intersection:** Where the TR and TC lines cross is the **Break-Even Point**.
*   **Zones:**
    *   **Below BEP:** The area where TC > TR (The **Loss** Zone).
    *   **Above BEP:** The area where TR > TC (The **Profit** Zone).

## 2.6.5 Margin of Safety (MoS)
(From page 60).
**Definition:** The difference between the **Actual Sales** and the **Break-Even Sales**.
*   **Formula:** $Margin \text{ of Safety} = Actual \text{ Sales} - Break \text{-Even Sales}$
*   **Significance:** It tells the business how much sales can drop before they start making a loss. A "high" margin of safety is a sign of a very safe business.

---

## 2.6.6 Example Calculation (From Page 55-56)
**Data:**
*   Price (P) = ₹10
*   Fixed Cost (TFC) = ₹200
*   Variable Cost (AVC) = ₹5

**Steps:**
1.  **Contribution:** $10 - 5 = ₹5$ per unit.
2.  **Break-Even Quantity:** $200 / 5 = \mathbf{40 \text{ units}}$.
3.  **Verify:** 
    *   TR at 40 units: $10 \times 40 = ₹400$
    *   TC at 40 units: $200 + (5 \times 40) = 200 + 200 = ₹400$
    *   **TR = TC**, so 40 units is correct.

### 📝 Exam Prep Tip:
**Question 4 from your Internal Assessment (2 marks):** "Calculate BEP if TFC = 30,000; AVC = 25; P = 35."
*   *Calculation:* $Q_B = 30,000 / (35 - 25) = 30,000 / 10 = \mathbf{3,000 \text{ units}}$.
