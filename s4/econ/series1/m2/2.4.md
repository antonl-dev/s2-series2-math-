# Module 2.4: Short-Run and Long-Run Cost Curves

## 2.4.1 The Cost Function
**Definition:** A cost function expresses the relationship between the cost of production and the level of output ($Q$).
> **Formula:** $TC = f(Q)$

---

## 2.4.2 Short-Run Cost Components
In the short run, we analyze cost using "Average" and "Marginal" values. (From pages 46, 49, and 51 of your notes).

### 1. Average Fixed Cost (AFC)
*   **Formula:** $AFC = TFC / Output (Q)$
*   **Behavior:** As output increases, AFC **decreases continuously** because the same fixed cost is spread over more units.
*   **Graph:** Known as a **Rectangular Hyperbola** (it never touches the X or Y axis).

### 2. Average Variable Cost (AVC)
*   **Formula:** $AVC = TVC / Q$
*   **Behavior:** It is **U-shaped**. It first falls due to efficiency (increasing returns) and then rises due to inefficiency (diminishing returns).

### 3. Average Total Cost (AC or ATC)
*   **Formula:** $AC = TC / Q$  OR  $AC = AFC + AVC$
*   **Graph:** Also **U-shaped**. The distance between AC and AVC gets smaller as output increases (because AFC is shrinking).

### 4. Marginal Cost (MC)
*   **Definition:** The addition made to the total cost by producing one extra unit of output.
*   **Formula:** $MC = \Delta TC / \Delta Q$  (or $TC_n - TC_{n-1}$)
*   **Graph:** **U-shaped**. It falls faster and rises faster than AC.

## 2.4.3 The Critical Relationship: MC vs. AC
Your notes (Page 46-47) and the graphs show a very specific relationship:
1.  When **MC < AC**, the Average Cost (AC) is **falling**.
2.  When **MC > AC**, the Average Cost (AC) is **rising**.
3.  **MC cuts AC (and AVC) at its minimum point.** This is the most efficient point of production.

## 2.4.5 Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) Curve
In the long run, there are no fixed costs. The LAC curve is formed by combining many short-run average cost (SAC) curves.
*   **The "Envelope Curve":** The LAC curve "envelopes" or supports all the SAC curves.
*   **Planning Curve:** It helps managers decide what size of factory to build for the long term.
