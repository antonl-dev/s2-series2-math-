# Module 2.3: Economic Cost Concepts

## 2.3.1 Explicit vs. Implicit Costs
(From page 45 of your notes and Question 5 in your exam paper).

1.  **Explicit Cost:** These are actual "out-of-pocket" payments made to outsiders for resources.
    *   *Examples:* Wages paid to laborers, rent for a building, payment for raw materials.
    *   *Key:* They are clearly identified and recorded in accounting books.

2.  **Implicit Cost:** These are the costs of using resources **owned by the firm** itself. No money actually leaves the firm.
    *   *Examples:* The salary the owner would have earned working elsewhere, interest on the owner's own money invested in the business.
    *   *Key:* These are basically "opportunity costs" of the owner's resources.

> **Economic Cost = Explicit Cost + Implicit Cost.**

## 2.3.2 Social, Private, and External Costs
(From page 48 of your notes). This concept is used to analyze the impact of a factory on society.

1.  **Private Cost:** The cost actually incurred by the producer to make a product. (e.g., Raw materials + Labor).
2.  **External Cost (Externality):** The cost imposed on society by production, for which the producer does not pay. (e.g., Pollution, health problems).
3.  **Social Cost:** The total cost to society of an economic activity.

**The Formula:** 
> **Social Cost = Private Cost + External Cost**

**Example from your notes:**
*   A factory produces goods at a private cost of **₹10**.
*   The pollution caused leads to health problems costing society **₹3 per unit**.
*   **Total Social Cost** = $10 + 3 = \mathbf{₹13}$.

## 2.3.3 Sunk Cost
**Definition:** A cost that has already been incurred and **cannot be recovered**, regardless of any future decision. (From page 48).

*   **Rule:** Sunk costs should be ignored when making future business decisions.
*   **Example from notes:**
    *   Cost of old machinery that cannot be sold.
    *   A firm spends **₹21 Lakhs** on market research for a project that later turns out to be unprofitable. That ₹21 Lakhs is a "Sunk Cost."

## 2.3.4 Other Important Cost Definitions
(From pages 47-48).

*   **Opportunity Cost:** The value of the next best alternative foregone. (Already covered, but vital here).
*   **Historical Cost:** The original cost paid to acquire an asset at the time of purchase.
*   **Past Cost:** Costs incurred in the past that cannot be changed by present or future decisions.
*   **Future Cost:** Costs that will be incurred in the future based on decisions taken now.
*   **Separable Cost:** Costs that can be clearly identified with a particular product or department.

---

## 2.3.5 Fixed vs. Variable Costs (Short Run)
(From page 45).

1.  **Fixed Cost (TFC):** Costs that **do not change** with the level of output. You pay them even if output is ZERO.
    *   *Example:* Rent, Interest on loans, Salaries of permanent staff.
    *   *Graph:* A horizontal straight line.
2.  **Variable Cost (TVC):** Costs that **change directly** with the level of output. If output is zero, TVC is zero.
    *   *Example:* Raw materials, power/electricity, casual labor wages.

> **Total Cost (TC) = Total Fixed Cost (TFC) + Total Variable Cost (TVC)**
