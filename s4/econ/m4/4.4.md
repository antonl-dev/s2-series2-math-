# Module 4: Section 4.4 - National Income and the Three Sectors of an Economy

### 1. Definition of National Income
National Income is the total money value of all final goods and services produced by the residents of a country during a specific period (usually one financial year).
*   **Factor Income Approach:** It is the sum total of factor incomes (Rent + Interest + Wages + Profit) received by the residents of a country.
*   **Accounting Responsibility:** In India, the **Central Statistical Organization (CSO)** is responsible for national income accounting.

### 2. The Three Sectors of an Economy
Economic activities are classified into three main sectors based on the nature of the work:

1.  **Primary Sector (Agriculture and Allied):**
    *   Consists of activities related to the extraction and exploitation of natural resources.
    *   **Examples:** Agriculture, mining, quarrying, forestry, fishing, and animal husbandry.
2.  **Secondary Sector (Manufacturing/Industrial):**
    *   Involves the transformation of raw materials into finished goods through manufacturing.
    *   **Examples:** Registered and unregistered manufacturing, construction, electricity, and gas supply.
3.  **Tertiary Sector (Service Sector):**
    *   Does not produce physical goods but provides services that support the primary and secondary sectors.
    *   **Examples:** Banking, insurance, transport, communication, health, education, trade, and tourism.

### 3. Key Concepts in National Income Estimation
To understand national income, we must distinguish between different types of goods and incomes:

*   **Final Goods vs. Intermediate Goods:**
    *   **Final Goods:** Goods ready for consumption (e.g., a shirt, a car). These are included in National Income.
    *   **Intermediate Goods:** Goods used as raw materials for further production (e.g., cotton used to make a shirt). These are **excluded** to avoid **Double Counting**.
*   **Factor Income vs. Transfer Income:**
    *   **Factor Income:** Earned in exchange for providing a service (e.g., wages for labor). Included in National Income.
    *   **Transfer Income:** One-sided payments received without providing any service (e.g., old-age pensions, gifts, unemployment allowance). **Excluded** from National Income.
*   **Consumer Goods vs. Capital Goods:**
    *   **Consumer Goods:** Used for immediate satisfaction (TV, Fridge).
    *   **Capital Goods:** Used to produce other goods (Machinery, Buildings). Economic growth depends on the stock of capital goods.
