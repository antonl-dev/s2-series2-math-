# Module 4: Section 4.3 - Circular Flow of Income: Two Sector and Multi-Sector Models

### 1. Definition
The circular flow of income refers to the continuous movement of money, goods, and services between different sectors of an economy. It shows how the national income and expenditure of an economy flow in a circular manner over time.

### 2. The Four Sectors of an Economy
1.  **Household Sector:** Owners of factors of production (land, labor, capital) and consumers of goods.
2.  **Business Sector (Firms):** Producers of goods and services; they hire factors of production.
3.  **Government Sector:** Collects taxes and provides subsidies/public services.
4.  **Foreign Sector:** Engages in exports (X) and imports (M).

---

### 3. Models of Circular Flow

#### A) Circular Flow in a Two-Sector Economy
This is the simplest model consisting of only **Households** and **Firms**.
*   **Real Flow:** Households provide *Factor Services* (labor, etc.) to firms. In return, Firms provide *Goods and Services* to households.
*   **Money Flow:** Firms make *Factor Payments* (wages, rent, interest, profit) to households. Households use this money for *Consumption Expenditure* to buy goods from firms.
*   **With Capital Market:** If households save money, it flows into the **Capital Market** (banks), which then lends it to firms as **Investment**.

#### B) Three-Sector Model (Adding Government)
*   The **Government** interacts with Households and Firms.
*   **Inflows (Leakages):** Government collects **Taxes** from both households and firms.
*   **Outflows (Injections):** Government provides **Subsidies** to firms and **Transfer Payments** (pensions, etc.) and wages to households. It also buys goods and services from firms.

#### C) Four-Sector Model (Open Economy)
*   This model adds the **Foreign Sector**.
*   **Exports (X):** Money flows *into* the domestic economy from foreign countries (Injection).
*   **Imports (M):** Money flows *out* of the domestic economy to foreign countries (Leakage).
*   **Net Flow:** Foreign remittances and payments for imports/exports.

---

### 4. Leakages vs. Injections
*   **Leakages (Withdrawals):** Variables that reduce the flow of income (Savings, Taxes, Imports).
*   **Injections:** Variables that increase the flow of income (Investment, Government Spending, Exports).
