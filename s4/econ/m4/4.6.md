# Module 4: Section 4.6 - Inflation: Causes, Effects, and Measures to Control

### 1. Meaning of Inflation
Inflation is a situation characterized by a **persistent rise in the general price level** of goods and services in an economy over a period of time.
*   **Coulborn’s Definition:** "Too much money chasing too few goods."
*   **Impact:** When prices rise, the purchasing power of money decreases (i.e., a unit of money buys fewer goods than before).

### 2. Types of Inflation (Based on Rate)
*   **Creeping Inflation:** Prices rise slowly (less than 3% per annum). Generally considered good for economic growth.
*   **Walking Inflation:** Prices rise moderately (3% to 10% per annum). It acts as a warning signal for the government.
*   **Running Inflation:** Prices rise rapidly (10% to 20% per annum). Requires strong counter-measures.
*   **Galloping or Hyperinflation:** Extreme rise in prices (20% to 100% or more). Leads to a total collapse of the monetary system.

### 3. Theories of Inflation
1.  **Demand-Pull Inflation:** Occurs when aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply. Associated with J.M. Keynes.
    *   *Causes:* Increase in money supply, higher government spending, or increase in population.
2.  **Cost-Push Inflation:** Occurs when the cost of production increases, forcing firms to raise prices.
    *   *Causes:* Increase in wages, higher profit margins, or supply shocks (e.g., oil price hikes).

### 4. Causes of Inflation
*   **Demand Side:**
    *   Increase in Money Supply (Printing more currency).
    *   Increase in Disposable Income (Due to tax reductions).
    *   Deficit Financing (Govt spending more than its revenue).
    *   Cheap Money Policy (Low interest rates).
*   **Supply Side:**
    *   Shortage of raw materials or capital.
    *   Speculative Hoarding (Traders hiding goods to create artificial scarcity).
    *   Natural Calamities (Droughts/Floods affecting crop production).
    *   Industrial Disputes (Strikes leading to lower production).

### 5. Effects of Inflation
*   **On Distribution of Income:** Debtors gain (they pay back money that is worth less), while Creditors lose. Fixed-income groups (salaried class) suffer as their real income falls.
*   **On Investment:** Initial inflation may boost investment due to higher profits, but very high inflation creates uncertainty and discourages long-term investment.
