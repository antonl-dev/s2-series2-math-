# Module 4: Section 4.1 - Indian Economy: Brief Overview of Post-Independence Period & Plans

### 1. Introduction
At the time of independence (1947), the Indian economy was stagnant, predominantly agricultural, and suffered from low productivity and widespread poverty. To address this, India adopted a **Mixed Economy** model, combining features of both Socialism (Public Sector) and Capitalism (Private Sector).

### 2. The Era of Economic Planning (1951–2017)
India adopted centralized economic planning through **Five-Year Plans (FYPs)**, overseen by the **Planning Commission**.

*   **1st Five-Year Plan (1951–56):** Focused on **Agriculture** and irrigation to ensure food security.
*   **2nd Five-Year Plan (1956–61):** Known as the **Mahalanobis Model**, it shifted focus to **Rapid Industrialization** and heavy industries (Steel, Power).
*   **Green Revolution (Mid-1960s):** Introduced High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds and modern fertilizers to make India self-sufficient in food grains.
*   **Key Goals of Planning:**
    *   **Growth:** Increasing GDP and per capita income.
    *   **Modernization:** Adoption of new technology and social changes.
    *   **Self-Reliance:** Reducing dependence on foreign imports.
    *   **Equity:** Ensuring that the benefits of growth reach the poor.

### 3. Shift to NITI Aayog (2015)
In 2015, the Government replaced the Planning Commission with **NITI Aayog** (National Institution for Transforming India). 
*   Unlike the "Top-Down" approach of the Planning Commission, NITI Aayog follows a **"Bottom-Up"** approach focusing on **Cooperative Federalism** (involving State governments in policy making).

### 4. Characteristics of the Pre-1991 Economy
*   **License Raj:** Strict government regulations and licensing requirements for private businesses.
*   **Inward Looking:** High tariffs on imports to protect domestic industries (Import Substitution).
*   **Dominance of PSUs:** Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) controlled the "commanding heights" of the economy.
