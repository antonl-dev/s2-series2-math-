# Module 4: Section 4.7 - Credit Control Methods and Fiscal Policies

To control inflation, the authorities use two main types of policy: **Monetary Policy** (by the Central Bank/RBI) and **Fiscal Policy** (by the Government).

### 1. Monetary Policy: Credit Control Methods
These measures are adopted by the Central Bank to regulate the volume and cost of credit in the economy.

#### A) Quantitative Credit Control (General)
These aim at regulating the *overall volume* of bank credit.
*   **Bank Rate:** The rate at which the Central Bank lends to commercial banks. Increasing the Bank Rate makes borrowing expensive, reducing the money supply.
*   **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR):** The percentage of total deposits that commercial banks *must* keep with the Central Bank in cash. Raising CRR reduces the funds available for banks to lend.
*   **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR):** The percentage of deposits banks must maintain in safe/liquid assets like Gold or Govt Securities *with themselves*.
*   **Open Market Operations (OMO):** The sale or purchase of government securities by the Central Bank. Selling securities sucks out liquidity from the market.
*   **Repo Rate:** The rate at which RBI lends money to banks for the short term against collateral. Higher Repo Rate = Lower Inflation.
*   **Reverse Repo Rate:** The rate at which RBI absorbs liquidity by borrowing from commercial banks.

#### B) Qualitative/Selective Credit Control
These aim at regulating the *purpose* for which credit is used.
*   **Margin Requirements:** The difference between the value of a security (collateral) and the loan amount.
*   **Moral Suasion:** Persuading banks to follow certain guidelines through meetings or hints.

### 2. Fiscal Policy Measures
These are actions taken by the **Government** to manage aggregate demand through its budget.
*   **Public Revenue (Taxation):** Increasing direct taxes (Income Tax) reduces the disposable income of people, thereby lowering demand.
*   **Public Expenditure:** Reducing government spending on non-essential projects helps cool down an overheated economy.
*   **Public Borrowing:** The government borrows from the public to reduce the excess money circulating in the economy.

### 3. Summary: Controlling Inflation vs. Deflation

| Tool | To Control Inflation (High Prices) | To Control Deflation (Low Prices) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rates (Repo/Bank)** | Increase | Decrease |
| **Reserves (CRR/SLR)** | Increase | Decrease |
| **Govt Securities** | Sell (Absorb Money) | Buy (Inject Money) |
| **Taxes** | Increase | Decrease |
| **Govt Spending** | Decrease | Increase |
